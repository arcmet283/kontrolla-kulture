Which actually cuts the other way for Hugh Freeze, at least for one week — a win over a ranked opponent is a win, OT or not, and that's exactly the kind of result that buys a hot-seat coach a little room to breathe. Ask us again in October whether it mattered.
Voss
And Georgia survived Georgia Tech by three in Week 1.
Castellan
A win's a win, but a three-point margin over an in-state rival isn't the kind of statement that quiets questions about a roster that's been bleeding transfers for three straight years. Two weeks in, that story's not settled either way.

Voss
Week 3's results aren't up yet, but the matchups are set. Let's run the whole spotlight slate, Renee — seven games, both of us on record with a pick for each one.
Castellan
Let's do it. Tennessee and Oklahoma first.
Voss
Tennessee's in at #15, Oklahoma's unranked after losing to Oklahoma State as a ranked team themselves in Week 1. I'm actually going the other way here — I like Oklahoma as a bounce-back spot off that loss.
Castellan
I can't follow you there. Oklahoma's trended down hard under second-year coach Will Stein — 7-5, then 5-7 — no CFP appearance since 2025, and their 2028 recruiting class fell all the way to No. 13. A bad loss doesn't automatically mean a bounce-back, sometimes it's just confirmation. I'll take Tennessee, even though they don't have a CFP or Final 25 appearance in three tracked years either, just a couple of bowl wins, one of them over Southern in the 2026 Gasparilla Bowl.
Voss
Alabama and Boise State. Alabama's coming off its first loss of the year, to Texas.
Castellan
One loss doesn't erase the gap for me. Boise State's best tracked finish anywhere is #21, with bowl losses in both 2026 and 2027. I'm taking Alabama.
Voss
Same here. A shaky start is still a program with pedigree Boise State's three-year sample doesn't have. Alabama for me too.
Voss
Miami and Georgia Tech looks like the most lopsided game of the week.
Castellan
Agreed, no argument from me on this one. Three straight ACC titles for Miami, a CFP quarterfinal or better all three tracked years. Georgia Tech's best finish is #21, and the one time they ran into Miami-tier competition in a bowl game, they lost it. Miami, and it's not close on paper.
Voss
Michigan and Wisconsin. Michigan was a #1 overall seed last year, I'm sticking with them.
Castellan
I'll push back on that. That same Michigan team got upset by a lower-seeded UNC in the playoff, and Wisconsin just lost its offensive coordinator to the Cincinnati head job this offseason — that's a real wildcard, not a program playing with house money. I'm actually taking Wisconsin here.
Voss
I hear it, but Wisconsin's own ceiling has topped out around #23-25 the last two years. I need to see it before I pick the upset. Michigan stays my pick.
Voss
UNC and California might be the biggest gap on paper — a national runner-up against a program that's never sniffed the playoff. UNC, easily.
Castellan
I'll take UNC too, but I'm less comfortable with it than you are. "Never sniffed the playoff" with three straight tracked bowl wins is a program that knows how to close out a season, and if UNC's carrying any kind of title-game hangover, this is exactly the kind of game that catches a favorite looking ahead.
Voss
Texas Tech and Texas A&M. Tech's been the most dominant program in the country not named Miami or Ole Miss the last three years — three straight Big 12 titles, a national championship game appearance. I'm with Tech.
Castellan
I'm going the other way on this one. A&M's reached the CFP multiple times too, including a #3 overall seed last year, but both times they've been knocked out it was by Ole Miss specifically — the conference title game and the CFP semifinal, same season. Their problem has a name, and it isn't Texas Tech. I like A&M to get through this one.
Voss
Fair point on the Ole Miss specificity, but I still think Tech's résumé is the best in this whole slate. We'll see who's right.
Voss
Last one, Ohio State and UCLA.
Castellan
Neither program's been a true playoff fixture — Ohio State got in as the #18 seed last year, UCLA hasn't been in at all, just a 2026 bowl win over Auburn. Closer than the ranking gap suggests, but I'll lean Ohio State off the better recent form.
Voss
I'll go the other way one more time — UCLA for me. That program's had recurring Heisman-watchlist buzz at quarterback the last few years, and Ohio State's recent ceiling hasn't been much higher than UCLA's anyway.
