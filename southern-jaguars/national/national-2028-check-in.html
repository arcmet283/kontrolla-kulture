Castellan
Which actually cuts the other way for Hugh Freeze, at least for one week — a win over a ranked opponent is a win, OT or not, and that's exactly the kind of result that buys a hot-seat coach a little room to breathe. Ask us again in October whether it mattered.
Voss
And Georgia survived Georgia Tech by three in Week 1.
Castellan
A win's a win, but a three-point margin over an in-state rival isn't the kind of statement that quiets questions about a roster that's been bleeding transfers for three straight years. Two weeks in, that story's not settled either way.

Voss
Week 3's results aren't up yet, but the matchups are set. Let's run the whole spotlight slate, Renee — seven games, seven picks.
Castellan
Let's do it. Starting with Oklahoma hosting Tennessee.
Voss
Oklahoma's unranked after losing to Oklahoma State as a ranked team themselves in Week 1. Tennessee comes in at #15. That reads like an easy pick.
Castellan
It's thinner than it looks. Oklahoma's trended down hard under second-year coach Will Stein — 7-5, then 5-7 — no CFP appearance since 2025, and their 2028 recruiting class fell all the way to No. 13. But Tennessee doesn't have a CFP or Final 25 appearance in three tracked years either, just a couple of bowl wins, one of them over Southern in the 2026 Gasparilla Bowl. I'll lean Tennessee off the ranking and Oklahoma's trajectory, but it's not a program I trust much right now either.
Voss
Boise State at Alabama. Alabama's coming off its first loss of the year, to Texas.
Castellan
One loss doesn't erase the gap. Boise State's best tracked finish anywhere is #21, with bowl losses in both 2026 and 2027. Alabama's shaky start is real, but it's still a program with pedigree Boise State's three-year sample just doesn't have. I'll lean Alabama.
Voss
Miami hosting Georgia Tech looks like the most lopsided game of the week.
Castellan
It is. Three straight ACC titles for Miami, a CFP quarterfinal or better all three tracked years. Georgia Tech's best finish is #21, and the one time they ran into Miami-tier competition in a bowl game, they lost it. Miami, and it's not particularly close on paper.
Voss
Wisconsin at Michigan. Michigan was a #1 overall seed last year.
Castellan
They were, and that same team got upset by a lower-seeded UNC in the playoff. Wisconsin's ceiling has topped out around #23-25 the last two years, and they just lost their offensive coordinator to the Cincinnati head job this offseason. I'll still take Michigan, but I wouldn't be shocked if this is the upset of the week.
Voss
UNC at California might be the biggest gap on paper — a national runner-up against a program that's never sniffed the playoff.
Castellan
On paper, sure. But "never sniffed the playoff" with three straight tracked bowl wins is a program that knows how to close out a season. And if UNC's carrying any kind of title-game hangover, this is exactly the kind of game that catches a favorite looking ahead. I still take UNC, I just wouldn't bet the house on it.
Voss
The one I keep coming back to is Texas A&M at Texas Tech. Tech's been the most dominant program in the country not named Miami or Ole Miss the last three years, but A&M's a real CFP team too, not a pretender.
Castellan
Three straight Big 12 titles and a national championship game appearance for Tech — that's the most consistent résumé in this entire slate. A&M's reached the CFP multiple times too, including a #3 overall seed last year, but they've been eliminated by the same program, Ole Miss, in back-to-back seasons. Their problem has been Ole Miss specifically, not the sport in general. I'll lean Tech, but I wouldn't call it comfortable.
Voss
Last one, UCLA at Ohio State.
Castellan
Neither program's been a true playoff fixture — Ohio State got in as the #18 seed last year, UCLA hasn't been in at all, just a 2026 bowl win over Auburn. Closer than the ranking gap suggests, but I'll lean Ohio State off the better recent form.
