SEGMENT 02

Ole Miss: The Champion's Encore

Voss
Ole Miss opens at No. 2 after that stunning 7-seed title run. Not No. 1, though — that's interesting on its own.
Castellan
We've actually seen this pattern before on this desk. Notre Dame won it all in 2025 and took a real step back the very next season. Defending a title in this sport, in this data, has never once meant returning as the unquestioned favorite. Ole Miss at No. 2 fits right into that pattern.

But their own recruiting class fell four spots into this cycle (No. 6 to No. 10), while Texas Tech's rating and coaching résumé both point the same direction. Miami's my pick to three-peat the ACC. Texas Tech's my pick to win the whole thing.

Coaching Casualties

The Pick
2–3 Firings From The Current Hot-Seat Board
High Confidence
Castellan
This is the safest pick on the show. Every single tracked season has produced at least one in-season or end-of-season firing from that hot-seat list, and last year alone gave us two mid-season changes. Seven names are on the 2028 board. I'd bet on Missouri's Eliah Drinkwitz finally running out of runway after surviving a 1-and-11 season, and I wouldn't be shocked if Virginia Tech or TCU is the second.

What about Hugh Freeze? He's been on this board multiple cycles now despite genuinely good seasons.
Castellan
That's exactly why I'm not picking him. Whatever's keeping Auburn nervous about him clearly isn't about the scoreboard, and if he's survived a CFP appearance already, I don't think a good-not-great season changes that calculus either. He's the name most likely to survive this list a fourth time.

SEGMENT 09

Heisman Winner: The Pick

The Pick
A New Winner — Not Jaray Rask
High Confidence
Voss
Rask is the reigning winner and he's already on the preseason board. Why bet against him?
Castellan
Because it's never happened, and it's not just the Heisman — we checked every major individual award this time, going year by year. Fernando Mendoza won the Heisman in 2025, gone the next year. Arch Manning in 2026, gone. Maddox Marcellus won Bednarik and Butkus in 2026 and didn't repeat either one in 2027 — Suntarine Perkins swept both instead. Every voted national award we've tracked has turned over completely, every single year. It's really only the pure statistical leaderboards — not the voted awards — where we've seen anyone repeat: Molinich in rushing yards, Marcellus in tackles.
Voss
That's a much stronger case than we made last time.
Castellan
It is. I'll still take the field over Rask, and I'll lean toward Alabama or Texas Tech producing the winner — the last two winners both came from a team ranked in the preseason top 3, and both of those programs are again this year.
SEGMENT 10

National Stat Leaders: Expect New Names, With Two Exceptions

Castellan
This is a pattern we've never actually said out loud before, Derek, and it's remarkably consistent. Passing, receiving, sacks, interceptions — every one of those national leaderboards has been a completely different name every year we've tracked. Only two categories have actually repeated.
Voss
Molinich in rushing, and…
Castellan
Maddox Marcellus in tackles — 134 in 2026, 147 in 2027, the national leader both years, same as Molinich. But Marcellus is NFL-bound, not back for 2028 — so tackles goes back into the "new name every year" pile regardless. Molinich's the only one of the two actually positioned to make it three straight.

SEGMENT 12

The Bold Call

Voss
Last one, Renee. Give me the pick you're least confident in but can't stop thinking about.
Castellan
Another outsider run to the title game. We watched a 7-seed beat a 9-seed for the whole thing last year — the first time either finalist wasn't one of the bracket's top seeds since we started tracking this format. If we get a repeat of that this year, it stops being a fluke and starts being the actual identity of this playoff format — and it would mean picking Texas Tech to win it all is actually the contrarian choice on this board, not the safe one.
SEGMENT 13

Storylines To Watch

Voss
Before we close, let's get personal for a minute. Renee, who are you actually watching once the season starts?
Castellan
Jaray Rask, and it's not close. We just laid out that every voted national award on record has turned over the very next year — Mendoza, Manning, Marcellus and Perkins splitting Bednarik and Butkus between them. Rask is the first reigning Heisman winner we've tracked walking into a season as the preseason favorite to repeat. Either he breaks the pattern, or he becomes the next name on a list that's three deep already.
Voss
And he's not even the only Heisman name in that building. Deshawn Bernard's right there with him on the preseason board.
Castellan
Which is its own storyline. Iowa State doesn't have a quarterback problem or a receiver problem, it has an embarrassment-of-riches problem — two legitimate Heisman cases on the same roster, both chasing the same trophy their own teammate already has. If the Cyclones are great again, the awards voters are going to have to pick one of them, and that's a fight that didn't exist in this sport last year.
Voss
What about on the coaching side?
Castellan
Hugh Freeze. Third straight cycle on a hot-seat board despite a CFP appearance last year — that Round 1 loss to Ole Miss clearly didn't buy him any goodwill. If a program's patience is running out after a season that good, that's not really about the record anymore, that's about something behind the scenes we're not seeing. Whatever it is, 2028 reads like the last chance to make it go away.
Voss
And that puts his quarterback in an odd spot too — Stephon Benjamin's on the Heisman board, but he's also the guy whose every incompletion gets read as a referendum on his coach.
Castellan
That's real pressure for a player who's never had to answer for someone else's job security before. A big year from Benjamin probably buys Freeze another cycle regardless of anything else on this list. A rough one, and he becomes part of the case against him whether that's fair or not.
Voss
And Kirk Ferentz landing on a hot-seat board for the first time ever, after two bowl wins the last two years?
Castellan
That's the one I still can't explain. That's either the quietest coaching change of the year building, or the most overblown name on the entire board. A coach with that much tenure doesn't show up on a list like this over a couple of bowl losses that didn't even happen — something else is driving it.
Voss
One more before we go — Georgia. They're not walking away with anybody this cycle, they're the ones losing players. Jordan Hall to Oklahoma, Zayden Walker to Notre Dame, now J'Vari Flowers all the way to Southern Jaguars football.
Castellan
Three portal losses in three years and not a single name walking in the other direction, as far as we've tracked. No losing season to point to, so it's not the obvious story yet. But if that drain continues into a fourth year, it stops being a coincidence and starts being a referendum on the staff, regardless of what the win total says.
SEGMENT 14

What To Watch

Voss
Renee, close us out. What's the one thing you want eyes on as this season kicks off?
Castellan
Whether that ratings-versus-ranking gap we opened with actually means anything once games are played. If Texas at No. 16 starts blowing out teams the way a 91-rated roster should, that's a real story about this preseason poll undervaluing somebody. And keep half an eye on Iowa — a coach with that much history landing on a hot-seat board for the first time is the kind of storyline that either fizzles by October or completely dominates a season.
Voss
And that's the board: Texas Tech to finally cash in a three-peat conference streak and win it all, no Heisman repeat, Alabama and LSU as the recruiting-backed darkhorses, and at least two coaches out of a job by year's end. We'll see everyone back here once the games actually start counting.
