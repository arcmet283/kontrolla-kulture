SEGMENT 02

The Bracket That Ended In A Blowout

Voss
Play-by-Play
54-14, Renee. After a bracket full of tight finishes, the national championship game itself wasn't one of them. Walk me through how Texas and Georgia both got there.
Castellan
Analyst
Georgia entered unbeaten at 13-0 and the CFP's No. 1 overall seed, and their path there was a survival act — a 37-35 semifinal escape over No. 12 Alabama in the Fiesta Bowl after Alabama had just knocked off No. 4 seed Michigan 31-28 in the Orange Bowl. The No. 2 seed Texas's road ran through No. 11 Clemson, 27-24 in the Peach Bowl semifinal, after Clemson had upset No. 3 seed Texas Tech 48-34 in the Rose Bowl. Two semifinal classics setting up a title game that wasn't close at all.

A completely different pattern from the year before: Texas won the national title and only signed the No. 16 class the following cycle — a real, measurable step down for a defending champion, not a reload. Georgia, the runner-up, jumped from No. 19 the previous cycle to No. 5 — a 14-spot climb for a team that didn't actually win it all. And Pittsburgh, bounced in Round 1, still landed a No. 3 class nationally the following cycle — proof a first-round playoff exit doesn't automatically sink a program's recruiting trajectory either.

SEGMENT 06

The Coaching Carousel

Castellan
Analyst
Three jobs changed hands this cycle, all set up to debut in 2027: Oklahoma moves on from Brent Venables for Will Stein, promoted up from Oregon's offensive coordinator role. USC parts ways with Lincoln Riley for Jesse Minter, coming over from the Chargers' defensive staff. And Kentucky replaces Mark Stoops with Bob Surace out of Princeton.
Voss
Play-by-Play
Three job openings at three very different tiers of the sport — a blue blood in Oklahoma, a resurgent power in USC, and a rebuild in Kentucky. All three of those hires get their first test in 2027.

Play-by-Play
The 2027 class is in the books too, and Iowa — not a name you'd guess — takes the No. 1 class in the country.

Analyst
A transfer-built haul again, led by JC Ajian, a 5-star defensive tackle in from Oklahoma — a mountain at 6'6", 286 pounds, though his 74 speed and 76 acceleration are actually middling for the position, more space-eating nose tackle than sideline-to-sideline disruptor. Four different 5-star transfers total for Iowa's class — that's the story here, not a single blue-chip name.
