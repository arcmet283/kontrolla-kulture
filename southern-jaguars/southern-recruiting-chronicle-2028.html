No. 36: The Best Class In Program History, By Far

Class RankNo. 36 (from 59th, 63rd)
4-Stars Signed3
5-Star Transfers2
Headline StoryJ'Vari Flowers Comes Home

Every prior recruiting chronicle for this program ended with the same conclusion: Southern recruits in bulk at the bottom of the ratings scale, hits a ceiling in the 60s nationally, and occasionally lands one great player. The 2028 cycle breaks that pattern completely — three 4-stars in one signing class, two 5-star transfers, and a class rank jump from the 60s to No. 36 nationally. This is the full update, and it includes the best individual story this chronicle has ever tracked.

SEGMENT 04

J'Vari Flowers Comes Home

Rewind two recruiting cycles. Southern was chasing J'Vari Flowers as a high school prospect out of Florida — a 4-star cornerback the program wanted badly and lost, watching him sign with Georgia instead. He transferred out of Georgia, bounced to Florida, then to Alabama, developing the whole way — and this cycle, he transferred again. This time, to Southern.

He arrives a 5-star, the No. 5 overall transfer add in the entire country — the single highest-rated player this program has ever landed by any method, signing or portal. It's the exact recruit this program lost years ago, come back a completely different player, choosing Southern this time when he didn't have to.

SEGMENT 05

The Louisiana Pipeline: Still Bleeding

Even in the program's best recruiting cycle yet, the in-state pipeline problem hasn't gone away. The 2028 Stolen Recruits log grew to twelve names, the largest single-cycle miss total this chronicle has tracked — and Louisiana talent is still walking out the door to blue bloods and in-state rivals alike:

Alabama 3 Misses

Darian Weathersby (DE), Jonathan Bowen (T), Chevin Jackson (G) — all Louisiana natives.

LSU 2 Misses

Cole Houston (DT) and Jon Harper (T), both in-state.

Texas Tech 2 Misses

Austin Overshown (ATH) and GG Shorter (ATH), both Louisiana natives.

Miami 1 Miss

K.D. Wilcox (HB), Louisiana.

Louisiana Tech 1 Miss

Fadil Wilcher (DT), in-state.

The class rank jump this cycle came almost entirely from the transfer portal and a handful of out-of-state high school signees, not from winning the in-state recruiting battles this program has lost every single cycle tracked so far.

SEGMENT 07

The Takeaway

This is the cycle where Southern's recruiting operation actually changed shape. Two straight years of 60s-ranked classes built on 2-star volume just became a No. 36 class built on two 5-star transfers and three 4-star signees — and the headline name is a player the program already lost once and finally won back. The in-state misses haven't stopped, and won't be solved by one good cycle. But for the first time in three tracked years, this program signed a class that looks like it belongs in the same conversation as the teams it's chasing.
