SEGMENT 04

The 2028 Signing Class Arrives

Alongside the two headline transfers, the 2028 high school class brings three 4-stars to campus — Will Johnston (SS), Jaelen McCracken (ATH/DE), and Danny Johnson (CB) — tying the 2026 class for the most 4-stars this program has ever signed in one cycle. The class finished ranked No. 75 nationally, a step back from 2027's No. 36 — the high-school haul didn't keep pace with the transfer portal this cycle.

SEGMENT 05

Coach Graves On The 2028 Class

Reporter: Flowers and Jones have been through a full camp now. Are they living up to the billing?

Graves: Flowers looks like a No. 5 overall transfer, honestly. He's matched up with our best receiver every day of camp and held his own more often than not. Jones has been exactly what we hoped — that preseason All-Conference nod wasn't a courtesy, he's earned every bit of it before playing a real down.

Reporter: Where does the high school class fit into the two-deep right now?

Graves: Johnston and McCracken are both going to play early, probably more than a normal true freshman would at their spots, just because of where our depth sits. Danny Johnson's still working his way up the corner room behind some older guys. And watch Deshawn Hall — three-star corner, Louisiana kid, further along than guys we signed at twice his rating.

Reporter: You've talked before about the misses in this class. Anything change your mind on those since signing day?

Graves: No. Bowen and Houston both would've helped us, and both are good players somewhere else now. What's changed is we've got actual film this fall on what we did land, and I like our hand better than I liked it in February.

Reporter: Twenty-one players departed after last season, including two All-Conference starters. How do you fill that much at once?

Graves: You don't replace Byron Allen and DJ Hill-Smith with one guy apiece, that's why we went and got two of them. Ricardo Jones steps in for Byron at safety, and between Jones and the corners we're bringing in, we think we've covered Hill-Smith's loss in the back end too. Christian Martin graduating hurts up front, we haven't settled that one yet. But that's the job every offseason — you lose good players because they earned the right to move on, and you go find the next ones.

SEGMENT 08

Heisman Watch: A Blank Slate

The 2028 Heisman Watchlist opens with five names: Stephon Benjamin (Auburn), Jaray Rask (Iowa St., the reigning winner), Kevin Washington (Notre Dame), Thomas Teague (LSU), and Deshawn Bernard (Iowa St.). Molinich isn't among them at this earliest checkpoint — too soon to call it a snub with the season not yet played, but after a mixed track record on this list (on it in 2027, off it in 2026), it's the first thing worth checking once Week 1 numbers are in.

SEGMENT 10

Does The Run Defense Finally Turn?

Three straight seasons, Southern's run defense has finished ranked 135th, 132nd, and 132nd nationally — the single most stubborn problem this program has tracked, surviving multiple full transfer classes aimed directly at fixing it. This year's headline additions, Flowers and Jones, are both defensive backs, not the front seven — the portal class does add front-seven depth too (Zach Gray and Greg Salguero at DT, Emmanuel Karmo at DE, David Shorter and Anthony Webber at linebacker), but none of them carry the pedigree of the two secondary names, the same mismatch between where the headline roster investment goes and where the actual problem lives that's plagued every previous transfer class. Prediction: it doesn't turn. A fourth straight season in the bottom 10 nationally against the run is the most likely outcome unless one of those lower-profile front-seven adds emerges.

SEGMENT 11

Molinich's Third Season

Molinich's rushing total has declined each season he's been the feature back — 2,349 yards as a true freshman, 1,669 last year. If that trend continues, expect a total closer to 1,300–1,500 rather than another national-leader campaign, especially with a full backfield now including Carlos Allen entering his second season. That's not a decline in quality so much as a shift toward a more balanced offense — and it could actually help his case if Southern is winning more games instead of needing to feed him 25+ carries just to stay competitive.

SEGMENT 12

Foxworth, Year Two As A Starter

Foxworth closed his first full season at 130-of-246 (52.8%) for 2,067 yards, 21 touchdowns, and 10 interceptions. A sophomore leap in completion percentage is the single most common development-curve pattern for a young starting quarterback in this dynasty's own data — both Foxworth's own in-season improvement from his first spot start to his last, and Molinich's own freshman-to-sophomore refinement, point the same direction. Expect the touchdown total to climb and the interception rate to drop, even if the yardage total stays similar.

SEGMENT 14

From The Coach

Reporter: Two portal additions, a defense that jumped nine points on paper, and a run defense that's finished bottom-10 three years running. Does this finally fix it?

Graves: We'll find out in September like everybody else. I'm not going to sit here and declare it fixed off a roster card. What I'll say is we finally have the pieces at the right spots to find out for real, instead of hoping depth covers for it again.

Reporter: No bowl last year, the biggest roster turnover this program has had, and now you open with a brand-new opponent in Kentucky. How do you reset a locker room after a season like that?

Graves: You don't reset anything. Same two rules we started with in 2025. New faces, same plan. Kentucky's a clean slate for both sides — neither of us has anything on the other to go off of, which is exactly the kind of game where what you've built shows up or it doesn't.

SEGMENT 15

Storylines To Watch

The Corner Room Crunch Depth Battle

Flowers and Shelton are penciled in, but Danny Johnson, Deshawn Hall, and redshirt freshman Addison Tolefree are all fighting for the same handful of reps behind them. Southern has lost defensive backs to the portal before — Devin Sanchez to Texas Tech, Siale Siverand to Oklahoma State — and whoever loses this fall's battle has a real shot at being the next name on that list by spring.

Carlos Allen's Make-Or-Break Year Pressure

The program's first five-star high school signee is entering Year Two still unproven behind Molinich. Another season buried on the depth chart and the question stops being about his talent and starts being about whether he sticks around long enough to find out if it ever shows up here.

The Offensive Line's Quiet Question Unresolved

No transfer portal splash at tackle, no five-star signee waiting in the wings — just Christian Martin's empty spot and a room of unproven names (Jose Cox, Rodney Warmack, Chancellor Powell) who haven't had a signature moment yet. The flashiest roster turnover in program history could still get undone by the one position nobody's talking about.

Darian Weathersby, The Other Transfer Story Under The Radar

Southern usually loses the portal battle up front — Benedict Umeh left for Stanford two cycles ago. Weathersby flips that script: a two-star edge piece who originally committed to Alabama before landing at Southern instead. No hype attached, but he's the kind of name that turns into a real story by October if the pass rush needs him.

SEGMENT 16

The Takeaway

This is, on paper, the most talented roster this program has ever put on the field — two headline transfer additions, the biggest portal haul this program has tracked, and a returning core that includes the reigning national rushing leader and three All-Conference performers. The preseason rank hasn't caught up to any of that yet, still sitting at No. 89. If the defensive jump translates on the field, particularly on a run defense that's needed it for three straight years, this could be the season this program finally breaks out of the range it's occupied since the very first tracked year.

The game-by-game math lands on 7–5: five games leaning win, three leaning loss, and four true toss-ups that actually decide the season. USF is the one clean gap the roster upgrade doesn't obviously close; Texas is a talent-gap loss regardless of history. Break the four toss-ups — Kentucky, Louisiana, Memphis, UTSA — right and this is a 9-win team. Break them wrong and 5–7 is the realistic floor.
