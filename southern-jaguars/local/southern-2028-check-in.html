The first three games were a combined 123–36. USF was a different story from the opening snap — a reigning AAC champion that made Southern look like a team playing its first real road test of the year for the second week in a row, and this time the result matched the difficulty. ECU, a week later, was the opposite kind of week entirely. Louisiana, in the first-ever meeting between these two programs, went the other way again — a home loss in overtime, Southern's second defeat of the season. Texas made it three, and the most decisive of the bunch: a 45–27 final in Austin against a team that had spent the whole year climbing back into the top 10.

SEGMENT 02

Foxworth Hits A Wall

Starting Quarterback · Through 7 Games
Joseph Foxworth QB · SO
96-of-174, 1,390 yds, 12 TD, 5 Int

Three weeks of a clean sophomore leap — zero interceptions, eight touchdowns — ran into USF's defense and didn't survive it. Foxworth went 5-of-19 for 107 yards with 2 interceptions before getting pulled in the third quarter, the first real stumble of his season. James Moody came off the bench and was sharper in relief, going 7-of-18 for 165 yards and 2 touchdowns. Nothing was announced right away about who'd start at ECU — for the first time all season, that question was actually open.

There's a strange symmetry to it. The last time these two teams played, in 2027, it was Moody getting benched against ECU and a true freshman named Joseph Foxworth coming off the bench to run the offense for the first time in his career. A season later, against that same opponent, Foxworth was the one walking back to the sideline and Moody was the one finishing the game — and whether this rematch would settle anything the way the last one did was the whole subplot heading into the matchup.

Three games later, the shape of that question has changed again. The ECU response was clean: 16-of-19, 236 yards, 3 touchdowns, zero interceptions. Louisiana was the opposite — 14-of-29, 239 yards, 1 touchdown, 2 interceptions in a game that needed overtime to settle. Texas was rougher still, 17-of-29 for 199 yards with another pick, against the best defense he's actually faced. Add it up and he's at 96-of-174 for 1,390 yards, 12 touchdowns, and 5 interceptions through seven games — a sophomore who's clearly good enough to win most weeks, with the two or three genuinely hard matchups on the schedule the ones still finding his limits. Every interception of his season so far has come against USF, Louisiana, or Texas — not a single one against Kentucky, North Texas, Army, or ECU.

SEGMENT 03

Molinich's Workload

Through 7 Games
Jordan Molinich HB · JR
1,173 rush yds, 13 TD · 178 rec yds, 5 TD

112 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns against Kentucky, 226 and 2 more against North Texas, 222 rushing yards and 3 touchdowns at Army, then 125 more at USF — 685 yards on the ground through four games, plus 72 receiving yards and 3 receiving touchdowns on top of it. The preview's prediction was a decline toward 1,300–1,500 yards on the season as the offense balances out; this pace, if it held, would blow well past that. The one thing USF's defense couldn't take away was the run game — Molinich still got his, even in a loss. ECU didn't take it away either.

Three games further on, that question is settled. 105 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns at ECU, plus 3 catches for 40 yards and 2 more scores through the air — his biggest single-game output of the season at the time. 160 rushing yards and a touchdown followed at home against Louisiana, in a game that needed overtime to decide. Then a new career high against Texas, covered below. He's gone over 100 rushing yards in every game he's played this season — the lone quiet week for the whole operation was USF, where the ground game stayed productive (125 yards) but didn't find the end zone at all. Combined rushing and receiving, he's already within range of the preview's full-season projection with five regular-season games still to go, and whatever postseason football comes after that.

SEGMENT 04

The Defense: The Question Got Answered

Three weeks of a defense that hadn't been tested against the run ended with an answer nobody wanted: Southern gave up 276 rushing yards to USF. The preview's biggest question — whether the nine-point ratings jump actually moved this unit out of the national bottom 10 — looks like it didn't, at least not against a real AAC run game. Through three weeks the box scores simply hadn't forced the issue; USF forced it immediately, and the defense didn't hold.

It wasn't a total collapse. Christian Thatcher's 17 tackles and 4 sacks through three games and Ricardo Jones' two interceptions were real positives earlier in the season, and neither got worse at USF — the run defense problem specifically is what showed up. ECU finished 2027 at 5–7, 3–6 in the AAC, a step down from USF's level. Whether that was a chance to patch the problem against lesser competition, or whether it would show up again regardless of opponent, was the question ECU actually answered.

SEGMENT 05

Foxworth Answers Back At ECU

He got the start, and he answered about as emphatically as a quarterback can after a benching: 16-of-19 for 236 yards and 3 touchdowns, zero interceptions, in a 42–19 road win. A season ago it was Moody who got pulled against this same ECU team and a true freshman Foxworth who took the offense and never gave it back. This time Foxworth's the one who needed the response game, and he got it against the same opponent that launched his career in the first place. The "who starts at ECU" question that closed out last week's update didn't stay open long.

SEGMENT 06

Molinich's Four-Touchdown Day

105 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns on the ground, plus 3 catches for 40 yards and 2 more touchdowns through the air — four total touchdowns in one game, his biggest single-game output of the season. Through five weeks he's at 790 rushing yards and 112 receiving yards, 14 total touchdowns, still well ahead of the preseason decline the preview predicted.

SEGMENT 07

The Defense Responds Too

Jaelen McCracken logged 2 sacks and Dre Still picked off a pass — a cleaner defensive week than the USF game, against the lesser competition already noted above, which is exactly the caveat worth keeping in mind. This result alone doesn't answer whether the run-defense problem from USF is actually fixed. It's a real data point in the right direction, not a conclusion.

SEGMENT 08

Foxworth's Rough Night In Overtime

A week after answering the USF benching about as cleanly as possible at ECU, Foxworth ran into a much tougher night at home against Louisiana: 14-of-29 for 239 yards, 1 touchdown, and 2 interceptions in the overtime loss. It's not the same kind of stumble as USF — nobody got pulled this time, and the overall numbers aren't as ugly — but it's the second real rough outing of the season for the sophomore, both of them coming against the two toughest defenses Southern's actually faced so far.

SEGMENT 10

A First-Ever Meeting Decided In OT

21–28, Louisiana's first win in the first game these two programs have ever played — a reminder that the reigning 2027 Sun Belt champion was never the pushover a non-conference date might suggest. A one-score game at the final whistle that needed overtime to settle it — not a blowout either direction, just a game Southern came up a possession short in. It drops the Jaguars to 4–2 on the season, still 3–1 in AAC play since Louisiana was a non-conference date, with a measuring-stick road trip to a ranked opponent waiting immediately after.

SEGMENT 11

Molinich's Career Night At Texas

223 rushing yards on 21 carries, 3 touchdowns, plus 66 more yards through the air — his biggest ground performance of the season, against the best front seven Southern's faced all year. The preview's prediction of a decline toward 1,300–1,500 yards on the season looks wrong in the other direction entirely: he's at 1,173 rushing yards and 13 touchdowns through just seven games, a pace that would blow past even the optimistic end of that range with five regular-season games still left to play.

SEGMENT 12

Foxworth And The Defense In Austin

A rough one for the sophomore: 17-of-29 for 199 yards and an interception, his third real stumble of the season and his first against a team with a defense this talented. The unit on the other side of the ball didn't fare any better — 348 passing yards allowed, the kind of number that extends the same run-and-pass defense questions that first showed up against USF back in Week 4. Three of Southern's toughest defensive tests this season (USF, Louisiana, Texas) have all produced the same conclusion: the step up in competition is still finding real gaps in this unit.

SEGMENT 13

Jason Davis Steps Up

With the passing game otherwise struggling, Jason Davis quietly put together his best game of the season: 6 catches for 93 yards. It's not a result that changes the final score, but it's a real data point in an offense that's leaned on Molinich and Foxworth almost exclusively through the air so far — a third weapon showing up in the toughest game of the year is worth tracking into the back half of the schedule.

SEGMENT 14

What's Ahead

Week 8 · Away · Non-Conference
at Navy

A measuring-stick trip in a different sense than Texas was — Navy's triple-option attack is unlike anything else on Southern's schedule, and unlike the shootout in Austin, this one figures to be decided in the trenches and on time of possession rather than through the air. Southern and Navy met once before, back in 2025, and Navy won that one — a result Coach Graves and this program haven't forgotten heading back into the series.

Stopping an option offense is as much about discipline and assignment football as it is about raw talent, which makes this a very different kind of test for a defense that's already shown real cracks against USF, Louisiana, and Texas. Whether that unit can adjust to an entirely different style of attack — one that lives on long, clock-draining drives instead of explosive plays — is the whole story of this trip.

SEGMENT 15

Storylines To Watch

The MLB room after Pritchett. Todd Pritchett, the sophomore middle linebacker, tore a shoulder muscle in the USF game and is out for the season — the only serious injury on the roster through seven weeks. The depth and inexperience behind him will get tested, right in the middle of a defense that's already been exposed once against a real run game, heading into the back half of the schedule.

Jaelen McCracken's freshman season. A true freshman defensive end already has multi-sack games twice in Southern's first five weeks — 2 against North Texas, 2 more against ECU. That's happening at a position the preview flagged as a question mark. Worth tracking how big a role he's carved out by midseason.

James Moody's crossroads. The backup quarterback outplayed Foxworth in relief at USF — 7-of-18 for 165 yards and 2 touchdowns — and then watched Foxworth answer right back at ECU and reclaim the job cleanly. Moody's shown he can play winning football when called on; whether that's enough to hold his spot as the clear backup, or whether a strong relief outing like that starts a longer conversation about his future here, is worth watching the rest of the way.

Teddy Graves' best start yet, with an asterisk underneath it. 5–7, then 8–6, then 6–6 — three seasons, three different ceilings, and still no real breakout. A 4–3 record through seven games, with both losses now coming against ranked/established competition (Louisiana, Texas), is still the best this program has looked under Graves at this point in a season. The odd wrinkle: the program's own prestige rating actually dropped heading into this year, on the back of its worst recruiting class finish of the Graves era. A good record and a declining recruiting trajectory pulling in opposite directions is exactly the kind of tension worth watching the rest of the way.

The recruiting trail keeps producing storylines. The program landed a commitment from Graham Pierre, a running back out of Louisiana, for the 2029 class this week — a name worth tracking well before he ever takes a snap, and a reminder that the recruiting trail doesn't pause just because the toughest stretch of the schedule is underway.

SEGMENT 16

The Takeaway

4–3, 3–1 in AAC play, after the most decisive loss of the season — a measuring-stick trip to No. 10 Texas that didn't go Southern's way. Molinich had the best game of his season regardless, Jason Davis announced himself as a real third option in the passing game, and the defense's struggles against top competition showed up again. Navy is next, a completely different kind of test — an option attack built to slow this team down rather than outscore it, and a program Southern still owes one from 2025.
