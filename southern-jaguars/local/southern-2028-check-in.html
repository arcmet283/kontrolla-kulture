The first two wins came at home and came comfortably. Army was supposed to be different — a road trip to West Point against the one program that's actually given Southern trouble before, a 59–6 blowout loss back in 2025. Instead it was the most lopsided result of the season: 44–12, Southern's third straight win over the Black Knights.

SEGMENT 02

Foxworth's Clean Start

Starting Quarterback
Joseph Foxworth QB · SO
44-of-78, 609 yds, 8 TD, 0 Int

Foxworth opened the year 21-of-33 for 260 yards and 4 touchdowns against Kentucky, followed it with a more efficient 13-of-24 for 148 yards and 2 more scores against North Texas, then went 10-of-21 for 201 yards and 2 touchdowns at Army. Three games, eight total touchdowns, zero interceptions — exactly the sophomore leap the preview flagged as the most likely development-curve pattern in this program's own data, and it hasn't slowed down against tougher competition.

SEGMENT 03

Molinich's Workload

Through 3 Games
Jordan Molinich HB · JR
560 rush yds, 7 TD · 72 rec yds, 3 TD

112 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns against Kentucky, 226 and 2 more against North Texas, then 222 rushing yards and 3 touchdowns at Army — 560 yards on the ground through three games, plus 72 receiving yards and 3 receiving touchdowns on top of it. The preview's prediction was a decline toward 1,300–1,500 yards on the season as the offense balances out; this pace, if it held, would blow well past that. Three games in, it's still the opposite direction the preview expected.

SEGMENT 04

The Defense: Still An Open Question

The biggest question from the preview — whether the defense's nine-point ratings jump finally moves the run defense out of the national bottom 10 — still doesn't have a clean answer. Kentucky and North Texas both leaned on the pass, and the Army recap doesn't flag a run-defense problem either, the same clean mark it's carried all three weeks. Christian Thatcher has been the early story up front: 2 sacks in the opener, then 7 tackles and 2 more sacks at Army, for 17 tackles and 4 sacks through three games. Ricardo Jones added his second interception in three games. It's three weeks of a defense that hasn't been embarrassed — but USF, coming off a win over a ranked team, is a tougher measuring stick than anything Southern has faced so far.

SEGMENT 05

What's Ahead

Week 4 · Road · AAC Play · USF Leads, 1–0
at USF

These two have only met once: USF won 21–14 at Southern back in 2025, the program's only loss to a current AAC rival on this list. USF closed out 2027 at 9–5, dropped the AAC title game, and lost in the first round of the CFP as a 12-seed — a step below the top of the conference, but not a pushover. They've looked sharp again early in 2028, knocking off a ranked UCF team. The pick here: Southern's offense has been too clean through three weeks to expect a letdown, but a true road revenge game against a team playing with confidence is the kind of AAC test this schedule hasn't offered yet. Southern, in a game that's closer than the first three.

SEGMENT 06

The Takeaway

3–0, 2–0 in AAC play, a quarterback who still hasn't turned it over, and a running back pacing well ahead of his own preseason projection. Army was supposed to be the first real test and it wasn't one. USF is next — an AAC opponent playing with real momentum, and the only team currently on the schedule that's already beaten Southern once. It's the best chance yet to find out what this team actually is.
