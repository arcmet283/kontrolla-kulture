Two Seven-And-Nine Seeds Just Played For The National Title

ChampionOle Miss, 21-17
HeismanJaray Rask, Iowa St.
Coaches Fired2
2028 Hot Seat7 Coaches
Derek Voss
Play-by-play
Renee Castellan
Analyst
Voss
Welcome back to the National Desk. The confetti's down, the trophies are handed out, and somehow the two teams standing at the end of this thing were seeded seven and nine. Renee, in three years of doing this, have you ever seen a title game like that?
Castellan
Never. Not once. Every single tracked championship before this one had at least one blue-blood, top-4-seed team standing at the end. This year the committee's top two seeds — Michigan and Iowa State — were both gone before the semifinals. We are recapping the most chaotic season this desk has covered, start to finish.

Voss
Walk me through it. Iowa State goes into the Cotton Bowl 12-and-1, the No. 2 overall seed, and Ole Miss just — ends it. 38-24.
Castellan
And it's not a fluke, Derek, that's the part people are going to miss. Ole Miss beat a 12-1 Iowa State team, then beat a 12-2 Texas A&M team in the semifinal, then beat a UNC team that had just knocked off the No. 1 overall seed, Michigan, in the Sugar Bowl. That's three consecutive elite wins to close a season. You don't back into that as a 7-seed. You earn it.
Voss
And on the other side, UNC's road to the title game might be the single most impressive run of upsets we've ever charted on this desk — a 9-seed knocking off the No. 8 team, then Michigan, the No. 1 overall seed, in the Sugar Bowl, then Notre Dame in the Fiesta Bowl semifinal.
Castellan
Three straight ranked kills to get to the title game, and they still came up two points short. That's how good this final actually was — 21-17, and it did not feel like a mismatch for one second.

Voss
Jaray Rask sweeping the Heisman and the Davey O'Brien is the headline, but Renee, look at Ole Miss on this board — they didn't win the whole thing on scheme alone.
Castellan
Suntarine Perkins wins Bednarik AND Butkus — a defensive player of the year sweep — and Julius Tisdol takes home the Mackey. That's a national champion that's also producing individual hardware on both sides of the ball. It's not a fluke title. The roster was built for exactly this.
Voss
And one quiet footnote for our regulars — Iowa State's Matt Campbell wins Best HC at 12-1, even after that Cotton Bowl loss. Same pattern as our very first Best HC winner, actually — Ball State's Mike Uremovich won it back in 2025 without winning a title either.
Castellan
Makes sense to me. Twelve wins, a Heisman winner on his roster, and the only loss all year outside of the eventual national champion came in the Big 12 title game itself. Losing to Ole Miss in Round 2 doesn't erase a season like that.

Castellan
Jordan Molinich closes it out as the national rushing leader for the second year of his career, Derek. Second straight season, both years he's been on a college field.
Voss
And yet he's nowhere on the awards board we just showed. Not Heisman, not Doak Walker — that went to Richard Young at Alabama.
Castellan
It's the story of his career so far. He's produced at a national-leader level in back-to-back years and hasn't converted it into a single major award. Compare that to Michael Hallock, who wins the Lombardi as the same guy topping our sacks leaderboard — production and hardware lining up perfectly. Molinich's production just isn't translating the same way, and it's worth watching whether that ever changes.

SEGMENT 06

What To Watch

Voss
So Renee, as we turn the page to 2028 — what's the one thing you're carrying out of this season?
Castellan
That seeding means less than it's ever meant. A 7 and a 9 played for the title this year. If that happens again next season, it's not a fluke anymore, it's just how this format works now, and everybody building a roster needs to plan around that instead of chasing a 1-seed.
Voss
And on the carousel side — Kirk Ferentz and Eliah Drinkwitz are both going to be the two most-discussed names of the entire offseason, for completely opposite reasons.
Castellan
Agreed. That's the desk for this recap. We'll see everybody back here for the road to 2028.
