Of the sixteen 2-star signees in this class, the raw development curve is brutal: Johari Fludd was cut after one year, Patrick Miller and Dwayne Davis and Kainoa Acker were all cut by year three. SEGMENT 03

The 2027 Signing Class: A 5-Star Arrives

Twenty-two signees. One 5-star, one 4-star, four 3-stars, sixteen 2-stars — same volume shape as before, but this class delivered Southern's first-ever 5-star signee.

Headliner
Carlos Allen, HB · 5★ · No. 16 Nationally

A Virginia product and the highest-rated recruit in program history. Now a true freshman contributor in 2027, already showing up in the box score — 108 rushing yards on 12 carries in the 97–10 blowout of Tennessee State in Week 9.

The Quarterback Bet
James Moody & Joseph Foxworth, both 2★ QBs

Neither was viewed as a difference-maker on signing day — both 2-star, both outside their position's top 100 nationally (Moody at 106, Foxworth at 215). Both ended up starting as true/redshirt freshmen in 2027 anyway, after injuries and benchings up the depth chart. Foxworth in particular has taken over the job by midseason and was the quarterback of record in the Tennessee State blowout.

SEGMENT 05

The Transfer Portal Hauls

Sixteen portal additions across two full cycles, almost entirely at the bottom of the ratings scale — and the best player of the entire haul was a 2-star.

Portal Steal Of The Chronicle
Gavin Hoffman, WR · 2★ · from Missouri

Signed as a low-rated TE/WR convert for the 2027 class. Caught the Week 1 game-winning touchdown from Ty Wilson with nine seconds left against Stanford, and by Week 10 led the team in receiving at 748 yards and 8 touchdowns — 2nd in the conference in both categories. The single best value pickup in this entire chronicle.

SEGMENT 06

The Revolving Door

Three players left Southern via the portal after the 2026 season. In that same offseason cycle, Southern went right back into the portal to try to win all three back. Southern lost all three — to the exact schools they'd already left for or come from.

Devin Sanchez Lost Twice

CB, came from Oklahoma St. in 2026, went All-Conference (39 tkls, 5 INT, a pick-six), entered the portal and left for Texas Tech. Southern's same-cycle attempt to bring him back lost to Texas Tech again.

Benedict Umeh Lost Twice

DT, came from Stanford in 2026, contributed (13 tkls, 3 TFL, 1 sack), entered the portal and left for Stanford. Southern's same-cycle re-recruitment lost him back to Stanford.

Siale Siverand Lost Twice

FS, a 2026 HS signee, played as a true freshman (11 tkls, 1 FF), entered the portal and left for Oklahoma St. Southern's same-cycle pursuit lost him to Oklahoma St. again.

A 0-for-3 record trying to win back players who already chose to leave once is a real, repeatable pattern worth watching in future cycles — Southern has not yet successfully reversed a single outgoing transfer decision.

SEGMENT 07

The Ones That Got Away

Biggest Miss Of The Chronicle
Zayden Walker, OLB · 5★ Transfer Target Lost To Notre Dame

Southern had him on the board as a transfer target out of Georgia, already rated a 5-star and the No. 7 overall transfer add in the country. Lost him to Notre Dame, where he became that class's No. 1 overall signee — on the roster that finished ranked No. 1 nationally. The single most significant recruiting battle Southern is confirmed to have lost outright.

Also lost: J'vari Flowers (4-star CB, went to Florida, then transferred again to Georgia) and Ryan Allen (4-star CB, went to UCF, then transferred to Tulane) — two more 4-star misses from the 2026 board who kept moving and never landed at Southern at any stage.

The Louisiana Pipeline, 2028: all three known misses from the still-open 2028 cycle are Louisiana kids who signed with Alabama — Amari Erickson (DE), Darian Weathersby (DE), and Jonathan Bowen (3★ T). Alabama is currently out-recruiting Southern in its own backyard before this cycle has even closed.

SEGMENT 09

The Takeaway

Three cycles in, the pattern is consistent: Southern's class rankings sit in the 60s nationally, driven by volume 2-star signing rather than top-to-bottom talent, and that tier's hit rate is close to a coin flip. But the program has landed two legitimate program-altering prospects late in that same window — a Doak Walker winner at 4-stars and a true 5-star in Carlos Allen — proving the staff can win at the top of the board when it matters, even while the middle and bottom of the class remain inconsistent.

The portal tells almost the same story from a different angle: the headline hit (Gavin Hoffman) came in as a 2-star afterthought, while three actual contributors were lost for good to a revolving door Southern has yet to win a single rematch in. And with the 2028 cycle already down three Louisiana prospects to Alabama before a single signee beyond Dee Stephens is locked in, the in-state pipeline is the storyline to watch heading into next year's edition of this chronicle.
