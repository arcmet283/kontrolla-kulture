SEGMENT 01

Before Southern

Head Coach · Southern Jaguars · Hired 2025

Teddy Graves

Eleven years on NFL sidelines — four as a quality-control assistant, five as a position coach, two coordinating special teams — without ever getting the call that turns an assistant into a head coach. Three different franchises, three different coordinators, and by his own account, three different philosophies he picked apart and kept what worked.

When Southern went looking for someone to rebuild a program picked to finish at the bottom of a crowded American Conference, they weren't looking for a name. They were looking for someone who'd coached under real pressure without the ego that usually comes with it — someone who'd spent a decade being told what to do well enough to know exactly what he'd do differently with the final say.

He said as much the day he took the job: "We're not out-recruiting anybody here. We're going to out-work them." That line is spray-painted on a wall inside the team's weight room today. His full introductory press conference is in the 2025 Season Recap.

SEGMENT 02

Year One: The Bet Takes Shape

The identity showed up immediately. Southern finished 2025 with the best pass defense in the country — and one of the worst run defenses, exactly the trade-off Graves promised in his first press conference. Establish the run on offense, dare teams to run on you on defense, and bet that a shutdown secondary covers for a leaky front seven long enough to find out what the roster could become.

The roster he built it with came in almost entirely without blue-chip ratings attached — this program's name and budget weren't landing five-stars in year one, so his staff went looking for players other schools had passed on. Mark Fiaseu, an afterthought on every recruiting board but Southern's, finished the year as the best player on the roster. The bet paid off on its very first try.

SEGMENT 03

Year Two: Same Identity, Higher Stakes

8–6, a conference championship appearance, and the program's first bowl bid — and the exact same split underneath it. Pass defense finished 2nd nationally. Run defense finished 132nd, barely different from year one's 135th, despite an entire offseason of defensive transfer additions brought in specifically to fix it. Graves didn't walk it back. Asked about it late in the season, he was blunt: the plan wasn't to fix the run defense overnight, it was to win anyway while it got built the slow way.

SEGMENT 04

Year Three: No Longer A Theory

Three tracked seasons, three finishes with a pass defense ranked in the nation's top 2 and a run defense stuck in the bottom 10. Nothing this program has tried has moved that second number even once. At this point it reads less like a weakness that hasn't been fixed and more like Graves' actual bet, confirmed a third straight time: dare opponents to run on you while the secondary locks the game down through the air. The record dipped to 6–6 and the bowl bid didn't come — but the identity itself never wavered, through a true freshman quarterback taking over mid-season and everything else that went wrong around it.

SEGMENT 05

The Philosophy, In Full

Strip away three years of box scores and the program's identity is simple: establish the run first, then lean on a secondary that's coached to take away the pass and dare opponents to try to run through it instead. It isn't subtle, and it isn't an accident — it's a trade-off Graves has made on purpose every single year, betting that a shutdown pass defense wins more games than a leaky run front loses.

The roster underneath that scheme is almost entirely developmental. The overwhelming majority of every signing class since he arrived — high school and transfer portal alike — has come in without a blue-chip rating attached. Graves' staff is in the business of finding players other programs passed on and getting more out of them than their recruiting grade ever promised; at a program with Southern's name recognition and budget, the alternative isn't really on the table. The results, three years running, are a team that handles business against the teams it's supposed to beat and has to scrap for everything against the ones picked ahead of it.

SEGMENT 07

What's Next

Three seasons in, Graves is 19–19 at Southern with one bowl trip, zero conference titles, and an identity that hasn't blinked once. Whatever comes next for this program, it's going to come from the same two bets he made on day one: run the ball, and make the other team beat you through the air to beat you at all.
