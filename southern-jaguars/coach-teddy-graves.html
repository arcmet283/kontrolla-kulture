SEGMENT 01

Eleven Years, No Call

Head Coach · Southern Jaguars · Hired 2025

Teddy Graves

Eleven years on NFL sidelines — four as a quality-control assistant, five as a position coach, two coordinating special teams — without ever getting the call that turns an assistant into a head coach. Three different franchises, three different coordinators, and by his own account, three different philosophies he picked apart and kept what worked from each.

He interviewed for head jobs twice before Southern and didn't get either. Both times, by his own telling, the feedback was some version of the same thing: good coach, no track record of being the guy in charge. The only way around that problem was somebody willing to be the first to hand him the keys.

SEGMENT 02

Why Southern Called

Southern wasn't hiring for a name. The program was picked to finish at the bottom of a crowded conference, with a roster that had been passed over by better-funded schools for years, and a budget that wasn't going to change that on its own. What the administration wanted was somebody who'd coached under real pressure without the ego that usually comes with it — somebody who'd spent over a decade being told what to do well enough to know exactly what he'd do differently with the final say.

Graves fit that search in a way a bigger name wouldn't have. No head-coaching record meant no outside expectations to manage, and eleven years without his own program meant he'd had a long time to think about exactly how he'd run one if anybody ever let him.

SEGMENT 03

Taking The Job

He said as much the day he took it: "We're not out-recruiting anybody here. We're going to out-work them." That line is spray-painted on a wall inside the team's weight room today. It wasn't a slogan picked for a press release — by every account from inside the building, it's the literal operating plan, repeated in meetings often enough that new hires hear it before they've unpacked their office.

"We're not out-recruiting anybody here. We're going to out-work them."
SEGMENT 04

The Approach He Walked In With

Graves came in with a specific plan, not a vague one: establish the run on offense, build a defense that makes teams beat it through the air to win, and fill the roster with players other programs had passed on rather than chase recruits Southern's budget was never going to land anyway. It's a scheme built around what he actually had to work with, not what he wished he had — the kind of plan eleven years of watching other people's rosters get built teaches a coach to make.

How that plan has actually played out, season by season, is its own story — the full accounting is in each year's season recap.
