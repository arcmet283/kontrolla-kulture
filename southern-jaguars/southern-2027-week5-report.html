The preview called this "year three: rebuild around the record" and warned the quarterback room was the single biggest open question on the roster. Four games in, that question has answered itself in the worst possible way: the presumptive starter got benched after three interceptions in Week 2, tore his shoulder in Week 3, and a true freshman is now running the offense. Southern is 1–3, 0–2 in conference play, sitting 12th of 14 teams in the American, and Jordan Molinich — the reigning Doak Walker winner, the best player in college football five months ago — has 374 rushing yards and two fumbles through four games, 6th in his own conference. This report stays close to Southern's own season; the national landscape gets its own companion piece.

Week 1 was as good as it gets: a road win at Stanford decided by a 55-yard game-winning touchdown from Ty Wilson to Gavin Hoffman with 9 seconds left, Southern outscoring the Cardinal 17–7 in the fourth quarter alone. Everything since has gone the other direction. North Texas held Molinich to 10 total rushing yards and forced three first-half interceptions out of Wilson before benching him. Texas Tech, exactly the measuring-stick game this preview flagged, turned into a 30-point rout — Southern managed only 27 points, 17 of them in garbage time in the third quarter, while Wilson (hurt mid-game) and freshman James Moody never found rhythm. And Tulane, the team that beat Southern for the AAC title last December, beat them again in the rematch this preview specifically flagged as "worth avenging" — Molinich fumbled at the goal line in a pivotal moment, and Moody threw three more interceptions in his first career start.

SEGMENT 02

The Quarterback Collapse

The preview flagged this as the single biggest open competition on the roster, with Ty Wilson the "presumptive favorite by default." Four games later, default has become crisis. Wilson threw three interceptions in the first half against North Texas and was benched. He returned to start against Texas Tech, went down again in the second quarter with a torn shoulder muscle, and is now out roughly six weeks — a timeline that erases the rest of this evaluation window entirely and likely a good chunk of the schedule beyond it. His numbers across the two-plus games he played: a combined 10-of-39 passing for 230 yards, 2 touchdowns, and 2 interceptions. In his place, true freshman James Moody (a two-star, 106th-ranked position signee) has been thrown into the deep end immediately: 18-of-42 for 375 yards, 3 TD, and 4 INT combined across his two appearances, including a 3-interception, 8-of-28 line in his first career start against Tulane. There is no third scholarship quarterback with meaningful experience on this roster — whatever happens over the next six weeks happens with a true freshman running the offense, full stop.

SEGMENT 03

What Happened To Molinich

Reigning Doak Walker Winner · Now 6th In Conference Rushing
Jordan Molinich HB · SO
73 car, 374 yds, 1 TD, 2 fumbles

A year removed from 2,349 yards and the national rushing record, Molinich has been a non-factor in two of his four games this season: 10 total rushing yards against North Texas, 61 yards on 20 carries against Texas Tech. His best game, 164 yards and a touchdown against Tulane, ended with a fumble at the goal line during what the game log calls "a pivotal moment." Some of this is almost certainly scheme — opposing defenses have had a full offseason to build a gameplan specifically around stopping the reigning national rushing champion, and a shaky, injury-ravaged quarterback room gives them no reason to respect anything else. But the fumbling is a new and separate problem: two lost balls in four games from a back who didn't have a documented fumbling issue in his historic freshman season. Whatever the offensive line additions were supposed to fix this offseason, they haven't yet shown up in Molinich's per-carry averages.

SEGMENT 05

A New Hero: Gavin Hoffman

Receiving Leader · 3rd in Conference in Yards, Tied 1st in TDs
Gavin Hoffman WR · JR (RS)

A transfer addition from Missouri, flagged in the preview as part of an offensive-line-and-tight-end-heavy portal class. He's the one skill-position piece of that haul who's already delivered — including the Week 1 walk-off touchdown catch against Stanford.

With Jonathan Truss presumably still working through a shorthanded receiving room (he's not the team's leading receiver through four games, a real shift from the preseason expectation) and Molinich neutralized on the ground, Hoffman has become the offense's most reliable weapon almost by default. Whether that holds once Wilson returns from injury, or whether Moody keeps finding him at the same rate, is one of the more interesting subplots of the next six weeks.

Southern sits 12th of 14 — ahead of only UTSA and Charlotte, both winless in conference play. North Texas, the team that beat Southern 10–30 in Week 2, is off to a perfect 4–0 start atop the standings, and Tulsa (this year's Week 8 opponent) is right behind them at 3–1. SEGMENT 09

Molinich Is Still Heisman-Listed. Here's Why That Matters.

Despite the fumbles, despite 6th in his own conference in rushing, despite a season that has looked nothing like 2026 — Jordan Molinich is still on the Heisman watchlist, logging his 4th straight week. That's a real signal about how much credibility his 2026 season banked: voters and pollsters are still watching him play through a rough stretch rather than dropping him the moment the numbers dipped, the exact opposite treatment this report series documented all of last season, when a historic year produced zero watchlist weeks. Whether that patience survives another six games without him finding his 2026 form is the single most interesting subplot left to track. SEGMENT 10

What's Left

Nine games remain, and the path back starts immediately after the Week 5 bye with a run of opponents Southern is favored against on paper: ECU, Army, Tulsa, Tennessee St., and Temple all sit at or below Southern in the American standings or are new non-conference additions with no established form. The real test is whether a true freshman quarterback and a fumble-prone Molinich can be trusted to close out winnable games, something this roster hasn't had to prove yet under this level of adversity. Ty Wilson's six-week recovery timeline lines up almost exactly with the Week 11 trip to Memphis — last year's most volatile fixture, a 66–10 blowout win in 2025 that flipped into a 35-point loss in 2026 — which means his potential return could land squarely in the season's most unpredictable rematch.

The honest read at the quarter pole: this is a talented, shorthanded team absorbing the worst-case version of its preseason questions all at once. The defense's pass coverage and the offense's redzone execution are still elite, proof the underlying infrastructure survived the roster turnover. Everything else — the quarterback room, the run defense, Molinich's ball security, the receiving corps beyond Gavin Hoffman — is either broken or unproven. Nine games is enough time to find out which of those problems are real and which are just bad luck compounding at the worst possible moment.
