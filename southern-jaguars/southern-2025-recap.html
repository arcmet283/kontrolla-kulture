Year One: 5-7, And A Star Is Found

Final Record5-7, 4-5 AAC
RankNo. 136 → No. 84
Recruiting59th Nationally
BreakoutMark Fiaseu, HB

This is where the chronicle begins. Southern opened this save at No. 136 nationally, a program with no established identity and a schedule that didn't do it any favors. Year One ended 5-7 — not a winning season, but a program that climbed 52 spots in the rankings, found its first real star, and set up a recruiting class that would define the next three years.

A season of two extremes: the 6-59 loss to Army is still the worst defeat in this chronicle's record, and it came sandwiched between a 52-point outburst against ECU and a brutal four-game skid. But the closing stretch is the real story — three straight blowout wins to close the year, including a 56-point margin at Memphis, the single largest win in the program's history at that point.

SEGMENT 03

The Mark Fiaseu Chronicle

Fiaseu is this program's first real star. 265 carries for 1,647 yards, plus 52 catches for 428 more, for 19 total touchdowns — the 2nd-most rushing yards in the entire NCAA that season. He won National Offensive Player of the Week three separate times (Weeks 5, 12, and 13), capping his run with a 414-yard, 5-touchdown all-purpose explosion in Week 12. He finished the year 2nd Team All-American and 1st Team All-Conference, then heard his name called in the NFL Draft. Every rushing record he set would eventually fall to Jordan Molinich, but Fiaseu is the one who proved this program could produce a star at all.

SEGMENT 05

Transactions: Who Left

The departures were modest for a first season: Corey Grant transferred to Kansas, and Jamari Golden's departure was rescinded (he stayed). Two of the season's headline names went to the NFL Draft — Fiaseu and cornerback Brennon Fonua, both riding 2025 All-Conference honors into the league.

SEGMENT 07

The National Championship

Notre Dame beat Texas Tech 34-31 for the national title, capping a bracket that saw Indiana knock out Alabama in Round 1 and Miami take down Wisconsin in the Orange Bowl. Fernando Mendoza (Indiana) won the Heisman Trophy behind a run that also carried Indiana deep into the playoff field. Mike Uremovich (Ball St., 9-4) won the Best HC Award — a non-champion winning that award, a pattern that would repeat the following year too.

SEGMENT 08

Next Class: The 2026 Recruiting Haul

This is the class that would eventually define the program. Headlined by three 4-stars — Jordan Molinich (HB, national position rank 28th), Christopher Shelton (CB, rank 13th), and Matt Brown (ATH) — plus a loaded transfer haul that included Christian Thatcher (Utah), Devin Sanchez (Oklahoma St.), and DJ Hill-Smith (New Mexico St.). Ranked 59th nationally at signing, this class would go on to produce a Doak Walker Award winner, two straight national rushing champions, and the defensive cornerstone that anchored the next two seasons. Not bad for a program that had just finished 5-7.

SEGMENT 09

The Takeaway

5-7 doesn't look like much on paper, but this season did exactly what a Year One needs to do: it found a star (Fiaseu), it revealed a real defensive identity (elite pass coverage, porous run defense) that would persist for years, and it landed a recruiting class that would carry the program's next three seasons. The record was modest. Everything it set up was not.
