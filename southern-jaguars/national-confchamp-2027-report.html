SEGMENT 01

The Ten Championship Games

Big 12 · Arlington, TX
Texas Tech (10–2) vs. Iowa State (12–0)

The headline game of the weekend. Iowa State is the nation's last unbeaten team; Texas Tech is the program that played for the national title two years ago in this same tracked window. Whoever wins walks straight into the top of the CFP field.

SEC · Atlanta, GA
Ole Miss (9–3) vs. Texas A&M (11–1)

Texas A&M enters as the clear favorite and the SEC's last realistic 1-loss playoff hope.

Big Ten · Indianapolis, IN
Nebraska (10–2) vs. Michigan (11–1)

Michigan enters with the better record and a chance to lock up a top-4 seed.

ACC · Jacksonville, FL
Miami (10–2) vs. UNC (10–2)

A genuine coin-flip on paper — identical records entering the title game.

American · Chicago, IL
North Texas (8–4) vs. USF (8–4)

North Texas is one of the two teams that beat Southern this season, a 30–10 win back in Week 2 — both finalists finished three full conference games ahead of the three-way tie Southern ended up in.

Mountain West · San Francisco, CA
San Jose State (7–5) vs. Wyoming (6–6)

The lowest combined win total of any title game this weekend.

CUSA · El Paso, TX
Missouri State (8–4) vs. Kennesaw State (9–3)
MAC · Detroit, MI
Ball State (8–4) vs. Central Michigan (9–3)

Ball State is back in a MAC title game for the first time since 2025 — see Segment 5 for why that's a meaningful return.

PAC-12 · Seattle, WA
San Diego State (6–6) vs. Texas State (7–5)
Sun Belt · Mobile, AL

SEGMENT 05

The Coaching Carousel: Full Journeys

Records alone don't tell the whole story of a coaching tenure. Here's every notable coach this chronicle has tracked, followed through every award winner they've produced and every bowl or championship game they've coached in across three seasons.

Joey McGuire · Texas Tech

Untouched by this window's coaching carousel — in Lubbock the entire tracked history
2025National Championship runner-up, lost to Notre Dame 34–31. Coached John Mackey Award winner Terrance Carter Jr.
202612–1, Big 12 champion (beat Cincinnati 35–23), Best HC Award winner, CFP Round 2 loss to Clemson in the Rose Bowl (48–34). Coached Jim Thorpe Award winner Marcus Ramon-Edwards.
202710–2, ranked No. 8 in the CFP field entering Championship Week — back in the Big 12 title game against Iowa State.

Three years, three deep national runs, and two different award-winning players developed under him at two different positions. No coach in this chronicle has a longer or more decorated résumé, and he's never once appeared on a hot-seat list.

Hugh Freeze · Auburn

2027 hot-seat entry, entering the season already employed before this window's tracking began
20267–6, Las Vegas Bowl loss to UCLA, 21–17.
20279–3, ranked No. 10 in the CFP field, built on a Week 4 upset of then-No. 1 Texas, 53–47. Sitting outside his own conference title game hoping for an at-large bid.

A full-turnaround story in one offseason: a losing bowl trip becomes a signature win over the defending national champion and a CFP-caliber season, all under the same coach who opened this year on a hot-seat list.

Brian Kelly · LSU

2027 hot-seat entry, entering the season already employed before this window's tracking began
2025Alamo Bowl champion, beat Kansas 42–7.
202611–3, CFP Round 1 win over UNLV (52–14), Round 2 Sugar Bowl loss to Georgia (38–35) — Georgia went on to reach, and lose, that year's national title game to Texas.
20278–4, held over from the hot-seat board despite a genuinely reasonable record.

The single strangest case on this board: a coach who beat a Round 1 CFP opponent and pushed that year's national runner-up in the Sugar Bowl just one year ago, now on notice for essentially treading water at 8–4. Name recognition and expectations, not results, are what's driving this one.

Jesse Minter · USC

2026 hire, from LA Chargers defensive coordinator
2027Debut season: 9–3, ranked No. 11 in the CFP field — the best first-year record of any coach hired in this chronicle's tracked window.

USC has never once cracked a Top 25 recruiting class across either cycle this chronicle has tracked. Minter's entire debut season was built on development and the transfer portal, not blue-chip recruiting — the single clearest proof in this whole database that a coaching hire can outrun a roster's raw talent level.

Adam Fuller · Virginia

2025 hire, from the LA Chargers' safeties coach
2026Debut season: 7–5 in the regular season, capped by a Sun Bowl championship over Houston, 27–21 in overtime — finishing 8–5. The best debut season of any coach in the 2025 hiring class.
2027Season 2: 4–8. A complete collapse from a bowl-winning debut to one of the worst records in the sport.

Still the single steepest one-year fall this chronicle has tracked for any coach, debut included — from a bowl trophy to a 4–8 season in the space of one offseason, with no roster overhaul severe enough on paper to explain it.

Mike Uremovich · Ball State

The first-ever Best HC Award winner tracked in this chronicle
20259–4, Best HC Award winner, lost the MAC Championship to Western Michigan by a single point, 28–27.
2026Missed the MAC title game entirely.
20278–4, back in the MAC Championship this week against Central Michigan — his first title-game appearance since that 2025 award-winning season.

A genuine full-circle story most of this carousel doesn't get: the very first coach this chronicle ever recognized with its top individual honor, back in the title game two years later after a quiet year in between.

SEGMENT 06

The Two Coaches Fired This Season

Wake Forest Fired In-Season

Jake Dickert finishes 2–10, his second straight near-winless season. Replaced by Brian Hartline, Ohio State's offensive coordinator.

Cincinnati Fired In-Season

Scott Satterfield finishes 3–9. Replaced by Tremaine Jackson, head coach at Prairie View A&M.

Both hires pull from opposite ends of the coaching pipeline — one a coordinator stepping up from a College Football Playoff-contending program, the other a proven head coach jumping up from the FCS level. Meanwhile, two coaches with even worse records survived the season entirely: Missouri's Eliah Drinkwitz (1–11) and Eastern Michigan's Chris Creighton (1–11) both kept their jobs through a season that ended two other coaches' tenures at less extreme records.

SEGMENT 08

What To Watch

Texas Tech and Iowa State in Arlington is the whole weekend in one game — a proven national title contender against the only unbeaten team left in the country, with a near-lock CFP seed on the line either way. Watch Auburn and USC too: both are outside their own conference championship games and both have the résumé to make the field anyway, which means Selection Sunday is going to matter more for those two programs than for almost anyone actually playing this weekend.

And keep an eye on the coaching carousel's bottom tier. Two firings already happened mid-season — a first for this chronicle's tracked history — and Missouri and Eastern Michigan both enter the offseason at 1–11, among the worst records anywhere in the sport, still employed for now. Bowl season and its immediate aftermath is exactly when the rest of this year's carousel gets decided.
