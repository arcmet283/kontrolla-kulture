The Best Team On Paper Isn't The Preseason No. 1, And That's The Whole Show

Title PickMichigan
Heisman PickIsaiah Mack, Penn St.
Bold CallAlabama Wins The SEC
Title DefenseFlorida St. Misses The Field

Every pick here leans on the same two inputs the rest of this chronicle's coverage has used all offseason: the preseason ranking (a proxy for reputation and recent history) and the preseason team rating (the actual roster-talent number, which hasn't lined up with the poll all offseason). Two straight national champions have come from outside the top four seeds. This is the year we find out if that's a real pattern or a coincidence.

This field is picked straight off the preseason poll order, which is exactly the assumption this desk has spent all offseason poking holes in. The team with the best case to blow that order up is Alabama — a 95-rated roster, the second-best in the country, currently projected outside the field entirely at preseason No. 17. If last year's pattern (a non-top-four seed winning it all) makes it three in a row, a talent gap that big sitting outside the field is exactly where to look for it.

SEGMENT 02

Bold Predictions

Bold Winner Pick
Michigan Wins It All

The best roster in the country, on paper, finally matches the trophy to the talent. Two straight champions have come from outside the top four seeds while the highest-rated team in the country went home early both years — this is the year that stops.

Dark Horse
Alabama Makes A Run

Kalen DeBoer is on the hot seat and his roster (95 rating, the second-best in the country) doesn't remotely match a No. 17 preseason ranking. If Alabama starts fast enough to force its way into the field, it has the talent to make this exact prediction show look foolish for leaving it out of the top 12.

Regression Watch
Florida St. Misses The Field Again

The defending national champion opens the year rated 80 overall — the lowest number tracked anywhere on this preseason board, a full 17 points below Michigan. No program in this chronicle has ever defended a title, and this roster looks like the least likely one yet to break that streak.

Only the four power conferences get a pick here — the rest of the national board (American, Mountain West, CUSA, MAC, Sun Belt, PAC-12) don't have enough ranked or rated teams tracked on this preseason board to call with any real confidence. Southern's own conference race is covered in full in the companion local show.

SEGMENT 04

Heisman Pick

Isaiah Mack (WR, Penn St.) is the pick. His 2028 season — 98 catches, 1,516 yards, 16 touchdowns — is the single best number on the entire preseason watchlist, and Penn State's preseason rating (87, 3rd nationally) gives him the contending-team backdrop Heisman voters have favored in three of the four winners tracked so far. Georgia's Jonathan Locklear (214-310, 3,272 yards, 35 TDs, 11 INTs) is the clearest alternative if Penn State stumbles. The name worth watching for a different reason entirely is Southern's Jordan Molinich: his numbers have been Heisman-caliber for three straight years, but he's only opened a season on this preseason watchlist once before (2027) in four tries, and this board has otherwise snubbed him more than it's recognized him — the single most reliable trend on this list is betting against him, right up until it isn't.

SEGMENT 05

Hot Seat Watch: Who Actually Goes

Ten coaches are on this offseason's hot-seat board, the largest class this chronicle has tracked. Not all ten survive the season. The two names worth watching hardest are the two that made this board a genuinely new kind of story: Alabama's Kalen DeBoer and LSU's Brian Kelly, the first time two programs at this level of national standing have shared a hot-seat class. DeBoer has the roster (95 rating) to talk his way off the list with a big season; Kelly, at a program with a shakier recent track record on this exact board, looks like the shorter-odds firing of the two if either goes.

SEGMENT 06

The Takeaway

The last two national champions came from outside the top four seeds, and this preview's whole thesis is that the gap between who's actually good and who's ranked that way is wide enough this year to make it three in a row. Michigan is the safe pick to finally close that gap itself; Alabama is the pick if the gap swallows another season's field entirely. Either way, the preseason poll and the preseason ratings are telling two different stories again, and by now that's not a surprise — it's the pattern.
