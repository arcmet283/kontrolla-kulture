The regular season is over, the conference championships are in the books, and thirty-seven games stand between now and a national champion — twenty-six standalone bowls, four Round One playoff games, four Round Two games, two semifinals, and a title game still fully blank. We're not just previewing this year's matchups tonight. Every team in this field has a paper trail — three prior tracked seasons of bowl games and CFP brackets — and we pulled every game of it before writing a single pick. Some of what we found reshapes the board entirely.

SEGMENT 01

The Regular Season, In Review

Start at the top, because it's the cleanest story in the country: Georgia beat Texas twice. Once in Week 4, 30-17, when Georgia was ranked 18th and Texas 7th and nobody outside Athens thought much of it. Then again in the SEC Championship, 35-28, with both teams sitting at 11-1 and a spot as the field's undisputed No. 1 seed on the line. There's a deeper layer to that rivalry, too: these two met for the national title in 2026, and Texas won that one in a rout, 54-14. Two years and a full roster turnover later, Georgia has now beaten this exact program twice in a single season. If this is a rebuilding of that old wound, it's a real one.

That's the other headline. Ken Ward threw for 3,831 yards and 45 touchdowns at Texas and won the Heisman Trophy outright — and his team never beat the team that finished the year ranked directly above it. It's not a contradiction so much as a reminder that these two things measure different stuff: Ward's trophy is about a season of individual production, Georgia's seed is about who found a way to win the games that mattered most.

UCLA had the season's other complete résumé: 12-1, with the only loss an upset at Oregon in Week 12, and a season sweep of Penn State — a 21-10 statement win in Week 9, when Penn State was the country's actual No. 1, then a tighter 35-32 Big Ten Championship rematch a week ago, by which point Penn State had slipped to No. 7. Beating a No. 1 team once is a signature win. Beating that same program twice in one season is as complete a case as anyone in the field can make — and UCLA is doing it with zero tracked CFP history of its own. This is a program walking into the playoff for the first time in this chronicle with a résumé that looks like a veteran's.

Missouri State is this season's best argument against dismissing the Group of 5. A 12-1 CUSA champion that walks into the bracket at the No. 4 seed carrying three major national awards — Jashawn Cooper won both the Bednarik and the Butkus, Dillon Hipp won the Mackey, and Ryan Beard was named the sport's Best Head Coach. And, for what it's worth on the résumé side: the last time this program saw postseason action, in the 2027 Independence Bowl, they were run off the field by California, 28-59. This is a much better roster than that one, but it's the one real data point suggesting this program has a ceiling problem on the big stage. Keep it in mind.

And then there's Southern. The Jaguars finished 8-5, runner-up in the American after a gutting 20-17 loss to Charlotte in the conference title game. Jordan Molinich carried the season anyway: 2,203 rushing yards and 18 rushing touchdowns, both tops in the country, on top of the Maxwell and Doak Walker Awards — his second Doak Walker, after also winning it as a freshman in 2026. He is a national award winner, twice over now, on a team that will not play in the playoff. Ward and Molinich are the two cleanest individual seasons in the sport this year, and neither of their teams is the one everybody thinks is best positioned to win it all.

SEGMENT 04

The Wildest Games Of The Year

Week 9
No. 4 UCLA 21, No. 1 Penn St. 10

The single most important result of the regular season. Every UCLA résumé argument this postseason starts here.

Week 9
No. 8 Ole Miss 51, No. 2 Texas A&M 45 (OT)

A shootout for the ages and the biggest upset of the year by seed. The reigning national champions were building their title defense even while the rest of the country was still learning their record.

Week 8
No. 2 Texas A&M 41, No. 6 Georgia 17

The other side of that coin — a month before the Ole Miss loss, Texas A&M looked like a genuine title contender, blowing out a Georgia team that would go on to finish the year No. 1.

Week 12
Oregon 32, No. 1 UCLA 25

UCLA's only loss, on the road, to an unranked Oregon team — the same Oregon team that opens bowl season as the pick in the ReliaQuest Bowl.

Week 12
No. 13 Florida St. 34, No. 5 Miami 31

Florida State's signature win of the season, and it came against the same team they'd face again for the ACC title a few weeks later — and beat again, 20-7. A clean regular-season sweep of a top-five team, from a program that's now 3-0 in tracked bowl games and making its first-ever CFP appearance.

Week 13
Mississippi St. 35, No. 11 Ole Miss 31

The reigning national champions limping into championship week on an unranked loss to an in-state rival. The exact kind of late fade that should worry anyone picking them deep — if they hadn't already banked a title.

Week 13
Minnesota 31, No. 3 Wisconsin 24

Wisconsin's only truly bad loss of the season, and it landed the week before they'd need to be at their best in the playoff's first round — against a Miami team they've already lost to on the biggest stage once before.

There's never been a rule capping this field at one Group of 5 team, and there's never been a lock to seed 12 — Navy was a No. 5 seed in 2025, UNLV a No. 9 in 2026, and 2027 had none at all. The committee has simply seeded every team, G5 included, on résumé, every year. Three G5 teams at once in 2028 isn't an outlier breaking a pattern; it's the strongest year the Group of 5 has had across this chronicle. And look at the playoff-experience column above: half this field — UCLA, Missouri State, Florida State, Oklahoma State, Akron, Charlotte — is making its first tracked CFP appearance. This is a genuinely new-look bracket, not a rerun of familiar contenders.

BRACKET

Round One

No. 9 vs. No. 8
Penn St. vs. Oklahoma St.

Medium Confidence — Penn St.
Penn State's season is inconsistent — a win over a top-two Wisconsin team, then a 34-14 embarrassment against an unranked Ohio State — but they've been to a CFP semifinal before, even if it ended badly. Oklahoma State is a clean program on the rise (a 2027 Guaranteed Rate Bowl win over Navy, now a Big 12 title) but has never been here. Experience, even bad experience, breaks the tie.

No. 10 vs. No. 7
Akron vs. Michigan

Akron's Davey O'Brien-winning quarterback and 11-2 MAC title are a great story for a program with zero tracked postseason history of any kind. Michigan's the far deeper roster, has a 2026 CFP Round 2 appearance already banked, and hasn't shown the same late-season cracks.

BRACKET

Round Two (Byes Await)

The four byes are locked into their Round Two sites and just waiting on an opponent: Missouri State hosts the Charlotte/Florida State winner in the Orange Bowl, Texas gets the Miami/Wisconsin winner in the Rose Bowl, UCLA draws the Akron/Michigan winner in the Cotton Bowl, and Georgia gets the Penn State/Oklahoma State winner in the Sugar Bowl. If Round One goes to form, that's Georgia-Penn State (a rematch of nothing, but Penn State's been in a semifinal before and Georgia hasn't been past a title game), UCLA-Michigan, Texas-Wisconsin (Texas won it all in 2026 and has been here before; Wisconsin's already lost on this exact stage to a team, Miami, that just showed it can be had), and Missouri State-Florida State — a 12-1 Group of 5 juggernaut with zero playoff pedigree against an ACC champion that's 3-0 in bowls and just proved twice it can beat a top-five team. That last one would be the round's best game by a mile.

SEGMENT 07

Bowl Season: All Twenty-Six Games, Full History

Every non-playoff bowl on the board, with every tracked prior postseason result for each participant — bowls and CFP appearances alike, going back to 2025.

LA Bowl
Mississippi St. (7-5) vs. UTEP (10-3)

Mississippi State's only tracked postseason trip was a loss (2027 ReliaQuest Bowl, 24-34 to Ohio State). UTEP has no tracked history but is simply the far better team — a Mountain West champion who still missed the CFP field at No. 14. Pick: UTEP, High confidence.

StaffDNA Cure Bowl
Florida Atlantic (7-5) vs. Southern Miss (8-4)

Both arrive with a bowl win in the bank — Florida Atlantic beat Utah 35-28 in the 2026 Armed Forces Bowl, Southern Miss beat Hawai'i 24-14 in the 2025 New Mexico Bowl. Southern Miss has the better record and more recent form to break the tie. Pick: Southern Miss.

68 Ventures Bowl
Miami (OH) (7-5) vs. Coastal Carolina (6-6)

Miami (OH) has real recent pedigree — Mason Bojorquez won the Biletnikoff and the national receiving-touchdown title out of this program last year, and the tracker had him marked to return. Coastal Carolina has no tracked postseason history. Pick: Miami (OH), High confidence.

Myrtle Beach Bowl
ECU (7-5) vs. San Jose St. (7-5)

Both have a tracked postseason loss and nothing else — ECU fell 21-31 to Boston College in the 2026 Dukes' Mayo Bowl, San Jose State fell 21-28 in the 2025 LA Bowl. Genuinely nothing to separate them. Pick: Toss-up.

Gasparilla Bowl
Auburn (6-6) vs. Bowling Green (7-5)

Auburn's season was rough, and their only tracked bowl trip is a loss (2026 Las Vegas Bowl, 17-21 to UCLA) — but they're carrying the Hornung Trophy winner in true freshman Bryan Matuatia, a player who can single-handedly flip a game with a punt return. Bowling Green has no tracked history at all. Pick: Auburn, leaning on the X-factor.

Famous Idaho Potato Bowl
USF (7-5) vs. USC (6-6)

This one actually has real playoff pedigree buried in it: USF made the 2027 CFP as the No. 12 seed (a Round 1 loss to Miami), and USC made the 2027 field too, upsetting a ranked Texas Tech team in Round 1 before a blowout Round 2 loss to Texas A&M. USC's is the better result of the two. Pick: USC, flipping off the deeper playoff résumé.

New Orleans Bowl
Iowa St. (8-4) vs. South Alabama (8-5)

South Alabama enters red-hot off a Sun Belt title but has no tracked postseason history at all. Iowa State is the opposite: 2-0 in tracked bowls (2025 Independence, 2026 Alamo over Boise State), and was the actual No. 2 overall CFP seed just last year before falling to the eventual national champion in Round 2. Their 2027 Heisman winner, Jaray Rask, is back and still on this year's watchlist, alongside fellow returnee Deshawn Bernard. This isn't close. Pick: Iowa St., High confidence.

Guaranteed Rate Bowl
Texas Tech (7-5) vs. Old Dominion (8-4)

Old Dominion has the better record, but their only tracked postseason trip was a 7-34 blowout loss in the 2025 GameAbove Sports Bowl. Texas Tech's résumé runs deep even in a down year: 2025 national runner-up (a title-game loss to Notre Dame after beating Miami in the semifinal), a 2026 Round 2 exit, and a 2027 Round 1 exit — a declining trend, but Joey McGuire has coached this program's entire tracked history and won Best HC honors off a 12-1 2026 season, and Groza-winning kicker Tyson Smith was tagged to return. Pick: Texas Tech, off the program pedigree.

GameAbove Sports Bowl
Ball St. (7-5) vs. Colorado (6-6)

Both have a tracked postseason loss and nothing else — Ball State fell 17-27 in the 2025 68 Ventures Bowl, Colorado fell 7-48 in the 2026 Guaranteed Rate Bowl, the worse margin of the two. Pick: Ball St.

Hawai'i Bowl
Illinois (6-6) vs. UNLV (7-5)

Illinois has no tracked history. UNLV made the 2026 CFP as a No. 9 seed before a 14-52 Round 1 blowout loss to LSU — a real playoff berth is still a real playoff berth. Pick: UNLV.

Pop-Tarts Bowl
No. 19 Clemson (8-4) vs. No. 25 Cincinnati (8-5)

The best coaching story of the year — Jeff Grimes turned a 3-9 Cincinnati program into an 8-5 team that just played Oklahoma State to a 42-32 shootout for a conference title — but Cincinnati is 0-2 in tracked postseason play, including a 10-52 loss in this exact bowl back in 2026. Clemson's recent bowl history is a stumble too (a 2027 loss to North Texas), but zoom out one more year and they made an actual CFP semifinal in 2026, beating Miami and Texas Tech before falling to Texas. Much higher recent ceiling. Pick: Clemson.

Snoop Dogg Arizona Bowl
UMass (7-6) vs. Boston College (7-5)

UMass has won this exact bowl before (2026, 38-21 over Northern Illinois) and added a 2027 Sun Bowl win on top of it — 2-0 in tracked bowls. Boston College is 1-0 (a 2026 Dukes' Mayo Bowl win over ECU), a real but thinner résumé. Pick: UMass.

Gator Bowl
No. 22 Arizona St. (8-4) vs. No. 23 Duke (8-4)

Duke's the cleaner bowl track record (a 2026 Cure Bowl win) against Arizona State's mixed 1-1. But Arizona State's 2027 Rimington Trophy winner, center Ron Pulu, was tagged to return, which matters a great deal up front. Close enough that either result is believable. Pick: Duke, Low confidence.

New Mexico Bowl
Houston (7-5) vs. Arkansas St. (8-5)

Houston's only tracked postseason trip was a loss (2026 Sun Bowl, 21-27 in OT to Virginia). Arkansas State went 1-1 in tracked bowls the last two years — a 2026 Cure Bowl loss to Duke, then a 2027 win in this exact bowl, 31-27 over Air Force. A team that's already won on this specific stage. Pick: Arkansas St., High confidence.

Birmingham Bowl
SMU (7-5) vs. Southern (8-5)

Full breakdown on Southern's own broadcast this week. The short version: this isn't the mismatch the raw record suggests. SMU is 1-1 in tracked bowls (a 2027 win) with a No. 19 recruiting class this cycle, and Southern's own only tracked bowl trip is a loss (2026 Gasparilla). Pick: Southern, tempered off Molinich alone, not the résumé.

Independence Bowl
No. 20 Tennessee (8-4) vs. Kennesaw St. (7-6)

Tennessee's won the Gasparilla Bowl in back-to-back tracked years. Kennesaw State does have a bowl win of its own (2027 New Orleans Bowl), but Tennessee's track record and ranking both point the same way. Pick: Tennessee, High confidence.

Music City Bowl
NC State (7-5) vs. BYU (7-5)

Both arrive with a clean bowl win somewhere in the tracked history — NC State took the 2025 Dukes' Mayo Bowl, BYU the 2027 Famous Idaho Potato Bowl. That cancels out rather than breaking the tie. Pick: Toss-up, an informed one.

Alamo Bowl
No. 17 Ole Miss (8-4) vs. Boise St. (7-6)

This one undersells itself badly at 8-4. Ole Miss is the reigning national champion — they ran the table from a No. 7 seed through Auburn, Iowa State, Texas A&M, and North Carolina in the title game last year. Their 2027 Mackey-winning tight end Julius Tisdol was tagged to return, stacking on top of Caleb Cunningham's Biletnikoff year. Boise State, meanwhile, is 0-2 across its last two bowl trips, including a loss in this exact bowl to Iowa State in 2026. Pick: Ole Miss, High confidence.

ReliaQuest Bowl
No. 18 LSU (8-4) vs. No. 21 Oregon (8-4)

Oregon's the team playing its best football of the two — the win over then-No.-1 UCLA in Week 12 is the best result either roster has, and they've also got a 2025 Las Vegas Bowl win banked. But LSU made the 2026 CFP as a No. 9 seed (a Round 1 blowout win over UNLV before a narrow Round 2 loss to Georgia), and two of that team's stars — Thorpe winner Marvin Holliday and Heisman-watch quarterback Thomas Teague — were tagged to return. This is closer than the seeding suggests. Pick: Oregon, Medium confidence.

Sun Bowl
Stanford (7-5) vs. Arizona (7-5)

Stanford's only tracked postseason trip was a rough one — a 21-49 blowout loss in the 2026 Music City Bowl. Arizona, by contrast, won the 2025 Gator Bowl, 29-26 over a UNC team that's since gone on to a national championship game appearance itself. Pick: Arizona, High confidence.

Las Vegas Bowl
California (8-4) vs. Ohio St. (7-5)

Both have a bowl win in the bank — California took the 2025 Sun Bowl, Ohio State the 2026 Music City Bowl — but California's is more recent quality and they've got the better record. Pick: California, High confidence.

Armed Forces Bowl
Louisville (6-6) vs. Nebraska (6-6)

Closer than it looks: Louisville actually won its only tracked postseason trip, 30-9 over Wyoming in the 2027 LA Bowl. Nebraska's own trip ended in a loss — a Round 1 CFP exit to UNC as the No. 8 seed — but a playoff berth, even a losing one, is a higher level of competition than a Group of 5 bowl win. Pick: Nebraska, Low confidence.

Holiday Bowl
No. 16 Texas A&M (8-4) vs. Texas St. (7-6)

Texas A&M faded hard down the stretch — three losses in their final four tracked marquee games — but Texas St. just won the PAC-12 as a true underdog and has played this same Holiday Bowl before, losing to Texas by one score (31-38) last year. Pulling the other way: Texas A&M made a genuine CFP semifinal in 2027, blowing out USC 52-3 in Round 2 before falling to eventual champion Ole Miss. That's real pedigree fighting a real slump. Pick: Texas A&M, real doubt either way.

Liberty Bowl
Minnesota (7-5) vs. Baylor (7-5)

This one flips once you check the venue history. Minnesota just knocked off a top-three Wisconsin team in their regular-season finale, the best win of anyone in this game — but their only tracked postseason trip was a 17-38 blowout loss in this exact bowl last year, to Tulsa. Baylor's own résumé is a clean 2025 Armed Forces Bowl win, and their Heisman-watch quarterback Walker White was tagged to return. A great regular-season win doesn't erase a bad recent memory on the same field. Pick: Baylor, Low confidence — flipped off the same-venue history.

Dukes Mayo Bowl
Alabama (7-5) vs. Memphis (8-4)

Alabama actually has the deeper recent CFP résumé — a 2026 Round 1 win over Michigan State before a narrow Round 2 loss to Michigan — but that roster's turned over twice since, and Memphis has more current-season momentum plus a clean 2025 bowl win of its own. Pick: Memphis.

SEGMENT 08

Bold Predictions For The Stretch Run

Georgia wins it all, closing the loop from 2026. They're the only team in this field that's already been blown out on the sport's biggest stage — a 14-54 title-game loss to Texas two years ago — and the only team that's already beaten this postseason's most credentialed program (Texas again) twice in one year. If Georgia and Texas meet again in this bracket, it's a rubber match with real history behind it.

UCLA reaches the title game in its very first tracked playoff appearance. Zero CFP history of any kind, and still the resume that looks most like a veteran's — two wins over a team that spent time at No. 1, one loss all year. A debut this clean doesn't come around often.

Missouri State's ceiling question gets answered fast. Three major national-award winners and a Coach of the Year isn't a bracket footnote, but their only tracked postseason memory is a 28-59 blowout loss. If they get past a Florida State team that's 3-0 in bowls and just swept a top-five Miami squad, that ceiling question is answered for good.

The reigning national champion doesn't get a chance to defend the title.
