Every pick in this preview is built the same way this desk has built every prediction all season: off the full 2025-2029 record for each program, not just what's happened in the last two weeks. That matters more in Week 3 than it did in Week 1 or 2, because several of this week's headline games involve teams whose national reputation and recent postseason results tell two very different stories. This is the full preview, picks included.

SEGMENT 01

The Marquee Game: Notre Dame At Ole Miss

Oxford, MS
#4 Notre Dame (3-0) at #8 Ole Miss (1-0)

Both programs are working from a title, and both have had complicated relationships with defending one. Notre Dame won it all in 2025, then took a step back every year since — a Round 2 exit in 2026 (39-34 to eventual champion Texas), a semifinal exit in 2027 (31-21 to UNC, who'd go on to lose the national title game to Ole Miss itself), and missing the twelve-team field entirely in 2028. Ole Miss, meanwhile, won its own title as a 7-seed in 2027, opened 2028 at a very respectable preseason No. 2 — and then didn't make the CFP field that season at all, the softest-looking preseason ranking in this chronicle to produce the hardest fall.

Both programs, in other words, know exactly how fast a championship window can close. Notre Dame is 3-0 and playing angry after last year's miss; Ole Miss is defending a home field it hasn't had to defend under this kind of national spotlight since the title run itself. The tiebreaker is trajectory: Notre Dame's is pointed up after last year's low point, Ole Miss's is pointed down after last year's miss.

Pick: Notre Dame
SEGMENT 02

Texas Tech Fights For A Pulse

Lubbock, TX
#7 UCLA (1-0) at Texas Tech (0-2)

Texas Tech's five-year arc is one of the steepest declines this chronicle has tracked, and it starts from a much higher peak than "Big 12 contender" suggests: this program was the 2025 national runner-up, beating Oklahoma and Miami en route to the title game before falling to Notre Dame, 34-31. From there it's been a steady slide — Big 12 champion outright in 2026, Big 12 champion again plus a CFP Round 1 loss in 2027, a step down to just a bowl trip in 2028, and now 0-2 to open 2029 with a real chance to fall to 0-3 at home. UCLA arrives from the opposite direction — national runner-up just last season, a program that beat Michigan by 30 and Miami by 8 in the same CFP run. One program's runner-up finish is trending toward irrelevance four years later; the other's is fresh. Neither trend favors the home team here.

Pick: UCLA
SEGMENT 03

Alabama's Bounce-Back Test — Against A Program With Its Own Real Résumé

Jacksonville, AL
#19 Alabama (1-1) vs. Jacksonville St. (2-0)

Alabama's history argues for patience despite the ugly Week 1 loss to UAB. This program made the 2026 CFP as a 12-seed, the lowest bye-adjacent slot in that bracket, and rode it all the way to the semifinals before finally losing to Georgia 37-35 — a real track record of getting hot at exactly the moments other teams fold. Alabama also still carries the second-best roster rating (95) of any team tracked on this year's national board, regardless of where the preseason poll put them.

But Jacksonville State is not a two-week story either, and treating it that way undersells a real program. This is a team that's won its bowl game in three straight tracked seasons — the 2025 Hawai'i Bowl (21-14 over Syracuse), the 2026 Independence Bowl (a 34-14 win over UNC, the same UNC that would go on to play for a national title the very next season), and the 2027 Myrtle Beach Bowl (45-31 over Kentucky). It produced a national award winner in 2027: defensive end Michael Hallock swept the Lombardi Award as the country's outright sack leader (16 sacks). And Southern itself has firsthand experience of how tough this program can be — a 10-40 loss at Jacksonville State back in 2025. The Week 1 upset of Florida State wasn't a fluke off nothing; it was a program with a legitimate track record finally getting the marquee opponent to match it.

Pick: Alabama (Closer Than The Rankings Suggest)
SEGMENT 04

Pattern Watch: Michigan's Big-Game Problem

This desk picked Michigan to finally win it all this year, on the strength of the best roster rating (97) in the country. The full history complicates that pick more than it confirms it. Michigan has won the Big Ten in both 2026 and 2027, entering the CFP as a bye team both times — and been blown out both times: 48-24 to a No. 9 UNC team in the 2027 Sugar Bowl, then 50-20 to UCLA in the 2028 Round 2. Great regular-season roster, back-to-back postseason disasters. Minnesota this week is nowhere near a real test of that pattern, but it's worth saying plainly now, before this team is a 1-seed again in December: the talent has been there for two years running, and it hasn't mattered yet when the games get biggest.

UNC's history carries real weight here: this is the same program that upset a No. 1 Michigan team by 24 points in the 2027 CFP before losing the national title game itself to Ole Miss, 21-17. Clemson isn't the empty résumé that matchup might suggest, though — this program made the 2026 CFP as an 11-seed, beat Miami 51-31 in Round 1 and Texas Tech 48-34 in Round 2, and didn't lose until the semifinals (27-24 to eventual champion Texas). Two real CFP runs, one more recent and one step deeper for UNC; the pick leans UNC, but Clemson has actually been to the final four more recently than the ranking gap between these two teams implies.

Miami's own CFP pedigree (a 2028 semifinal run that beat Wisconsin and Texas before finally losing to UCLA) makes their trip to an unranked Georgia Tech a lower-stakes formality than the ranking gap alone suggests. Georgia, despite already dropping out of the rankings once this season, has the deepest recent CFP résumé of anyone on this list — a 2026 national runner-up (blown out 54-14 by Texas, but a runner-up nonetheless) and a 2028 CFP bye team that lost a competitive Round 2 to Penn State, 28-21. And Penn State's own win in that game is a real data point for this week's game, too — but so is South Carolina's actual tracked history, which isn't nothing: a 2026 Gator Bowl win over Baylor, 31-13, the same season Southern itself lost 14-49 on the road at South Carolina. That head-to-head result against Southern doesn't decide a Penn State game, but it's a real data point about South Carolina's ceiling that shouldn't be waved away as an unranked non-entity.

The lesson from building this table out properly: almost none of these programs are actually blank slates. Appalachian State winning both its conference and its bowl game in the same season, Texas State stringing together back-to-back conference titles, Pittsburgh making an actual CFP appearance — none of that shows up if a team gets waved off as "unranked, no history" without checking. Iowa St. at West Virginia and Arizona vs. Florida Intl. are the two games on this list where that search genuinely came up empty, and that's stated plainly above rather than guessed at.

SEGMENT 07

The Takeaway

The four-year record says more than the two-week one does almost everywhere you look this week. Notre Dame and Ole Miss are two former champions on opposite ends of the same boom-bust cycle. Texas Tech's decline and UCLA's rise are both multi-year trends, not two-week ones. Alabama's 2026 CFP run is a real point in its favor against Jacksonville State on Saturday — but Jacksonville State's own three straight bowl wins and a 2027 national award winner mean that game is a real contest between two proven programs, not a ranked team steadying itself against a nobody. And Michigan — still the best roster in the country on paper, still this desk's pick to win it all — carries two straight years of postseason letdowns into a season where the talent gap has never looked bigger. History doesn't guarantee anything repeats. It's still the best information available before the games are actually played, for every team on the schedule, not just the ranked ones.
