SEGMENT 01

Notre Dame's Turnaround: 0-3 To 4-3

Voss
Play-by-Play
Renee, last time we were on this desk, Notre Dame was 0-3 and we were asking whether the season was already lost. Four straight wins later, they're 4-3 and back in the Top 25 at No. 23.
Castellan
Analyst
And it's worth remembering exactly who we're talking about here. This isn't a program riding one good year — Notre Dame won the actual national championship in 2025, beating Texas Tech 34-31. They followed that with a CFP appearance in 2026 and a semifinal run in 2027, losing the Fiesta Bowl to UNC. A 0-3 start this September was the first real stumble this program has had in three tracked seasons, which is exactly why it felt like such a bigger story than a normal slow start would have.
Voss
Play-by-Play
Which makes the bounce-back read differently too — less "team finds itself" and more "team that's been elite for three straight years remembers how to be elite."
SEGMENT 02

Iowa State's Collapse Out Of The Rankings

Voss
Play-by-Play
Going the other direction, and this one's brutal: Iowa State was No. 4 in the country two weeks ago. They're not in the Top 25 at all this week.
ON SCREEN: WK5 — #21 ARIZONA ST. DEF. #4 IOWA ST. 38-19 · WK6 — CINCINNATI DEF. #14 IOWA ST. 31-28
Castellan
Analyst
The first loss to Arizona State dropped them from 4th to 14th, and the second loss to Cincinnati the very next week knocked them out of the rankings entirely. And this isn't a one-year mirage falling apart — this was a genuine three-year climb. A modest team in 2025, an Alamo Bowl-winning 10-3 season in 2026, and then last year's full breakout: Big 12 runner-up, a No. 2 CFP seed, a Heisman winner in Jaray Rask, and Matt Campbell taking home the Best HC Award. That's the steepest one-week fall we've tracked for a program that had actually built toward it for three straight seasons.
Voss
Play-by-Play
And Rask himself is a smaller version of the same story — still on this year's Heisman Watchlist, but only four weeks deep, modest company for a reigning winner. When the team craters like this, it drags the individual storyline down with it.
SEGMENT 03

Five Unbeatens Left, Miami And Penn State Lead

Voss
Play-by-Play
Five teams still without a loss through six or seven games: Miami and Penn State at 6-0 up top, then Wisconsin at 5-0, and UCLA and Georgia both at 6-0 just behind them.
Castellan
Analyst
Penn State's actually the bigger riser of the two — they were sitting at 6th on our last check-in and have climbed all the way to No. 2 while staying unbeaten. Miami's held a top-3 spot the whole way and now sits at No. 1 outright. If both keep winning, that's the two-team race the committee's going to be staring at heading into November.
SEGMENT 04

Texas Tech's Four-Peat Bid Keeps Fading

Voss
Play-by-Play
We flagged Texas Tech's bid for a fourth straight conference title a couple checkpoints back, right after their first loss dropped them to 12th. It's gotten worse since.
Castellan
Analyst
They're 3-2 now and down to 16th. Two losses this deep into the season doesn't kill a conference-title bid outright, especially in the Big 12, but it's a much steeper climb than it looked in September. Miami's path, meanwhile, is still spotless.

SEGMENT 06

Heisman Watch: Still No Room For The Nation's TD Leader

Voss
Play-by-Play
Fifteen names on the national Heisman Watchlist this week, mostly quarterbacks and backs off the unbeaten and one-loss group — Stephon Benjamin at Auburn, Jerrian Parker at Miami, a couple from SMU.
Castellan
Analyst
And once again, worth flagging for anyone following the Southern Jaguars broadcast: Jordan Molinich leads the entire NCAA in rushing touchdowns right now, 10 through six games, and he's still not one of the fifteen names on this list. He actually lost the rushing yardage crown this week by just four yards to Miami's Jerrian Parker — who is on the list. Same story as every checkpoint we've had this season. And this is the same back who won the Doak Walker Award as a true freshman in 2026 with 2,349 rushing yards, the NCAA's outright national leader that season, then got only modest watchlist recognition the year after — four weeks' worth. Now, in his best statistical season since that freshman year, he's off the list entirely. A player with a national award already on his shelf is trending the wrong way, not the right one. Elite production on a program still building its national profile just isn't getting the same look.

SEGMENT 08

Predictions From Here

Voss
Play-by-Play
Let's go storyline by storyline, Renee. Miami versus Penn State for that No. 1 spot — who's still standing at the end?
Castellan
Analyst
Miami. They've held a top-3 ranking the entire season, including the stretch where Notre Dame, Alabama, and Ohio State were all cratering — that's a program that's been consistently good against a schedule that's produced chaos everywhere else. Penn State's climb from 6th to 2nd is real, but Miami's the safer bet to still be unbeaten when these two are compared again.
Voss
Play-by-Play
Texas Tech's four-peat bid — dead or alive?
Castellan
Analyst
On life support, not dead. Two losses and a drop to 16th is a real setback, but they've won three straight conference titles doing it as a Big 12 team, and the Big 12 doesn't need you unbeaten in September to still be playing for it in December. I wouldn't bet on a fourth straight, but I wouldn't rule it out either.
Voss
Play-by-Play
Does Iowa State claw back into the rankings?
Castellan
Analyst
This one I'm less confident on. A program that just proved it can build to a No. 2 seed has the talent to string wins back together, but back-to-back losses to unranked teams is a red flag about depth or coaching adjustments, not just a rough two weeks. Call it a coin flip whether they're back in the Top 25 by season's end.
Voss
Play-by-Play
And the one we keep coming back to — does Jordan Molinich ever crack a Heisman Watchlist?
Castellan
Analyst
Honestly, I'll believe it when I see it. He got modest, four-weeks-on-the-list recognition the year after his Doak Walker win, and now — leading the country in rushing touchdowns, playing for a team with its best conference position yet — he's fallen off the list entirely. That's moving in the wrong direction, not building toward anything. It clearly takes more than production alone from a program outside the rankings. If Southern climbs into the AAC's upper tier and Molinich stays anywhere near this touchdown pace, that's the version of events that finally forces the issue — but I'm not predicting it happens this year.
