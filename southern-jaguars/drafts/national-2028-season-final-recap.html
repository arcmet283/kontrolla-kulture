Florida State Wins It All As A 5-Seed. Notre Dame Doesn't Even Make The Field.

National ChampionFlorida St. 38, UCLA 31
HeismanKen Ward, Texas
Best HCRyan Beard, Missouri St. (12–1)
Notre DameMissed The CFP Entirely

For the second straight year, the national champion did not enter the playoff as a top-four seed. Florida State climbed from the No. 5 slot through four straight ranked opponents to beat UCLA 38–31 in the title game, capping a season that also saw preseason No. 1 Notre Dame miss the twelve-team field completely for the first time in this chronicle's tracking. This is the full national recap: the bracket, the awards, the coaching carousel's quietest year on record, and the four-year Heisman lineage that just added a new name.

Florida State's title run started with a No. 5 seed and got harder every round: a 12-seed upset bid from Charlotte in Round 1, a 63-point demolition of Missouri State's Cinderella season in Round 2, and back-to-back one-score wins over Penn State in the semifinal and UCLA in the final. Quarterback Sam Williams was the engine the whole way — 14-of-21 for 286 yards and seven total touchdowns in the Round 2 blowout, then 34-of-47 for 366 yards and four scores in the semifinal, then 24-of-37 for 318 yards, three touchdowns, and three interceptions in the championship game itself, a wild enough final that UCLA's Nico Iamaleava (23-of-35, 299 yards, 2 TD, a rush TD) nearly matched him.

It's the second year running the champion has come from outside the top four seeds — Ole Miss won it all as a 7-seed in 2027. Two straight unconventional champions is itself a real trend after this chronicle's first two tracked titles (Notre Dame as the No. 1 seed in 2025, Texas as a top seed in 2026) went to the favorites.

Georgia's win over Texas is the only championship-weekend result involving the CFP's top two seeds, and it cost Texas dearly — the Longhorns entered the weekend ranked No. 2 and were eliminated by Miami in the very next playoff round they played. Missouri State's CUSA title (12–1) turned out to be the high point of its season; Ryan Beard's group would win the Best HC Award and then get run off the field by Florida State in the very next round.

SEGMENT 03

The Bowl Season

All 26 non-playoff bowls are complete. A few results carry real weight: Texas Tech closed 2028 at 9–5 with a 33–7 Guaranteed Rate Bowl win over Old Domin­ion, a quiet ending for a program that was the 2025 national runner-up under this same coach, Joey McGuire, and has made a CFP-or-bowl appearance in every tracked season since. Iowa State, still fronted by 2027 Heisman winner Jaray Rask, beat South Alabama 31–14 in the New Orleans Bowl to close a season that never got back to the CFP heights of 2027. And Alabama, the program that would enter the 2029 offseason with its own coach on the hot seat, closed 7–6 with a 43–23 Dukes Mayo Bowl loss to Memphis — the kind of season-ending result that tends to accelerate exactly that conversation.

Jordan Molinich's sweep of the Maxwell and Doak Walker awards is the headline pairing on this board — a running back from a program that didn't make the twelve-team field winning the award for the sport's best player regardless of position. Missouri State's Jashawn Cooper matched him with his own sweep, the Bednarik and Butkus, on the same 12–1 CUSA-champion roster that produced the Best HC winner. Not one of this year's sixteen national award winners repeated from 2027 — the fourth straight year this board has turned over completely.

Four different winners in four tracked seasons, and Texas becomes the first program to produce two of them (Manning in 2026, Ward in 2028) — though neither stuck around long enough to defend the trophy, both bound for the NFL the same offseason they won it. Ward's path to the award ran directly through the coaching stability Arch Manning left behind: Texas reloaded at quarterback and kept producing a Heisman-caliber season without missing a beat.

SEGMENT 06

Notre Dame's Three-Year Slide

Notre Dame won the national championship in this chronicle's first tracked season, 2025, beating Texas Tech 34–31. Every year since has been a step down: a Round 2 exit in 2026 (a 39–34 loss to eventual champion Texas), a semifinal exit in 2027 (falling 31–21 to No. 9 UNC, the eventual runner-up), and in 2028, missing the twelve-team field entirely for the first time. The Irish opened the year ranked No. 1 and lost twice before Week 3 — the second loss, to Ohio, is the one that dropped them out of the rankings altogether, and they never got back in. Three straight years of shrinking postseason returns, from champion to missing the bracket, is as steep a decline as this chronicle has tracked for any single program.

SEGMENT 07

The Coaching Carousel's Quietest Year

Zero coaching changes among the programs this chronicle tracks happened in the 2027-to-2028 offseason cycle — the first fully quiet cycle on record, following six hires in 2025 alone and three more in 2026. That calm didn't last: heading into 2029, ten coaches are simultaneously on notice, the largest hot-seat class this chronicle has ever tracked, and for the first time it includes two genuine blue bloods — Alabama's Kalen DeBoer and LSU's Brian Kelly — alongside Arkansas's Jake Spavital, Auburn's Hugh Freeze, Iowa's Kirk Ferentz, Michigan State's Jonathan Smith, Missouri's Eliah Drinkwitz, TCU's Sonny Dykes, Vanderbilt's Clark Lea, and Virginia Tech's Brent Pry. A program of Alabama's and LSU's standing being on this list at the same time is new territory for this carousel.

SEGMENT 09

The Takeaway

2028 will be remembered as the year the bracket stopped rewarding the favorites two years running, the year Notre Dame's championship pedigree finally ran out entirely, and the year the coaching carousel went from its quietest cycle ever straight into its most volatile hot-seat class, with two SEC blue bloods on notice at once heading into 2029. Ken Ward gives Texas its second Heisman in three years without slowing down at all. And a mid-major program's running back, Jordan Molinich, just won two of the sport's biggest individual awards on a team that never got within a sniff of the playoff — a reminder that this bracket and this awards board don't always tell the same story.
