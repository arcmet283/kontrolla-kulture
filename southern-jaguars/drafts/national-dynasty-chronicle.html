Two national champions, two fired head coaches, two Heisman winners now in the NFL, and a hot-seat list that grew from seven names to eleven over the course of the season — that's what three tracked seasons of college football actually look like laid end to end. This is the complete chronicle: every champion, every major coaching story, every statistical leader, and the full historical arc of a now-finished 2027 season, built from the same data this report series has tracked from the very first championship weekend forward.

SEGMENT 01

The Champion Lineage

2025 National Champion
Notre Dame def. Texas Tech, 34–31

Built on Doak Walker winner Jeremiyah Love — the Heisman itself went to Indiana's Fernando Mendoza that year, the award and the title landing on different programs. Both players are in the NFL now. Notre Dame reloaded around a five-deep Heisman watchlist presence in 2026, then lost their playoff quarterfinal to eventual champion Texas.

2026 National Champion
Texas def. Georgia, 54–14

A 43-point revenge swing on the same fixture that beat them in the SEC Championship six weeks earlier. Heisman winner Arch Manning closed his career with the Heisman, Maxwell, Davey O'Brien, a title, and Texas's single-season and career passing records (4,831 yards, 50 TDs, 85 career TDs). He's in the NFL now, and Texas has opened 2027 at 7–2, No. 13 — a real but modest step back, not a collapse.

2027 National Champion
Ole Miss def. UNC, 21–17

The most chaotic bracket this desk has ever covered: a 7-seed beat a 12-1 Iowa State team, then a 12-2 Texas A&M team, then a 9-seed UNC team that had just knocked off the No. 1 overall seed to get there. Heisman winner Jaray Rask (Iowa St.) and Best HC Matt Campbell (also Iowa St., 12-1) both came out of the losing side of that bracket — proof the hardware and the trophy don't have to land on the same team. Two straight seasons have now ended with the reigning champion's best player gone to the NFL and the program taking a real, measurable step back the following year; whether that pattern holds a third time is the first thing to watch once 2028 kicks off.

The Heisman Board: From Mendoza To Manning To Now

Fernando Mendoza won the Heisman for Indiana in 2025. Arch Manning won it for Texas in 2026. Neither is on this year's board — both exhausted their eligibility and are now in the NFL, continuing a pattern where the Heisman winner has never repeated on the following year's watchlist in this dynasty's tracked history. The current board has grown to over twenty names, but two are pulling away from the pack:

SEGMENT 05

The Virginia Cliff

Adam Fuller opened 2026 with the best debut season of any 2025 hire — 7–5, a Sun Bowl championship — then opened 2027 in freefall, 0–4 out of the gate. Virginia pulled out of the tailspin to close the year 4–8, not the total collapse it looked like at midseason, but still a real, measurable step back from the debut. Fuller kept his job. Two seasons ago this was the breakout story of the entire coaching carousel; this year it's the steepest single-season swing this chronicle has tracked for any coach, debut included.

SEGMENT 06

Signature Upsets Of The 2027 Season

Week 4
Auburn 53, Texas 47

Hugh Freeze's Auburn team knocked off the reigning national champion, then No. 1, in a shootout. Still the season's headline result.

Week 5
Maryland 30, Michigan 27

Andy Kotelnicki's Maryland — a coach who went 4–8 in his own debut season — knocked off a top-2 team in his second year.

Week 8
Florida 45, Texas 20

Dana Dimel's Florida blew out a top-5 Texas team by 25 points — the third 2025-cohort coach this season to land a signature win over a nationally elite opponent.

All three of this season's marquee upsets share a common thread: they were pulled off by coaches from the same 2025 hiring class this report has tracked from day one, each of them a program-turnaround story that took two full years to fully arrive. The 2025 coaching carousel, uneven as it's looked at times, has now produced three of the season's biggest results.

SEGMENT 07

How 2027 Actually Finished

Neither Notre Dame nor Iowa State, the sport's last two unbeatens deep into the season, ended up hoisting the trophy — Iowa State made it furthest of the two blue-chip programs before Ole Miss ended the run in the Cotton Bowl. The Heisman race did turn into a running back's/dual-threat year, but the name that actually won it was Jaray Rask of Iowa State, not the Alabama or Mississippi State names that spent the fall atop the midseason watch boards. And the coaching carousel that looked ready to blow up by the time Brian Kelly's name hit a hot-seat board for the first time ever mostly fizzled: Kelly survived, and Jake Dickert at Wake Forest was the only coach from that eleven-name list who didn't.

Three years of data now say the same thing every single cycle: the champion's best player leaves, the champion takes a real step back, a totally new cast of statistical leaders and Heisman contenders replaces the old one, and at least one coaching story nobody saw coming in August ends up defining the whole season. 2027 followed that pattern again — which, at this point, isn't really a prediction anymore. It's just what this sport does, year after year, and the chronicle exists specifically to keep proving it.
