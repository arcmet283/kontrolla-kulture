SEGMENT 02

The Biggest Variable: Cameron Seals

Every pick above assumes competent, if unproven, quarterback play. That's not a safe assumption. Seals won the job in fall camp over both Joseph Foxworth and James Moody on the strength of his arm and athleticism, but he's never taken a real college snap, and this program has a three-year habit of making in-season quarterback changes when the answer at that position isn't working. If Seals struggles early — particularly in the brutal three-game road-opening stretch against North Texas, Kennesaw St., and Oklahoma — both Foxworth and Moody remain on the roster and in the conversation. A clean start from Seals makes 8–4 a floor; a rough one makes it a ceiling.

SEGMENT 03

Does The Run Defense Finally Turn?

Four straight seasons, Southern's run defense has finished in the national bottom 10: 135th, 132nd, 132nd, 134th. The pass defense finished No. 1 in the country last season, so this isn't a talent problem across the board — it's specifically a run-defense problem that's survived every transfer class and coaching emphasis tried against it so far. Prediction: it doesn't turn. A fifth straight season in the bottom 10 nationally against the run is the most likely outcome unless a name not yet on this roster emerges up front.

SEGMENT 04

Breakout Candidate

Cameron Seals Boom Or Bust

Beat out two players with real starting experience on arm talent alone. If the accuracy holds up against real competition, he's the best pure thrower this program has had.

Carlos Allen Overdue

The program's first 5-star signee enters his third season still without a truly featured role. With Gavin Hoffman gone to the NFL and the receiving room thinner, more touches are simply available.

Jaelen McCracken Encore

Broke the program's single-season sack record as a true freshman (15). Whether that was a rookie-year explosion or a real level has real stakes for how scary this defensive front can be.

SEGMENT 05

The Takeaway

8–4, bowl eligibility clinched with a game or two to spare, and a run defense that still can't solve its one persistent problem — that's the call. North Texas and Oklahoma are the two games this roster's own history says to worry about, both on the road, both to open the year, both before anyone knows what this offense actually looks like with Cameron Seals under center. The four true toss-ups — Kennesaw St., Charlotte, ECU, and UTSA — are where the season actually gets decided: break all four right and this is a 10-win team that finally gets over the hump against Charlotte and UAB; break all four wrong and 6–6 is the realistic floor.
