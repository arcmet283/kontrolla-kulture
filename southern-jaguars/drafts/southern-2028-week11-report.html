Two Straight Blowouts, And Molinich Owns Every Rushing Category In America

Since the Texas loss, this team hasn't come close to losing again. A 41-21 rout of Navy on the road, then a 45-21 rout of Temple, and Southern sits 6-3 with three games left — bowl eligibility already clinched. Jordan Molinich has been the story of both wins, and as of this week's national leaderboard he leads the entire country outright in both rushing yards and rushing touchdowns. This report stays close to Southern's own season; the national landscape gets its own companion piece this week — the deepest one this chronicle has ever produced.

SEGMENT 01

Two Blowouts: Navy And Temple

Week 8 · Away · AAC
Southern 41, Navy 21 Won

Foxworth was nearly perfect when he did throw: 9-of-13 for 162 yards and a touchdown. Molinich did the rest — 293 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns on just 25 carries, an 11.7 yards-per-carry average. Carlos Allen chipped in 49 yards and 2 touchdowns on only 3 carries, and Jason Davis added 87 receiving yards and a score. A road revenge game, in a sense — the only prior meeting between these programs was a home loss for Southern back in 2025.

Week 10 · Away · AAC
Southern 45, Temple 21 Won

Foxworth accounted for four total touchdowns — 2 through the air (15-of-22, 206 yards) and 2 more on the ground himself. Molinich kept rolling: 251 yards and a touchdown on 25 carries. Jason Davis had his best game yet, 6 catches for 100 yards. Back-to-back 40-plus-point performances against AAC opponents is as complete a two-week stretch as this program has produced.

Two straight 250-plus-yard games have pushed Molinich's national lead from contested to commanding. 1,717 rushing yards through nine games projects to well over 2,200 if this pace holds across the final three — within range of his own freshman-year program record (2,349 yards in 2026). He is still not one of the eighteen names on the national Heisman Watchlist, a snub that gets harder to justify with every game he plays like this.

SEGMENT 03

Foxworth's Best Stretch Yet

Foxworth's season line is now 120-of-209 for 1,758 yards, 15 touchdowns, and 5 interceptions — a 57.4% completion rate and a 3-to-1 TD-to-INT ratio that's climbed steadily since the USF benching in Week 4. Jason Davis has quietly become his most reliable target, up to 34 catches, 534 yards, and 3 touchdowns on the season. The two Foxworth-to-Davis connections in the Navy and Temple wins were a big part of why both games were comfortable rather than close.

SEGMENT 05

What's Ahead: The Bowl Math

Bowl eligibility is already locked up at 6 wins. What's left to play for is seeding and a possible tiebreaker path in the conference. Next up is UAB at home — Southern is 3-0 all-time against them, and the site-matched 2026 meeting (also at home) was a win, 38-34. UAB is 3-6, 2-4 this year, clearly the weakest team left on the schedule. After that comes Memphis (home) — Southern is 2-1 all-time, but the one home meeting in this series (2026) was a 24-59 blowout loss, a real warning sign the raw record doesn't capture, and Memphis is 6-3 this year, playing well. UTSA closes the season at home — Southern is 1-2 all-time, including a 3OT home loss in the last meeting at this same site (2026), and UTSA is tied with Southern at 5-1 in conference play.

SEGMENT 06

The Takeaway

6-3, bowl-eligible, riding a two-game blowout streak, and a running back who leads the nation outright in every major rushing category there is. UAB should be win No. 7. Memphis and UTSA are where this final stretch gets real — both have already beaten Southern at these exact sites before.
