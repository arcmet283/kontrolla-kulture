Southern's 2028 season ends at 8–6, tying the program's best-ever win total, built on the best defense this chronicle has ever tracked and undercut by a run of close losses at the two moments that mattered most: the AAC Championship Game and the Birmingham Bowl. This is the full recap — every game, the historic year Jordan Molinich just put together, a defense that finished No. 1 in the country in total yards allowed, and the two one-score defeats that kept an 8–6 season from becoming something more.

SEGMENT 01

The Season, Start To Finish

Southern opened 3–0, running through Kentucky (48–17), North Texas (31–7), and a road trip to Army (44–12) behind Joseph Foxworth's efficient passing and Jordan Molinich piling up yards in bunches. The first crack showed in Week 4 at USF: Foxworth was benched in the third quarter after going 5-of-19 with two interceptions, and backup James Moody came off the bench to throw for 165 yards and two scores in a 23–45 loss — the first sign of a season-long quarterback rotation that would resurface at the worst possible moment months later.

The Jaguars bounced back to beat ECU 42–19 on the road, then dropped an overtime heartbreaker at home to Louisiana (21–28) and a road game at then-No. 16 Texas (27–45) despite 223 rushing yards and three touchdowns from Molinich. A 41–21 win at Navy, keyed by a 293-yard, two-touchdown night from Molinich, steadied the ship heading into the bye.

The back half of the season was the Foxworth-and-Moody story in full: a 45–21 rout at Temple, then a genuine gut-punch — a 13–17 home loss to UAB, a program Southern had never lost to across three prior tracked seasons. Foxworth was intercepted three times in that one. The Jaguars answered with a wild 42–35 win over Memphis after Foxworth was benched again (0-for-6, 1 INT) and Moody came in and threw for four touchdowns, then closed the regular season with a defensive slugfest at UTSA, winning 10–2 behind ten tackles and two forced fumbles from Christian Thatcher. That closed the regular season at 8–4 (7–2 in AAC play), good for the No. 2 seed in the conference and a second straight trip to the title game.

That game, against a No. 14-ranked Charlotte team, is the one that stung longest until Birmingham topped it. Southern's defense forced four fumbles and recovered three, including one with under thirty seconds to play, but the offense could never find the end zone enough, and the Jaguars fell 17–20. It's the program's second conference championship game appearance in this chronicle's four tracked seasons, and its second defeat in exactly that spot. The first came in 2026, a 31–38 loss at Tulane. Southern is now 0–2 in conference title games, both one-score margins.

Then came Birmingham. Moody started, was pulled in the second quarter after going 1-of-5 with an interception and a fumble with Southern already down 24–0, and Foxworth's relief rally (9-of-22, 165 yards, 2 TDs, 2 INTs) and a 186-yard, one-fumble night from Molinich weren't enough to erase that early hole. SMU won 30–25, closing the season at 8–6.

Southern finishes 8–6 for the second time in this chronicle's four seasons, tying 2026's win total exactly — and both of those 8-win seasons ended in a loss at the very last stop, a conference title game in 2026 and a bowl game this year. The Jaguars actually cracked the CFP poll at 29th right after the AAC title game (at 8–5), the best ranking this program has posted at any point in this chronicle, before the Birmingham Bowl loss dropped the final number to 42nd. The AAC conference record of 7–3 includes the title-game loss to Charlotte; in AAC regular-season play alone, Southern went 7–2, its best conference regular season yet.

SEGMENT 03

Molinich's Record-Breaking Year

Maxwell Award · Doak Walker Award · 1st Team All-American
Jordan Molinich HB · JR
337 car, 2,488 yds, 18 TD (full season) — 1st in the NCAA in rushing yards and rushing touchdowns

Molinich led the country in rushing yards and rushing touchdowns for the third straight season, and this time the sport handed him two of its biggest individual honors for it: the Maxwell Award, given to the best player in college football regardless of position, and the Doak Walker Award, given to the nation's top running back. It's his second Doak Walker — he won it as a true freshman in 2026 (2,349 rush yds, a program record at the time) before an off-year in the awards conversation in 2027, when he still led the country in rushing yards (1,669) but collected no national hardware. This is the first time he's paired a national rushing title with a second major national award in the same season, and the first time he's won two national awards at once.

The 2,488-yard season is also a new single-season program rushing record, breaking his own mark of 2,349 set as a freshman. Across three varsity seasons (2026–2028), his career rushing total now sits at 6,506 yards — by a wide margin the most any player has ever produced in this program's tracked history, and he's back for a senior season in 2029 with a chance to add to it.

The award-announcement stat line, compiled after the AAC Championship Game and before the bowl, showed 309 carries for 2,302 rushing yards and 18 rushing touchdowns, plus 41 catches for 357 yards and 5 more scores through the air — meaning the committees were voting on a player who was also a legitimate receiving weapon, not just a workhorse runner. His 186-yard bowl performance pushed the season rushing total to its final 2,488.

The headline number is the total defense: 3,681 yards allowed, No. 1 in the entire country. It's the best defensive season this program has ever tracked, ahead of 2025's previous best (8th nationally) by a wide margin, and it comes with the pass defense also finishing No. 1 nationally. And yet the run defense — 134th nationally — is stuck exactly where it's been every year of this chronicle: 135th in 2025, 132nd in both 2026 and 2027, now 134th. Four straight seasons in the bottom 10 nationally against the run, even in the same year the pass defense set the program's all-time standard. Whatever this coaching staff has tried against the run, across four full recruiting and transfer cycles, it has not moved the needle even once.

The offense told a different story than the record suggests: total offensive yards actually regressed to 96th nationally, the worst finish since the very first tracked season, dragged down by a passing attack that finished 131st. The rushing offense (4th nationally) and the red zone offense (87%, 6th nationally) were both elite, which is exactly how a team with a historically bad 3rd-down conversion rate (34%, 135th — dead last among ranked offenses) still managed 31 points a game. Southern won a lot of these games on explosive plays and clutch situational football (72% on 4th down, 17th nationally) rather than sustained drives.

SEGMENT 06

McCracken's Freshman Record

Buried a little in Molinich's headline year: true freshman Jaelen McCracken just posted the best single-season sack total this program has ever tracked. His 15 sacks blow past the previous program record of 12, set by Amir Tracy in 2025 — and McCracken did it as a true freshman, not a senior anchoring a defensive front for the fourth year. He was the No. 15 position-ranked defensive end nationally at signing, a 4-star out of the 2028 class, and he delivered on that grade immediately.

SEGMENT 08

The AAC Championship, One More Look

AAC Championship · Home
Charlotte 20, Southern 17 Lost

Southern's defense forced four fumbles and recovered three — including one with under thirty seconds left, on a run stuffed by Jordan Molinich getting tackled on top of a Charlotte defender that helped keep the clock moving against the Jaguars' own offense. Moody split time with an injured Foxworth; Foxworth finished 6-of-11 for 78 yards and a touchdown, Moody 4-of-8 for 39 yards on the final, unsuccessful drive. Molinich ran 24 times for 99 yards without finding the end zone, and Carlos Allen's fourth-quarter fumble loomed over the loss. Matt Brown's 19-yard touchdown catch was the offense's only trip to the end zone.

SEGMENT 09

Birmingham Bowl: How It Actually Happened

Birmingham Bowl
SMU 30, Southern 25 Lost

James Moody started and was pulled in the second quarter after going 1-of-5 for 8 yards with an interception and a fumble, with Southern already down 24–0. Foxworth relieved him and nearly engineered the comeback anyway — 9-of-22 for 165 yards and 2 touchdowns, with 2 interceptions of his own. Molinich carried 28 times for 186 yards, and both he and Matt Brown caught a receiving touchdown, but the 24-point hole was too much to fully erase. SMU quarterback Cole Tyler's 19-of-32, 191-yard, 1-touchdown day was efficient enough to hold on.

SEGMENT 10

The Takeaway

8–6 undersells this season in one direction and oversells it in another. This program fielded the best defense in its history — a genuine No. 1 total defense, No. 1 pass defense — and produced the best individual season this chronicle has ever tracked, a Maxwell-and-Doak-Walker sweep from a player who's now the program's all-time leading rusher by a landslide with a senior season still to come. But it also lost the two games that would have turned a very good season into the program's first conference title and first bowl win, both by a combined eight points, both with a backup quarterback on the field at the worst possible moment. The run defense is still broken after four straight years. The passing offense took a real step back. And a roster that's about to lose Gavin Hoffman, Ricardo Jones, and J'Vari Flowers to the NFL in the same offseason has real work to do to replace what it's about to lose. That's where the story goes next.
