SEGMENT 02

The Bounce-Back: 42-19 At ECU

Week 5 · Away · AAC
Southern 42, ECU 19 Won

The response to the USF benching was about as convincing as it gets. Foxworth went 16-of-19 for 236 yards and 3 touchdowns without an interception — his most efficient start of the season. Molinich had one of his best all-around games of the year: 105 rushing yards and 2 touchdowns on the ground, plus 3 catches for 40 yards and 2 more scores through the air, four total touchdowns. Jaelen McCracken added 2 sacks, and Dre Still picked off a pass. A road measuring-stick win over a team Southern was tied with in the standings, settled decisively.

SEGMENT 03

The Overtime Heartbreak: 21-28 To Louisiana

Week 6 · Home · Non-Conference
Louisiana 28, Southern 21 Lost, OT

The first overtime game of the season, and the first time Foxworth's second half of the year has looked shaky: 14-of-29 for 239 yards, 1 touchdown, and 2 interceptions. Molinich still ran for 160 yards and a score, and Hoffman had a big day of his own — 2 catches for 99 yards and a touchdown — but it wasn't enough to escape with the extra period. A non-conference loss, so it doesn't touch the AAC standings, but it snaps what had been a clean run of results since the USF benching.

SEGMENT 04

Foxworth, Fully Reclaimed

The USF benching two weeks ago raised a real question about whether Foxworth would hold onto the job. Since then, he's started and finished both games outright — no sign of Moody relieving him again. The results were a mixed bag (a near-perfect 84% completion day at ECU followed by a rough 2-interception overtime loss to Louisiana), but the job security question itself looks answered: this is Foxworth's offense, full stop, regardless of how any single game goes.

SEGMENT 05

Molinich Loses The Rushing Crown By Four Yards

Six games in, Molinich has 950 rushing yards on 138 carries and 10 touchdowns. As of this week's national leaderboard, that's actually dropped him to 2nd nationally in rushing yards — Miami's Jerrian Parker has 954, a four-yard gap after Molinich had held the national lead outright at the last check-in. The upside: Molinich is now the outright national leader in rushing touchdowns with 10, ahead of every back in the country.

Worth the full context here, not just this year's number: Molinich signed as a 4-star in Southern's very first tracked recruiting class in 2025, then exploded as a true freshman in 2026 — 2,349 rushing yards, 20 rushing touchdowns, the NCAA's outright national rushing leader that year, the Doak Walker Award, and 1st Team All-American, still the program's benchmark season. 2027 was a real step down amid the quarterback chaos (1,669 yards, 11 touchdowns, playing behind three different starters), and 2028 has him trending back toward that freshman peak. His Heisman profile has actually moved the wrong direction since that freshman year: he got modest national recognition in 2027 (4 weeks on the watchlist, playing behind a rotating cast of quarterbacks), and this year — leading the country outright in rushing touchdowns, on a team with its best conference position yet — he's not on the fifteen-name list at all. A Doak Walker winner two years ago getting less recognition now, not more, is the real story.

By far the toughest test left on the schedule: a road trip to a ranked Texas team, non-conference, with nothing on paper suggesting Southern is favored — and no history to lean on either way, since these two programs have never met in the years this chronicle has tracked. Navy is a different story: Southern's only prior meeting with them was a 24-31 home loss back in 2025, Year One of the rebuild. This is a road rematch three years later, against a program that's been building a very different profile in the interim, and it's the last real gut-check before the final bye week. From there, a closing conference stretch (Temple, UAB, Memphis, UTSA) against teams all sitting at or below Southern in the standings — a real chance to close strong if the Texas and Navy road games don't derail the season.

SEGMENT 08

Predicting The Final Six

One quick update before the picks: Texas has climbed since that "No. 16" label was set on the schedule — they're No. 10 in this week's national poll at 5-1, having already knocked off then-No. 2 Alabama 31-28 earlier this year. That makes Week 7 an even tougher draw than it looked when the schedule was written. For every pick below, we weighted the same-site result over the overall series record where the two disagree — that's the approach that's kept this chronicle's predictions honest all season.

Texas is the one true toss-up-leaning-away pick with zero history to lean on either way, but the other five all have a same-site data point, and three of them point in a different direction than the raw series record would suggest. Memphis is the clearest example: Southern is 2-1 in the all-time series, but the only game actually played at Memphis's place in this stretch was a 35-point blowout loss, and Memphis is playing well this year (4-2). UTSA is a similar story — a losing series record, a home loss in the last home meeting that went to three overtimes, and a UTSA team currently tied with Southern itself for third in the conference. Navy is the opposite case: the only meeting was a loss, but it happened in 2025 at a different site against a completely different roster, and Navy's own form this year (2-3) doesn't scare anyone. Add it up and the projected finish is 7-5 overall, 6-3 in the AAC — three wins and three losses split evenly across these final six games, which would be this program's first winning record since the 8-6 breakthrough in 2026 and likely enough for its first bowl bid since that season.

SEGMENT 09

The Takeaway

4-2 with the best conference positioning this program has had through six games, a quarterback question that looks settled, and a running back who's still the best story in the building even after losing a national statistical crown by four yards. Texas on the road is a game Southern isn't expected to win — but nothing about this season so far has gone according to expectation, and a projected 7-5 finish would be this program's best since the 2026 breakthrough.
