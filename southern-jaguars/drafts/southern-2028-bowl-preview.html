SEGMENT 01

The Season, Start To Finish

Southern opened the year 3-0, running through Kentucky (48-17), North Texas (31-7), and a road trip to Army (44-12) behind Joseph Foxworth's efficient passing and Jordan Molinich piling up yards in bunches. The first crack showed in Week 4 at USF: Foxworth was benched in the third quarter after going 5-19 with two interceptions, and backup James Moody came off the bench to throw for 165 yards and two scores in a 23-45 loss — the first sign of the season-long quarterback rotation that would define the year.

The Jaguars bounced back to beat ECU 42-19, then dropped an overtime heartbreaker to Louisiana (21-28) and a road game at then-No. 16 Texas (27-45) despite 223 rushing yards and three touchdowns from Molinich. A 41-21 win at Navy, keyed by a 293-yard, two-touchdown night from Molinich, steadied the ship heading into the bye.

The back half of the season was the Foxworth-and-Moody story in full: a 45-21 rout of Temple with Foxworth accounting for four total touchdowns, a gut-punch 13-17 loss to UAB in which Foxworth was picked off three times, and a wild 42-35 win over Memphis after Foxworth was benched again (0-6, 1 INT) and Moody came in and threw for four scores. The Jaguars closed the regular season with a defensive slugfest, beating UTSA 10-2 behind ten tackles and two forced fumbles from Christian Thatcher, then finished 8-4 in the regular season and 7-2 in the AAC — good for the No. 2 seed in the conference and a trip to the title game.

That game, against Charlotte, is the one that will sting the longest. Southern's defense forced four fumbles and recovered three, including one with under thirty seconds to play, but the offense could never find the end zone enough, and the Jaguars fell 17-20, dropping to 8-5 on the season. It's the program's second conference championship game appearance in this chronicle's four tracked seasons — and its second defeat in one. The first came in 2026, a 31-38 loss at Tulane. Southern is now 0-2 in conference title games in this chronicle, both single-digit or one-score margins.

SEGMENT 03

Molinich's Historic, Award-Winning Year

Jordan Molinich led the entire country in rushing yards (2,203) and rushing touchdowns (18) for the second time in three years, and this time the sport noticed: he won both the Maxwell Award, given to the best player in the country regardless of position, and the Doak Walker Award, given to the nation's top running back. The Doak Walker is actually his second — he won it as a true freshman back in 2026 (2,127 rush yds, 20 rush TD, also a national sweep that year) before an off-year in 2027 in which he still led the country in rushing yards but lost the touchdown title to Mississippi State's Fluff Bothwell. This is, however, the first time he's paired it with a second major national award in the same season.

What makes it remarkable is the context: Molinich did this behind an offense that never settled on a starting quarterback, on a team that missed the CFP entirely. He is, at this moment, the best player in college football on a team that will finish its season in a non-playoff bowl. That gap — individual greatness on a team that didn't break through — is the story of this whole season.

SEGMENT 06

The AAC Championship, One More Look

Worth revisiting now that the dust has settled: Southern's 17-20 loss to Charlotte wasn't a blowout, it was a handful of possessions. James Moody shared time with an injured Foxworth for most of the game, and it was Foxworth who came back for the final drive, moving the offense inside Charlotte's 30 before time ran out on a Molinich run that ended on top of a defender. Molinich finished with 99 yards on 24 carries but no touchdowns and a fumble; the lone offensive score came on a 19-yard catch from Matt Brown. Charlotte advanced to the CFP as the No. 12 seed. Southern heads to Birmingham instead — a two-point margin between a playoff berth and a non-playoff bowl.

SEGMENT 07

Birmingham Bowl Preview: Southern vs. SMU

Birmingham Bowl
Southern (8-5) vs. SMU (7-5)

This is the first meeting between these two programs in this chronicle's four tracked seasons — no head-to-head history to lean on. But both programs have their own bowl track records, and pulling them changes the shape of this preview.

SMU comes in 7-5, a middling season by American Conference standards — the same league Southern just finished second in at 7-2. There's no ranked win on SMU's résumé this season, and no equivalent of Molinich anywhere on their roster. But they're not the pushover the record alone suggests: SMU signed the No. 19 recruiting class in the country this cycle, and they've been to two bowls in this chronicle's tracked history, going 1-1 — a 2025 ReliaQuest Bowl loss to Iowa (28-31), then a 2027 GameAbove Sports Bowl win over Western Kentucky (41-14). Their most recent bowl trip was a win, and by a good margin.

Southern's own bowl history is thinner and, so far, winless: the program's only other tracked bowl appearance was a 2026 Gasparilla Bowl loss to Tennessee, 31-44 — a game in which starting quarterback Joshua Clark was hurt in the first quarter and a backup had to finish it. That's a real, if dated, data point against a "just trust the resume" pick, and it means Birmingham would be Southern's first tracked bowl win if they get it.

Southern's case is still real: the best individual player in this game by a wide margin, a defense that ranked top-three in the country in scoring defense even before accounting for who's sitting out, and a program that just proved it belongs in a conference title game. The case against is just as real — a passing offense that ranked 131st nationally, a third-down offense that ranked 135th, an 0-1 bowl history of its own, and now missing its top tackler and two starting defensive backs to draft prep. If Molinich has one more monster game left in him, this is a mismatch. If Southern needs Foxworth or Moody to win it through the air, that's the same problem that lost them the conference title, and SMU is a program that's shown it knows how to win exactly this kind of game recently.

Prediction
Southern Over SMU

Medium Confidence — Southern
No head-to-head history to lean on, and SMU's own bowl history (1-1, a recent win) and top-20 class are more credible than the plain 7-5 record suggests. This still comes down to the best player on the field — Molinich against a defense that hasn't faced anyone like him all season is enough to trust, even without Thatcher, Flowers, or Jones on the other side of the ball, and even with Southern chasing its first tracked bowl win.

SEGMENT 08

Bold Predictions

Molinich closes his Southern career (this season, at least) with a 150-yard bowl game against a defense with no answer for him, sending him into the offseason on a high note after an award-winning year that never got its playoff moment.

Jaelen McCracken keeps climbing. A true freshman tying for the conference sack lead is a headline in itself; two more seasons of this and he's a name on national watch lists, not just conference ones.

The passing game is the storyline that follows this program into 2029. A defense this good and a back this dominant should be enough to win a conference title outright, not finish as the runner-up. Until Foxworth, Moody, or whoever starts next fall solves the third-down offense, that ceiling stays exactly where it is.
