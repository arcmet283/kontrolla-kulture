No. 75: The Class Rank Falls, The Transfer Portal Doesn't

Class RankNo. 75 (from No. 36)
4-Stars Signed3
Transfer Rank No. 5J'Vari Flowers
Headline StoryJ'Vari Flowers Comes Home

The high school class actually steps back this cycle — No. 75 nationally, a real drop from 2027's No. 36 and the first year-over-year decline this chronicle has tracked. What keeps this from reading as a down cycle is the transfer portal: the two best transfer additions this program has ever landed, headlined by a player Southern lost as a recruit years ago and just won back anyway. This is the full update.

SEGMENT 03

The Transfer Haul: Flowers & Jones

National Transfer Rank No. 5
J'Vari Flowers, CB · from Georgia

The headline of this entire cycle — the highest-ranked transfer this program has ever landed. See Segment 4 for the full story.

From LSU
Ricardo Jones, FS

A second high-value transfer addition in the same cycle. Already listed on the 2028 preseason All-Conference second team before playing a single game.

Two Power Conference transfer additions in one class is new territory for this program — neither has a star rating tracked in this chronicle's data (Flowers' only confirmed number is his transfer rank, No. 5 nationally; Jones has none tracked at all), but the level of program they're both coming from is its own signal. Southern's only confirmed five-star signee remains Carlos Allen, the 2027 HS class's headliner.

SEGMENT 04

J'Vari Flowers Comes Home

Rewind to his high school recruitment. Southern wanted J'Vari Flowers badly and lost him — he signed with Georgia instead. Years later, via the transfer portal, he's a Jaguar anyway.

He arrives as the No. 5 overall-ranked transfer in the entire country — the highest-rated player this program has landed by any method, signing or portal, even without a star rating attached. It's the exact recruit this program lost years ago, choosing Southern this time when he didn't have to.

SEGMENT 05

The Louisiana Pipeline: Still Bleeding

Even in the program's best recruiting cycle yet, the in-state pipeline problem hasn't gone away. The 2028 Stolen Recruits log sits at twelve names — actually the smallest single-cycle miss total this chronicle has tracked, down from 23 in the 2026 cycle and 19 in 2027 — but Louisiana talent is still walking out the door to blue bloods and in-state rivals alike:

Alabama 4 Misses

Amari Erickson (DE), Darian Weathersby (DE), Jonathan Bowen (T), Chevin Jackson (G) — all Louisiana natives.

LSU 2 Misses

Cole Houston (DT) and Jon Harper (T), both in-state.

Texas Tech 2 Misses

Austin Overshown (ATH) and GG Shorter (ATH), both Louisiana natives.

Miami 1 Miss

K.D. Wilcox (HB), Louisiana.

Louisiana Tech 1 Miss

Fadil Wilcher (DT), in-state.

The class rank jump this cycle came almost entirely from the transfer portal and a handful of out-of-state high school signees, not from winning the in-state recruiting battles this program has lost every single cycle tracked so far.

SEGMENT 07

The Takeaway

The signing-class number is a step back — No. 75, down from last cycle's No. 36 — and that's worth saying plainly rather than burying under the transfer portal headlines. But the portal haul is real: the two best transfer additions this program has tracked, headlined by a player it already lost once and finally won back. The in-state misses haven't stopped, and won't be solved by one good transfer class. Whether 2028 reads as a down cycle or a different kind of good one depends entirely on whether Flowers and Jones perform like their profiles suggest they should.
