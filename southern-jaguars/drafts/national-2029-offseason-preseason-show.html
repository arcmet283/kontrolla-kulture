The 2028 season ended with Florida State's improbable title run and Notre Dame missing the field entirely. The offseason that followed flipped the carousel from its quietest cycle on record into its most volatile: ten coaches on notice heading into 2029, two of them at programs (Alabama, LSU) that have never appeared on this board together before. This is the full offseason-and-preseason show — the hot seat board, the new recruiting class rankings, the preseason poll, and the Heisman watchlist reopening with a familiar face and four new ones.

SEGMENT 01

The Hot Seat Board: Ten Coaches, Two Blue Bloods

The 2027-to-2028 coaching cycle produced zero hires or firings among the programs this chronicle tracks — a first. The calm didn't carry into 2029. Ten coaches are on notice heading into the season, the largest hot-seat class tracked yet, up from three hires in 2026's cycle and two in 2027's.

Alabama Hot Seat

Kalen DeBoer. A No. 1 recruiting class in 2028 hasn't bought him security — a 7–6 finish with a bowl loss to Memphis put him on this board. This program did make the 2026 CFP as a 12-seed and reached the semifinals before losing to Georgia, 37-35, so the ceiling is real even if the seat is hot.

LSU Hot Seat

Brian Kelly. The other half of this board's first-ever pairing of two programs at this level of national standing on notice at once, and unlike Alabama, no CFP appearance shows up anywhere in this program's tracked record to point to.

Auburn Hot Seat

Hugh Freeze, a repeat name from this board in prior cycles. Auburn actually made the 2027 CFP (lost Round 1 to Ole Miss, 41-7) and won the 2028 Gasparilla Bowl outright (42-14 over Bowling Green) — real, recent success that hasn't bought him any preseason benefit of the doubt.

Arkansas Hot Seat

Jake Spavital, hired in the very first tracked coaching cycle back in 2025, still hunting for a winning season (2-10, 4-8, 3-9 in his first three years).

Iowa Hot Seat

Kirk Ferentz, the most tenured name on this entire board by reputation. No bowl, CFP, or award appearance for this specific program (not to be confused with Iowa State, a completely different and much more successful tracked program) shows up anywhere in this chronicle's record.

Michigan St. Hot Seat

Jonathan Smith. This program was a 2026 Big Ten runner-up (a 26-24 OT loss to Michigan) and a 2026 CFP participant (lost Round 1 to Alabama, 35-21) — a real recent ceiling for a coach still finding his footing.

Missouri Hot Seat

Eliah Drinkwitz. No bowl, CFP, or conference title tracked for this program in this chronicle's record — genuinely the thinnest résumé on this board (not to be confused with Missouri State, an entirely different program that won a national award and made the 2028 CFP).

TCU Hot Seat

Sonny Dykes. TCU produced the 2028 national passing yards leader (Tucker Parks, 4,116 yards), but no team-level bowl or CFP appearance shows up in the tracked record.

Vanderbilt Hot Seat

Clark Lea. Vanderbilt won the 2027 StaffDNA Cure Bowl outright, 38-28 over Troy — a real recent bright spot for a coach who's now also fielding the No. 22 recruiting class nationally this cycle.

Virginia Tech Hot Seat

Brent Pry. The program's only tracked postseason appearance is a 2025 StaffDNA Cure Bowl loss to Cincinnati, 36-22 — thin enough history that this hot-seat placement reads as a program still looking for its first real signature result.

This board is ranked, not rated — and the gap between the two tells its own story. Michigan sits at No. 2 in the poll but carries this preseason's single highest team rating (97), well clear of preseason No. 1 Texas (91). Alabama's team rating (95) would be the second-best in the country, but the Crimson Tide don't even crack this top 10, landing at No. 17 instead — the exact kind of ratings-versus-ranking gap this desk flags every year. And Florida State, the reigning national champion, opens defending its title from outside the polled field entirely: an 80 overall rating, the lowest of any team this preview tracked a number for.

SEGMENT 04

Florida State's Title Defense, By The Numbers

No program in this chronicle has successfully defended a national title, and Florida State opens its defense in the toughest starting position of any of them: an 80 team rating that doesn't crack the preseason polled board at all, a full 17 points below preseason No. 1 Michigan on paper. The two prior champions had rougher title defenses than a ratings dip, too — Notre Dame (2025 champion) was knocked out in Round 2 the very next year, and Texas (2026 champion) missed the CFP field entirely in 2027 before fighting back to a bye seed in 2028. Only Ole Miss's 2027-to-2028 defense was a soft landing, opening the next season at preseason No. 2. Florida State's drop-off, at least on paper, looks like the steepest of any of them before a single 2029 snap is played.

Five names on the board at this earliest preseason checkpoint, and the makeup is unusual — three wide receivers, a quarterback, and Jordan Molinich as the only running back, all logged at the same one-week starting point. Molinich's presence is itself a small milestone: after finally winning the Maxwell and Doak Walker last season, this is the first time he's opened a season already on this list. Reigning Heisman winner Ken Ward (Texas, now NFL) isn't walking through that door again, but Texas still has a name on this board in receiver Jaime Ffrench — the program trying to prove last year's Heisman wasn't a one-quarterback show. Oklahoma State's David Dehond is worth watching for his own program's sake, too — this is the same Oklahoma State that's climbed from a 4-8 coaching debut in 2025 to 7-5 to a 10-3 finish and a 2027 Guaranteed Rate Bowl win, then a 2028 CFP appearance, the single best coaching turnaround this chronicle has tracked. A Heisman-caliber season from Dehond would be the individual capstone on a program trending up every year.

SEGMENT 06

The Takeaway

This offseason turned the carousel's quietest cycle into its loudest hot-seat class yet, with Alabama and LSU sharing a board together for the first time. The recruiting rankings and the preseason ratings are telling two different stories about who's actually good — Michigan's the best team on paper without being the preseason No. 1, Alabama's roster rating outstrips its poll position by a mile, and the reigning national champion opens the year rated below every other team this preview tracked a number for. If there's a throughline from 2028 into 2029, it's that name recognition and on-paper readiness increasingly don't match — which is exactly the kind of season that produced two straight non-top-four champions already.
