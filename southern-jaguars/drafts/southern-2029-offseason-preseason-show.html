The 2028 season ended with the best defense this program has ever fielded and two losses that cost it a championship. This show covers everything that's happened since: who's gone, who stayed, who's arriving, and what the 2029 slate actually looks like. It's a smaller, quieter offseason than the one before it — no 5-star transfer headline this time — but the roster losing three players to the NFL Draft in the same cycle, and a recruiting class that fell hard from last year's high, make it one worth covering in full before Week 1.

SEGMENT 01

The Exodus: Who's Actually Gone

Eleven players are actually off the 2029 roster (three more tested the portal and came back — more on that in Segment 2). The headline is three players gone to the NFL Draft in the same cycle: Gavin Hoffman (WR, the program's best-ever receiving season in 2027), Ricardo Jones (FS, a 5-star transfer who was a preseason All-Conference honoree before he ever played a snap), and J’Vari Flowers (CB, the No. 5 national transfer add and the headline of last year's record-setting recruiting class). Flowers in particular is a one-and-done story worth sitting with — the player Southern lost as a high schooler, chased through three more stops, and finally landed via the portal, only for him to have exactly one season on campus (36 tackles, 1 interception, 3 forced fumbles, 2nd Team All-Conference) before turning pro.

SEGMENT 02

The Ones Who Stayed

Three players tested the transfer portal and chose to come back — the same pattern this program has shown every cycle tracked so far. Carlos Allen (HB, the program's first 5-star signee), Joe Eisworth (TE, now the longest-tenured tracked player on this roster), and Chancellor Powell (G) all re-signed with Southern rather than leave. Allen's return matters most on paper: a 4-star-caliber talent (81→84→87 OVR through three seasons) who's still searching for a featured role behind Molinich, now walking into the backfield with real vacancy after Hoffman's departure thins the passing game.

SEGMENT 03

The 2029 Signing Class: A Real Step Back

After last year's record-setting No. 36 class — two 5-star transfers, three 4-star high school signees — the class signed for 2029 fell all the way to No. 75 nationally, a 39-spot drop in one cycle. There's no 5-star and no 4-star anywhere in this class; the best-rated signees are three 3-stars (Trey Case, Ron Diliello, Eric Coney). And the early returns are already rough: of eighteen high school signees, seven were cut before ever recording a single tracked overall rating — Justin Harris, Julius Tei, Kevin Johnson, Jaren Tunufi, Amir Vakauta, Dan Taylor, and Ben Withrow, all gone before playing a down. That's close to 40% of the class cut on arrival, the roughest immediate attrition rate this chronicle has tracked for a single cycle.

SEGMENT 04

The Transfer Portal Haul, And A Player Who Came Back

Eight true outside transfer additions this cycle, none rated higher than 3-star: Ben Bass (MLB, Florida Atlantic), Anthony Butler (DT, Northwestern), Innis Jordan (HB, Kansas St.), Cedric Allen (CB, Nebraska), Marvin Cook (CB, Minnesota), Sajid Alexander (MLB, Uconn), Robert King (DT, Nebraska), and the one with the actual story attached — Darian Weathersby (DE, from Alabama). Weathersby was a Louisiana native Southern lost as a high school recruit to Alabama just one cycle ago; he's now transferring in after a redshirt season there. It's the same shape as last year's J’Vari Flowers story, on a much smaller scale — a player this program lost, coming back through the portal instead of the other way around.

SEGMENT 06

The QB Question: Cameron Seals Wins The Job

The single biggest storyline of the offseason has nothing to do with the transfer portal or the recruiting board: Cameron Seals is the confirmed Week 1 starting quarterback, taking the job from Joseph Foxworth — the same player who ran the offense last season on a team that finished 8–6, made a second straight AAC Championship Game trip, and got there behind a defense that finished No. 1 in the country in total yards allowed. Seals signed with Southern in the 2028 class as a true afterthought even by this program's own standards: a 2-star quarterback out of Alaska, ranked 166th nationally at his position, with no ranked pedigree to speak of. He redshirted behind Foxworth as a true freshman in 2028, rated a modest 63 overall; that number has already climbed to 66 heading into what's technically still his redshirt freshman season. The separator in fall camp was the complete package — by the staff's own evaluation, Seals brings real athleticism and a strong arm, and while his accuracy trails Foxworth's, it's a clear step ahead of Moody's, and that combination was apparently enough to win the job outright.

It's a strange kind of program history repeating itself — Foxworth himself was a buried 2-star afterthought (unranked nationally, outside the position's top 200) who only got his own opening because of someone else's misfortune: Ty Wilson, the starter at the time, went down with a torn shoulder muscle in Week 3 of 2027, Moody took over and struggled, and Foxworth didn't take the job for good until Moody was pulled mid-game against ECU in Week 6. Two years later, an even lower-pedigree 2-star has taken the job from Foxworth the clean way — won it outright in a fair fall-camp competition, with no injury or benching required to create the opening. What's confirmed is the outcome, and it's the headline going into Week 1 at North Texas.

Nothing about this is being treated as settled beyond the opener, either. Both Foxworth and James Moody — QB2 and QB3 on paper now, but each with real starting experience of his own — remain in the room, and any real stumble from Seals puts both of them right back in the competition. This roster has now made a QB change mid-competition, mid-season, and mid-game at various points across three straight years; there's no reason to assume 2029 is any different if Seals doesn't hold up.

The overall ratings don't line up with the depth chart at all — Moody actually carries the highest number of the three (74) despite sitting third on the chart, with Seals (66) winning the job over both a higher-rated backup and the incumbent starter, Foxworth (62), whose rating has climbed the slowest of the group across three tracked seasons. Whatever separated Seals in camp, it wasn't the spreadsheet.

Each arm in this room comes with a different trade-off. Foxworth isn't an athlete at this position and doesn't have a strong arm, but he's the most accurate passer of the three and has a live case that his ball placement is what took this offense to the AAC title game last season. Moody is the opposite bet entirely — some real athleticism and the strongest arm in the room, but the least reliable accuracy of the group, the exact profile of a change-of-pace option a staff turns to mid-game when the starter isn't getting it done. Seals sits in between the other two on accuracy — behind Foxworth, but ahead of Moody — while adding the athleticism and arm strength neither of them fully offers on its own. He's not the best at any single trait in this room; he's the only one without an obvious hole, which is the actual case for starting him over two players who've each already played meaningful snaps and he hasn't.

SEGMENT 09

AAC Preseason Predictions: Picked Behind Both Teams That Beat Them

1. UAB Beat Southern In 2028

Prestige 2. The team that snapped Southern's unbeaten streak against them last season is the preseason favorite — and now starts Montray Wilson, a Michigan transfer QB rated No. 7 nationally (90 OVR projection), to back it up.

2. Charlotte Beat Southern In The AAC Title Game

Prestige 2. Picked ahead of Southern after splitting the series 1–1 — Southern's 2026 road win canceled out by last year's title-game loss.

3. Southern Preseason Pick

Prestige 2. The reigning conference runner-up, picked third behind the two teams that ended its season.

Both programs picked ahead of Southern are the exact two teams that ended its 2028 season — UAB in the regular season, Charlotte in the title game. The preseason media clearly isn't grading last year's near-miss as progress, and UAB's case got real backing this offseason: it landed Montray Wilson, a quarterback transferring over from Michigan, ranked the No. 7 overall transfer add in the entire country with a projected 90 overall rating — a number that would clear every quarterback in Southern's own room by a wide margin. Both rematches (UAB in Week 11, Charlotte in Week 4) arrive with a real chance to answer the preseason pick directly, but Southern will have to do it against a UAB quarterback with a considerably higher ceiling than anyone this show has covered so far.

SEGMENT 10

The Takeaway

This offseason looked quiet on paper — no marquee transfer addition, a recruiting class that fell nearly 40 spots, three NFL departures to replace — right up until a redshirt freshman nobody was talking about won the starting quarterback job away from the returning starter who'd just taken this team to an 8–6 finish and a second straight AAC title game. What Southern does have beyond the QB uncertainty is the best player in program history entering his senior season already on the Heisman watchlist and a preseason 1st Team All-American, a defense that just finished No. 1 in the country returning most of its identity, and a schedule that opens with three straight true road games against brand-new or historically difficult opponents. The preseason media has Southern third in its own conference, behind the two teams that ended its season last year, and now Cameron Seals gets to answer that question with a first career start at North Texas.
