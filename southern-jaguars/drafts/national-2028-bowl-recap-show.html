The season is over. All twenty-six standalone bowls are played, the full playoff bracket is complete, and Florida State is the national champion — a No. 5 seed that entered this postseason with zero tracked playoff history of any kind and left it 15-2, having gone through Charlotte, Missouri State, Penn State, and UCLA to get there. Tonight's the full autopsy: every bowl, the entire bracket, and a final scorecard on everything we called before a single game was played.

SEGMENT 05

Florida State's Run, Start To Finish

National Championship
Florida St. 38, UCLA 31

Sam Williams closes it out with a wild final line: 24-of-37 for 318 yards and 3 touchdowns through the air, but also 3 interceptions, plus 12 carries for 109 yards and a rushing score. Nico Iamaleava answered for UCLA with 299 yards and 2 touchdowns through the air and a rushing score of his own, and it still wasn't enough.

Trace it from the start: Florida State entered this bracket as the No. 5 seed with zero tracked playoff history of any kind, the deepest bowl résumé in the field (3-0 across three prior tracked seasons) but no proof it could win on the sport's biggest stage. It beat Charlotte in Round 1, blew out Missouri State 63-28 in Round 2, beat Penn State by 9 in the Fiesta Bowl semifinal, and beat UCLA by 7 in the title game. Four games, four wins, and the widest margin of the four came against the team we spent all preseason calling the round's best-argued underdog case.

Sam Williams is the story underneath the story. He wasn't in the Heisman conversation at any point we tracked this season. He finishes the year as a national champion with a title-game performance that included three interceptions and still walked away with the trophy, because his legs and his receivers bailed him out often enough that it didn't matter. That's as complete a redemption arc as this bracket produced — not a perfect quarterback, a winning one.
