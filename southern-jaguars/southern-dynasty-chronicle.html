The headline number that doesn't show up anywhere else on this table: Southern has now produced the nation's leading rusher in back-to-back seasons, with two completely different supporting casts around him both times. Eight games have produced three distinct chapters. The opening month (Weeks 1–4) was the disaster this report series already covered in depth: a good win at Stanford undone by three straight losses as the quarterback room fell apart. And Week 9 was simply an outlier in program history: a 97–10 demolition of Tennessee St., breaking down below.

A second true freshman, now the passing leader outright. Started slow, then produced back-to-back 300-plus-yard efforts against Army and Tulsa before an efficient, mistake-free 224-yard, 2-TD outing against Tennessee St.

Two different true freshmen have now started games for this program in the same season — a scenario this report series flagged as a real risk back in the preview, when it noted "no third scholarship quarterback with meaningful experience" existed behind Ty Wilson. That risk fully materialized. What's remarkable is that Foxworth has actually trended upward across his four starts rather than getting worse under pressure, and he's now thrown for 333, then 224 yards in his last two outings with a positive TD-to-INT ratio in the more recent one.

SEGMENT 04

Molinich's Redemption, In Full

National Rushing Leader · Again
Jordan Molinich HB · SO
145 car, 1,087 yds, 8 TD (1st in the nation, 1st in conference)

Four weeks ago, this report series documented Molinich's roughest stretch as a college player: 10 total rushing yards against North Texas, 61 against Texas Tech, two fumbles in four games, 6th in his own conference. The turnaround since has been almost as dramatic as the slump: 194 yards against Army, 165 against Tulsa, and then 218 yards on just seven carries against Tennessee St. — a 31.1-yards-per-carry average that included six touchdowns and earned him NCAA National Offensive Player of the Week. He's back to leading the country in rushing outright, the exact position he held for most of his historic 2026 season. Whatever the offensive line issues were in September, they've either been fixed or Molinich has simply willed his way through them.

SEGMENT 05

The Tennessee State Explosion

Jordan Molinich: 7 carries, 218 yards, 6 touchdowns. Final score: 97–10.

This is the most lopsided result in this dynasty's tracked history, on either side of the ball. Beyond Molinich's outlier stat line, the box score reads like a full roster showcase: Joseph Foxworth went 11-of-22 for 224 yards and 2 touchdowns without an interception, his cleanest outing yet. Carlos Allen, the program's first-ever five-star signee, got real carries and produced — 12 rushes for 108 yards, a strong sign for the eventual 1B to Molinich's 1A. Matt Brown, one of the two players who nearly transferred away last offseason before returning, scored three rushing touchdowns. And Stacy Vaughn, the senior receiver who missed the season's first seven weeks with a preseason quad injury, caught two touchdowns in only his second game back. A 97-point output is an outlier that shouldn't be extrapolated into a trend on its own, but it's also proof of exactly how much depth and talent this roster can put on the field when health and matchup both break the right way at once.

One number has been a constant across all three seasons and remains this program's single deepest unsolved problem: run defense, ranked 135th, 132nd, and 135th nationally in three straight years, regardless of coaching emphasis, transfer investment, or overall team quality. Everything else has moved — the offense recovered from its early-season stumble (41st through nine games, after bottoming out near 125th in the immediate aftermath of the QB chaos), and the turnover margin, deeply negative all of 2026, has swung back to positive. But the run defense is the true through-line of this dynasty, the one number that three different rosters, two different recruiting classes, and one full transfer-portal overhaul haven't been able to move.

SEGMENT 10

Molinich And The Heisman: A Season-Long Watch

Molinich has now logged 4 straight weeks on the Heisman watchlist — unchanged from where this tracking stood before his 218-yard, 6-touchdown explosion against Tennessee St., which suggests the watchlist entry hadn't yet caught up to his week 9 performance at the time of this data snapshot. What's most notable is that his name never came off the list during the slump, the same pattern this report series flagged as a real departure from his own 2026 experience, when a historic season produced zero Heisman weeks at all. Whether reclaiming the national rushing lead outright pushes him back up a crowded board — Richard Young (Alabama) and Fluff Bothwell (Mississippi St.) both now have 6 weeks logged, ahead of Molinich's 4 — is worth watching in the next data cycle.

SEGMENT 11

The Recruiting Trail: Three Cycles Deep

The 2028 board has its first name: Dee Stephens, a two-star wide receiver from Louisiana — the same in-state pipeline that's defined every recruiting cycle this program has run since the dynasty began. It's early, the same shape every prior class had at this exact point in its cycle. More concerning: three more Louisiana targets have already been lost to Alabama for the 2028 class (Amari Erickson, Darian Weathersby, Jonathan Bowen), continuing a pattern of blue-blood programs raiding Southern's home-state recruiting footprint that's persisted across all three tracked cycles.

SEGMENT 12

The Takeaway: Three Years In

Read the three-year ledger in Segment 1 again and the shape of this dynasty is unmistakable: a program that has gotten dramatically better at the things that are hardest to build (skill-position talent, a national-caliber running back two years running, real prestige movement) while staying stubbornly unable to fix the thing that's easiest to point to on a stat sheet (run defense, unmoved in three seasons). 2027 has been the most chaotic season yet by a wide margin — three different quarterbacks, a 47-point loss, a 97-point win, a season-altering injury to the presumed starter — and Southern is still only 3–5 with four games to determine whether any of it adds up to a bowl bid.

But zoom out past the win-loss record and the story is bigger than any single season's record can capture: this is a program that took a two-star afterthought and turned him into the national rushing champion two years running, developed a five-star signee's first meaningful carries in real time this season, and is now trusting true freshmen at the sport's most important position because the far side of that gamble — guys like Foxworth trending upward across four starts — is starting to look like it might actually pay off. Three years ago this was the 136th-ranked team in the country. Whatever 2027 ends up being, that's no longer a fair way to describe this program.
