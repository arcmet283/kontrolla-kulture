The headline number that doesn't show up anywhere else on this table: Southern has now produced the nation's leading rusher in back-to-back seasons, with two completely different supporting casts around him both times. That's not a fluke of scheme or a single great recruiting class — it's a program that has figured out how to build a run game good enough to carry a rebuild, even while everything else around it (the quarterback position most of all) stays in flux.

One number has been a constant across all three seasons and remains this program's single deepest unsolved problem: run defense, ranked 135th, 132nd, and 135th nationally in three straight years, regardless of coaching emphasis, transfer investment, or overall team quality. Everything else has moved — the offense recovered from its early-season stumble (41st through nine games, after bottoming out near 125th in the immediate aftermath of the QB chaos), and the turnover margin, deeply negative all of 2026, has swung back to positive. But the run defense is the true through-line of this dynasty, the one number that three different rosters, two different recruiting classes, and one full transfer-portal overhaul haven't been able to move.

North Texas (the Week 2 opponent that beat Southern 10–30) and Tulsa (the Week 8 opponent that beat Southern 26–40) are both firmly in the conference's upper tier, a real signal that those weren't bad losses so much as losses to good teams. Southern sits in a three-way tie for 11th with Army — whose center Brady Small swept the Outland and Rimington Trophies in 2025 — and Florida Atlantic, a 2026 Armed Forces Bowl winner, ahead of only Charlotte, the 2026 GameAbove Sports Bowl champion. Memphis, still ahead of Southern in the standings, is the reigning 2025 Famous Idaho Potato Bowl winner, and Navy carries real pedigree of its own as the 2025 American Conference champion and a CFP participant that same year. The four remaining games — Temple, UAB, Memphis, UTSA — are all against teams within a game or two of Southern in the standings, meaning bowl eligibility (6 wins) is a real stretch from 3–5 but not an impossible one if the Foxworth-Molinich combination holds.

SEGMENT 06

Molinich And The Heisman: A Season-Long Watch

Molinich has now logged 4 straight weeks on the Heisman watchlist — unchanged from where this tracking stood before his 218-yard, 6-touchdown explosion against Tennessee St., which suggests the watchlist entry hadn't yet caught up to his week 9 performance at the time of this data snapshot. What's most notable is that his name never came off the list during the slump, the same pattern this report series flagged as a real departure from his own 2026 experience, when a historic season produced zero Heisman weeks at all. Whether reclaiming the national rushing lead outright pushes him back up a crowded board — Richard Young (Alabama) and Fluff Bothwell (Mississippi St.) both now have 6 weeks logged, ahead of Molinich's 4 — is worth watching in the next data cycle.

SEGMENT 07

The Recruiting Trail: Three Cycles Deep

The 2028 board has its first name: Dee Stephens, a two-star wide receiver from Louisiana — the same in-state pipeline that's defined every recruiting cycle this program has run since the dynasty began. It's early, the same shape every prior class had at this exact point in its cycle. More concerning: three more Louisiana targets have already been lost to Alabama for the 2028 class (Amari Erickson, Darian Weathersby, Jonathan Bowen), continuing a pattern of blue-blood programs raiding Southern's home-state recruiting footprint that's shown up in each of the last two cycles.

SEGMENT 08

The Takeaway: Three Years In

Read the three-year ledger in Segment 1 again and the shape of this dynasty is unmistakable: a program that has gotten dramatically better at the things that are hardest to build (skill-position talent, a running back producing at a national-caliber level again in 2027, real prestige movement) while staying stubbornly unable to fix the thing that's easiest to point to on a stat sheet (run defense, unmoved in three seasons). 2027 has been the most chaotic season yet by a wide margin — three different quarterbacks, a 38-point loss, a 97-point win, a season-altering injury to the presumed starter — and Southern is still only 3–5 with four games to determine whether any of it adds up to a bowl bid.

But zoom out past the win-loss record and the story is bigger than any single season's record can capture: this is a program that took a two-star afterthought and turned him into a national rushing champion once already, with a real case to do it again, developed a five-star signee's first meaningful carries in real time this season, and is now trusting true freshmen at the sport's most important position because the far side of that gamble — guys like Foxworth trending upward across his first three starts — is starting to look like it might actually pay off. Three years ago this was the 136th-ranked team in the country. Whatever 2027 ends up being, that's no longer a fair way to describe this program.
