Southern Jaguars Football · The Complete 2026 Season

From No. 90 To A National Record

Final Record8–6
Conference7–3
Rank90 → 44
Prestige2
National AwardDoak Walker (Molinich)

A year ago, this program finished 5–7 with no bowl bid and a No. 84 ranking that felt, at the time, like real progress from a No. 136 start. This year's ledger reads almost nothing alike: 8–6, a conference championship appearance, a first-ever bowl bid, a final ranking of No. 44, and a true freshman running back who didn't just lead the country in rushing — he broke the national record outright and won the Doak Walker Award for it. And yet the season's shape was anything but a straight line up. Southern peaked at CFP No. 16 in November, lost three of its final five games, fell out of both national polls entirely, and closed with back-to-back losses in the AAC Championship and the Gasparilla Bowl. This is the full, unabridged account of how both of those stories are true at once — the deepest look this report series has ever produced, covering every game, every departure, every incoming signee, and the complete national picture the season closed against.

Three acts, cleanly divided. The first six games (1–7, skipping the bye) established the identity: one bad loss to open the year, then five straight wins, including a 41-point revenge blowout of North Texas after they'd opened 2025 with a win over Southern. The middle stretch (8–9) was the high point — an 81-point turnaround win over Army that flipped last year's worst loss into a statement, then a wild 49–42 shootout over Florida A&M. The final six games (10–bowl) were where the season actually got decided, and not in Southern's favor: one shutout loss to a genuinely elite Ole Miss team, a stunning 35-point reversal against Memphis (the same team Southern demolished by 56 a year earlier), a triple-overtime heartbreak to UTSA, a seven-point AAC title game loss to Tulane, and a 13-point bowl loss to Tennessee where starting quarterback Joshua Clark went down injured in the first quarter. Southern was 8–0 against teams outside that final stretch and 0–6 against everyone in it — the single clearest data point in the whole season about where this roster's ceiling actually sits.

SEGMENT 03

The Molinich Chronicle

National Rushing Record Holder · Doak Walker Award · 1st Team All-American
Jordan Molinich HB · FR
309 car, 2,349 yds, 24 TD (full season, including bowl)

No player in this dynasty's history has had a season like this one, and the progression is worth tracing in full, because it shows a player who never had a quiet week: 951 yards through 6 games at the first Week 8 check-in, already the national rushing leader. 1,400 yards through 9 games at Week 11, still No. 1, tied for the national TD lead. 2,127 yards and the outright national lead in both categories by conference championship week, having broken Maurice Turner's 2025 record (1,767 yards) by 360 yards with a month still to play. 2,200 yards and the Doak Walker Award by the start of bowl season. And finally, after a big bowl performance against Tennessee, 2,349 yards and 24 touchdowns to close the year — a true freshman, a four-star signee ranked No. 28 among HBs nationally in a class that finished 59th overall, finishing as the best running back in college football by a margin no one else on this roster came within a mile of matching. He also collected 1st Team All-American, Freshman All-American, and 1st Team All-Conference honors, plus three separate Offensive Player of the Week awards (Weeks 4, 5, and 12) — the same accolade pattern Mark Fiaseu set in 2025, except Fiaseu never led the nation outright in anything.

Read this list carefully and it's the entire spine of the 2025–2026 rebuild leaving at once: the starting quarterback, the tackling leader for two straight years, the sack leader from 2025, and the interception leader from 2026 are all gone, along with three of the eight defensive transfers this program brought in specifically to fix its run defense. SEGMENT 08

The Ones Who Almost Left

Two names on the departure sheet have an asterisk. Matt Brown (HB/WR) and Joe Eisworth (TE) both entered the transfer portal and both ended up back at Southern for 2027 — Brown after a 2026 season with 19 carries for 98 yards and 2 touchdowns plus 4 catches, Eisworth after a breakout 7-catch, 4-touchdown campaign that came from nowhere on this roster's radar. Whatever the portal process looked like from the inside, the program held onto both of its most interesting depth-chart wildcards. That's a quieter win than anything in the recruiting class below, but a real one.

SEGMENT 09

The Ones That Got Away

The stolen-recruits board grew considerably this cycle — a longer list of near-misses than either prior year, though notably fewer of them went to blue-blood programs this time. Southern's own three confirmed 2026 departures (Devin Sanchez to Texas Tech, Benedict Umeh to Stanford, Siale Siverand to Oklahoma St.) all appear here too, tagged plainly as "Left Southern." Among the recruits Southern pursued but didn't land, the notable misses landed at Louisiana Tech, UL Monroe, Florida Atlantic, New Mexico St., Kansas St., Washington St., and Nebraska — a spread of mid-major and Group of Five programs rather than the Power Four names that dominated the 2026 miss list. That's a subtle but real shift: Southern is increasingly losing recruiting battles to programs at or near its own level, not just getting outbid by blue bloods.

SEGMENT 16

The Takeaway

Strip away the ranking swings and the record and 2026 tells one consistent story: this program's ceiling rose dramatically, carried almost entirely by a single player nobody outside the building projected. Jordan Molinich didn't just have the best season in this dynasty's history — he had one of the best seasons any player produced anywhere in the country this year, a national record, a Doak Walker Award, and a spot on next year's Heisman watchlist before he's played a single 2027 down. That's the story that will define this era of Southern football regardless of what else happened.

But the season's floor didn't rise nearly as much as its ceiling did. The turnover margin cratered. The run defense stayed broken despite a full offseason built to fix it. And when the games mattered most — Ole Miss, Memphis, UTSA, Tulane, Tennessee — Southern went 0–5, exactly the pattern this report series flagged as far back as the Week 5 check-in. Fourteen players are leaving, including the starting quarterback and both leaders on defense. What replaces them — a five-star running back, a retooled offensive line, a defense still searching for its 2025 form — is the entire question of 2027, and it starts with a roster that's simultaneously more talented at the top and thinner everywhere else than the one that just finished 8–6.
