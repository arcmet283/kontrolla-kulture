This is the first national report with two full completed seasons of history behind it, and that changes what's worth writing about. A hot start or a bad loss doesn't mean much in isolation five weeks into a season — it means something when it's measured against what a program actually was a year ago, or two years ago, or against the exact team that beat them for a championship. Notre Dame won it all in 2025 on the strength of Jeremiyah Love. Texas won it all in 2026 on the strength of Arch Manning. Both players are in the NFL now, and both programs are already being tested by life without them. This report reads Week 4/5 of 2027 through that lens: every ranking, every coaching story, every Heisman name, measured against the two years of data already on the books.

SEGMENT 01

Texas After Manning: The Champion's Hangover

A year ago, Texas closed the most dominant championship run this report series has tracked: a 54–14 demolition of Georgia in the national title game, a direct 43-point swing on the same fixture that had beaten them in the SEC Championship six weeks earlier. Arch Manning left that season with the Heisman, the Maxwell, the Davey O'Brien, and single-season and career passing records for the program — 4,831 yards, 50 touchdowns, 85 career touchdowns. He's in the NFL now, and Texas has opened this season 3–1, No. 8 in the country. That's not a collapse — it's a normal, human-scale comedown from a historically great quarterback leaving on schedule, and it's worth stating plainly rather than treating as a crisis: winning a national title with a generational player and then taking a step back the year after he leaves is close to the default outcome in this sport, not an exception.

SEGMENT 02

The Auburn Upset, In Context

Week 5
Auburn 53, Texas 47

Hugh Freeze's Auburn team, unbeaten at 4–0, knocked off the reigning national champion in a 100-point shootout. This isn't a new story for Freeze — he's been flagged on this report series' hot-seat tracking for two straight offseasons now (2026 and 2027) and has answered both times with results that make the "hot seat" label look premature. Auburn is now the clearest early-season answer to the question this series has been asking since last year's coaching carousel report: does "on notice" actually predict poor performance? For Freeze specifically, across two full seasons of tracking, the answer keeps coming back no.

SEGMENT 07

The Virginia Cliff

One result buried in the coaching tables above deserves its own callout. Adam Fuller's Virginia was the single best story of the 2025 coaching class — a first-time college head coach straight out of an NFL position room, 7–5 in year one, capped with a Sun Bowl win. Two years later, Virginia is 0–4. That's not a modest step back; it's a complete collapse from the best debut season this report series tracked to the worst record of any program covered in this report, in the span of a single offseason. Whatever went wrong — roster turnover, schedule, injuries — it's the single biggest coaching-carousel story developing right now, bigger than any of the hot-seat names still hanging on.

SEGMENT 08

Two Champions, Two Years, One Pattern

Notre Dame beat Texas Tech 34–31 for the 2025 title on the strength of Jeremiyah Love. Texas beat Georgia 54–14 for the 2026 title on the strength of Arch Manning. Both stars are gone to the NFL. Both champions opened this season ranked in the top 10 regardless — Notre Dame at No. 1, Texas at No. 8 — which says something real about program infrastructure mattering more than any single player, even a Heisman winner. But it's also true that neither program has looked like a runaway favorite through four games: Notre Dame is unbeaten but untested, and Texas has already lost to Auburn. Two years of data now says the same thing twice: winning it all with a transcendent quarterback buys a program exactly one more good season on reputation, not permanent contention.

SEGMENT 09

What To Watch

Six unbeaten teams, a defending champion that's already lost, a Heisman board with no returning winner on it, and a coaching carousel where being on the hot seat increasingly predicts nothing at all — that's the state of the sport four weeks into 2027. The single most interesting thread heading into the next stretch of the season is Nebraska: unbeaten, No. 4, with two players on the Heisman board, and a program that missed the 2026 playoff field by three spots. If they keep this pace, they'll be the clearest example yet of a team that used a near-miss season as fuel rather than a setback.

And for programs still finding their footing — Virginia cratering from a Sun Bowl high, Wake Forest producing a national statistical leader on a 1–4 team, Southern navigating a season-altering quarterback injury four games in — the same lesson from the last two years keeps repeating: nothing in this sport is stable for more than about twelve months at a time. That instability is exactly what makes it worth tracking in this much detail, year over year, rather than one season at a time.
