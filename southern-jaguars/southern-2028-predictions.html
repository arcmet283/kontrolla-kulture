SEGMENT 02

Molinich's Third Season

Molinich's rushing total has declined each season he's been the feature back — 2,349 yards as a true freshman, 1,669 last year. If that trend continues, expect a total closer to 1,300–1,500 rather than another national-leader campaign, especially with a full backfield now including Carlos Allen entering his third season. That's not a decline in quality so much as a shift toward a more balanced offense — and it could actually help his case if Southern is winning more games instead of needing to feed him 25+ carries just to stay competitive.

SEGMENT 03

Foxworth, Year Two As A Starter

Foxworth closed his first full season at 130-of-246 (52.8%) for 2,067 yards, 21 touchdowns, and 10 interceptions. A sophomore leap in completion percentage is the single most common development-curve pattern for a young starting quarterback in this dynasty's own data — both Foxworth's own in-season improvement from his first spot start to his last, and Molinich's own freshman-to-sophomore refinement, point the same direction. Expect the touchdown total to climb and the interception rate to drop, even if the yardage total stays similar.

SEGMENT 04

Does The Run Defense Finally Turn?

Three straight seasons, Southern's run defense has finished ranked 135th, 132nd, and 132nd nationally — the single most stubborn problem this program has tracked, surviving multiple full transfer classes aimed directly at fixing it. This year's additions (Flowers and Jones) are both defensive backs, not the front seven — the same mismatch between where the roster investment goes and where the actual problem lives that's plagued every previous transfer class. Prediction: it doesn't turn. A fourth straight season in the bottom 10 nationally against the run is the most likely outcome unless a name not yet on this roster emerges up front.

SEGMENT 05

Carlos Allen Due

The program's first 5-star HS signee enters year two still unproven in a featured role — this is the season that storyline needs to resolve one way or the other.

SEGMENT 06

The Takeaway

7–5, bowl eligibility clinched well before the season finale, and a defense that still can't stop the run no matter who's added to it — that's the projection. USF is the one clean gap the roster upgrade doesn't obviously close, backed by real history; Texas is a talent-gap loss regardless of history. The four true toss-ups — Kentucky, Louisiana, Memphis, and UTSA — are where this season actually gets decided: break all four right and this is a 9-win team, break all four wrong and 5–7 is the realistic floor.
