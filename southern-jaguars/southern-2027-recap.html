Southern's 2027 season is over, and it ends on a strange note: bowl-eligible for the second straight year, and left home anyway. This is the full recap — the 1–4 start, the true freshman quarterback who rescued it, the final numbers, and the massive senior class walking out the door on the way into an offseason that's already producing the best recruiting news in program history.

SEGMENT 04

Molinich: The Heisman Snub, Part Two

Molinich's Heisman Watchlist count froze at 4 weeks and never moved again after an early-season checkpoint — even while he led the country in rushing from start to finish. He also didn't repeat as Doak Walker winner: that award went to Alabama's Richard Young, while Iowa State's Jaray Rask — the 5-star athlete-turned-quarterback who led the Cyclones to a Big 12 title and the CFP field — won the Heisman outright. Production without a signature national moment wasn't enough for Southern's best player two years running.

SEGMENT 07

No Bowl Bid: What Happened

Southern finished 6–6, the same win total that earned a Gasparilla Bowl trip a year ago — and this year, no bowl at all. Every bowl slot nationally has been filled without Southern's name appearing once. The most likely explanation sitting in this program's own data: last year's trip came at 8–6 with a winning overall record on paper, while this year's 6–6 finish is exactly break-even, and with dozens of other 6-win-and-better teams nationally competing for a shrinking number of lower-tier bowl slots, break-even wasn't enough to make the cut this time.

SEGMENT 08

The Exodus: Twenty-One Players Gone

This is the largest single-offseason roster turnover this program has tracked — fifteen graduations, two NFL Draft departures, and four portal transfers out, against just two players (Carlos Allen, Joe Eisworth) who tested the portal and chose to stay.

SEGMENT 09

The Takeaway

Three seasons in, the pattern keeps repeating with variations: a rough start salvaged by a midseason answer, a national rushing leader who can't crack the Heisman conversation, and a run defense that simply will not move. What's different this time is the scale of what's walking out the door — nineteen players, including two All-Conference starters and a full offensive line's worth of experience — right as the recruiting department is producing its best class in program history. The 2028 preview is where all of that gets weighed against each other.
