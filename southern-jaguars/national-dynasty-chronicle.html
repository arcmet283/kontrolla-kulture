SEGMENT 01

The Champion Lineage

2025 National Champion
Notre Dame def. Texas Tech, 34–31

Built on Heisman winner Fernando Mendoza (Indiana, not Notre Dame — the Heisman and the title went to different programs that year) and Doak Walker winner Jeremiyah Love. Both are in the NFL now. Notre Dame reloaded around a five-deep Heisman watchlist presence in 2026, then lost their playoff quarterfinal to eventual champion Texas.

2026 National Champion
Texas def. Georgia, 54–14

A 43-point revenge swing on the same fixture that beat them in the SEC Championship six weeks earlier. Heisman winner Arch Manning closed his career with the Heisman, Maxwell, Davey O'Brien, a title, and Texas's single-season and career passing records (4,831 yards, 50 TDs, 85 career TDs). He's in the NFL now, and Texas has opened 2027 at 7–2, No. 9 — a real but modest step back, not a collapse.

2027 Race, So Far
Notre Dame (7–0) leads a wide-open field

Notre Dame is the only unbeaten team left among the sport's traditional powers, with Iowa State (9–0) the only team in the country with more wins and no losses. Two straight seasons have now ended with the reigning champion's best player gone to the NFL and the program taking a real, measurable step back the following year — watch whether that pattern holds a third time once this season's champion is finally crowned.

The Heisman Board: From Mendoza To Manning To Now

Fernando Mendoza won the Heisman for Indiana in 2025. Arch Manning won it for Texas in 2026. Neither is on this year's board — both exhausted their eligibility and are now in the NFL, continuing a pattern where the Heisman winner has never repeated on the following year's watchlist in this dynasty's tracked history. SEGMENT 06

The Virginia Cliff, Updated

Last week's report flagged Adam Fuller's collapse at Virginia — from the best debut season of any 2025 hire (7–5, a Sun Bowl championship) to 0–4 in year two. The bleeding hasn't stopped: Virginia is now 2–7, the worst full-season trajectory this chronicle has tracked for any coach, debut included. Two seasons ago this was the breakout story of the entire coaching carousel. It's now the cautionary tale.

SEGMENT 07

Signature Upsets Of The 2027 Season

Week 4
Auburn 53, Texas 47

Hugh Freeze's Auburn team knocked off the reigning national champion, then No. 1, in a shootout. Still the season's headline result.

Week 5
Maryland 30, Michigan 27

Andy Kotelnicki's Maryland — a coach who went 4–8 in his own debut season — knocked off a top-2 team in his second year.

Week 8
Florida 45, Texas 20

Dana Dimel's Florida blew out a top-5 Texas team by 25 points — the third 2025-cohort coach this season to land a signature win over a nationally elite opponent.

All three of this season's marquee upsets share a common thread: they were pulled off by coaches from the same 2025 hiring class this report has tracked from day one, each of them a program-turnaround story that took two full years to fully arrive. The 2025 coaching carousel, uneven as it's looked at times, has now produced three of the season's biggest results.

SEGMENT 08

What To Watch

Notre Dame and Iowa State are the only teams left without a loss, and both have a real case to be the team a Southern-style Cinderella story measures itself against — Iowa State, in particular, has now won nine straight without the pedigree of a traditional blue blood. The Heisman race has turned into a running back's game for the first time in this chronicle's history, with Richard Young and Fluff Bothwell setting the pace. And the coaching carousel, which looked settled after three quiet firings last winter, is now bracing for what could be its biggest cycle yet — Brian Kelly's presence on a hot-seat board alone guarantees national attention on however this offseason resolves.

Three years of data now say the same thing every single cycle: the champion's best player leaves, the champion takes a real step back, a totally new cast of statistical leaders and Heisman contenders replaces the old one, and at least one coaching story nobody saw coming in August ends up defining the whole season. 2027 is following that pattern almost exactly — which, at this point, isn't really a prediction anymore. It's just what this sport does, year after year, and the chronicle exists specifically to keep proving it.
