SEGMENT 01

The Final Stretch

Week 12 · At Memphis
Southern 45, Memphis 38 Won

Foxworth led the go-ahead drive in the fourth quarter, finishing 15-of-30 for 216 yards and 3 touchdowns. Molinich added 139 yards and a touchdown on the ground, plus 6 catches for 35 yards and a receiving score — a complete game from Southern's two best players at once.

Week 13 · At UTSA
Southern 27, UTSA 30 (2OT) Lost

A rough final regular-season outing for Foxworth: 13-of-31, 224 yards, 2 touchdowns, but 3 interceptions. Molinich ground out 114 yards on 22 carries without finding the end zone. Gavin Hoffman and Jason Davis both scored, and freshman (redshirt) corner Addison Tolefree closed the first half with a pick-six — but it wasn't enough in double overtime.

SEGMENT 04

Molinich's Second Straight National Rushing Title

Jordan Molinich leads the country in rushing yards for the second consecutive season — his entire college career, both years. His 2027 total (1,669 yards) comes in below his freshman-year mark (2,349 yards in 2026), but it was enough to hold off the rest of the country again: 1st in the NCAA for rushing yards, 3rd in the AAC for rushing touchdowns. Two seasons, two national rushing titles, zero seasons where he wasn't the best running back on the field.

SEGMENT 05

Foxworth's First Full Season, By The Numbers

A 2-star true freshman who signed outside the position's top 200 finishes his debut season as Southern's full-time starter at 130-of-246 for 2,067 yards, 21 touchdowns, and 10 interceptions. He took over a broken offense in Week 6 and leaves the regular season as the reason it has a bowl game to play in.

SEGMENT 08

Missing The Championship Game

A year ago, Southern played for the AAC title, losing to Tulane 38–31 in a genuine shootout. This year, Tulane finished 6–6 and didn't come close to the title game either — instead it's North Texas and USF meeting in Chicago, two teams Southern never even played in conference. It's a real step back in one specific way (no championship appearance) even as the underlying story (a QB-change season salvaged into bowl eligibility) reads as a legitimate accomplishment on its own.

SEGMENT 09

Heisman Watch: Where It Ended

Molinich's Heisman Watchlist count closed the regular season exactly where it's sat for weeks: 4 weeks, unchanged since Week 10. He never stopped leading the country in rushing yards, and his name never moved on the board again after that early stretch. Bryce Underwood finished at 11 weeks, Richard Young and Fluff Bothwell both at 12, and Iowa State's Jaray Rask climbed all the way to 10 riding his team's unbeaten season. Production without a signature moment wasn't enough to keep Southern's best player in that conversation.

SEGMENT 10

What's Next

Southern's bowl destination hasn't been announced yet — the full bowl slate is still blank league-wide as conference championship weekend plays out. What's already decided is the record: 6–6, bowl-eligible for the second season in a row, on the strength of a true freshman quarterback who turned a broken 1–4 start into a winning finish. Last year's first-ever bowl trip ended in a Gasparilla Bowl loss to Tennessee. Whatever bowl comes next, it's a chance to win the program's first bowl game outright.
