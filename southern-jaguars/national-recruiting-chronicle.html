SEGMENT 04

Recruiting Meets The Scoreboard

Georgia & Miami CFP-Fueled Surge

Georgia was the 2026 CFP runner-up (13–0 into the bracket, lost the title game 54–14) and jumped 14 spots in the very next class. Miami was a CFP field team at 11–2 and jumped 17. Both of this cycle's two biggest risers were coming off deep national title runs.

Texas Champion, Modest Follow-Up

Won the 2026 national title and still only signed the No. 16 class in 2027 — the actual champion didn't reload at anywhere near the rate its CFP runner-up did.

Texas Tech & Joey McGuire Coach Outlasting The Class

Won the 2026 Best HC Award at 12–1 with the No. 3 recruiting class the same cycle. By 2027, that class fell off the Top 25 board entirely — yet Texas Tech is still a ranked team in the Week 10 2027 polls. Recruiting rank isn't holding this program up; the coaching is.

USC & Jesse Minter Success Without The Class

Minter's 2026 hire debuted at 7–1 in 2027 — and USC has never appeared on either Top 25 recruiting board in this chronicle. A genuinely elite season built on development and the portal below the Top 25 line, not blue-chip recruiting.

Notre Dame Bleeding Both Ways

Still 7–0 and No. 1 in the Week 10 2027 polls, but the two highest-ranked transfers in the entire 2027 portal class — Jaiden Ausberry (No. 1, to Auburn) and Mike Mitchell (No. 2, to Penn St.) — both left Notre Dame. A CFP-contending roster and a program-level talent drain, happening at the same time.

SEGMENT 06

How Schools Land Their Headliner

In 2026, 15 of the 25 Top Recruits were homegrown high school signees and 10 came through the portal. In 2027, that flipped completely: 15 of 25 headliners were transfers, only 10 were high school signees. In one cycle, the portal went from the minority way to land a program's best player to the majority way.

SEGMENT 09

The Zayden Walker Story

Georgia → Notre Dame (Transfer)
A 5★ Transfer Add Becomes A No. 1 Signee

Came out of Georgia and entered the portal rated a 5-star and the No. 7 overall transfer add in the country, landing at Notre Dame — where he became that class's No. 1 overall signee, on the roster that finished as the nation's top-ranked class. His pre-transfer high school profile isn't in the national recruiting data at all, which is its own point: however he got to Georgia, he left it as one of the single most sought-after players in the portal.

SEGMENT 10

The Jaray Rask Conversion

Iowa St. · 2026 HS Signee → 2027 Heisman Watchlist
Signed As An ATH, Now A Watchlist QB

Jaray Rask signed with Iowa State as a 5-star athlete in the 2026 class — Iowa State's own Top Recruit that cycle, at No. 11 nationally. By the 2027 Heisman Watchlist, he's listed at quarterback, four weeks on the board, for an Iowa State team that sits at 9–0 — tied with Notre Dame for the best record in the country at the Week 10 check-in. A position-flip recruit turning into a Heisman contender for the nation's most surprising unbeaten team is exactly the kind of outcome a class ranking alone can't predict.

SEGMENT 11

The NFL-In-A-Hurry Club

Three of the ten 2026 Top 10 Transfers were already draft-eligible and gone by their second tracked season — Jordan Hall (Oklahoma), A.J. Turner (UCF), and Barion Brown (ECU) all hit the NFL within two years of their portal move. All three were already older, experienced transfers rather than young additions, which is exactly the profile most likely to make an immediate impact and leave just as fast — a portal-era tradeoff every program chasing a top-10 transfer class is making, whether they say so or not.

SEGMENT 12

What To Watch

The 2027 recruiting class is still too young to grade in full, but the early shape of it already tells a story: Iowa and Pittsburgh's jumps from off-the-board to top-3 both leaned heavily on the transfer portal rather than high school recruiting, following the same playbook Notre Dame used to reach No. 1 the cycle before. Oklahoma remains the one program building at a consistently elite level through both approaches at once — and on the field, that consistency hasn't translated into a title yet, which is its own reminder that recruiting rank and winning are related, not identical.

Watch the retention numbers as closely as the rankings — two of the five best high school prospects in the country in 2026 were already gone from the schools that signed them within two years, and Notre Dame is bleeding its two best 2027 transfer prospects out the door while still sitting unbeaten. And watch the programs proving the class rank isn't destiny at all: USC winning without ever cracking the board, Texas Tech's coaching outlasting a class that fell off it entirely. Signing a great class and keeping a great class have become two different jobs — and neither one is the same job as winning with what you signed.
