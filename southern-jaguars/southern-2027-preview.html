Fourteen players are gone. The starting quarterback graduated. Both defensive team-tackle and sack leaders from the last two seasons are off the roster. And yet the single most important fact about Southern's 2027 season is one sentence long: Jordan Molinich is back, and for the first time in this program's history, he's already on the Heisman watchlist before a single snap has been played. That's the whole tension of year three in one paragraph — a program that just posted the best individual season in its history and now has to figure out who blocks for him, who throws it when he doesn't have the ball, and who tackles anybody on defense. This preview covers all of it: the schedule, the exodus, the returning core, the incoming class headlined by the program's first five-star signee, and the national picture Southern reenters at a projected No. 89.

SEGMENT 02

What's Gone: A Quick Recap

Fourteen players are off the roster: QB Joshua Clark (graduation), MLB Owen West and DE Amir Tracy (both NFL Draft), CB Devin Sanchez (transfer to Texas Tech), DT Benedict Umeh (transfer to Stanford), FS Siale Siverand (transfer to Oklahoma St.), and eight more seniors across the defensive front, secondary, and receiving corps — MLB Quincy Davis, DT Trevell Vivians, CB Jordan Matthews, WRs Jamari Golden and Amin Hunter, and TE Harry Kardell all graduated. That's the starting quarterback and the top tacklers on both sides of the ball, gone in a single cycle. Two names who nearly left, HB/WR Matt Brown and TE Joe Eisworth, both flirted with the transfer portal and ended up back on the roster — the only real retention win in an otherwise brutal offseason attrition cycle.

SEGMENT 04

The QB Question

This is the single biggest open competition on the roster. Joshua Clark's graduation leaves no clear returning starter — the only quarterback on this roster with real game experience is Ty Wilson (SO), who came off the bench in the first quarter of the Gasparilla Bowl after Clark's injury and went 11-of-22 for 188 yards and a touchdown against Tennessee, albeit with 2 interceptions. No transfer portal quarterback was added this cycle — the incoming help at the position is three true freshmen (James Moody, Jordan Wilder, Joseph Foxworth, all two-star signees), none likely to start immediately. Barring a late portal addition, Wilson enters fall camp as the presumptive favorite by default, inheriting a backfield with the best running back in the country and a suddenly retooled offensive line to throw behind.

SEGMENT 05

The Incoming Class Arrives

Southern's 2027 class is the deepest this dynasty has signed — twenty-two high school prospects headlined by Carlos Allen, a five-star running back and the program's first-ever five-star signee, plus four-star linebacker Todd Pritchett. The transfer portal class of eight leans almost entirely offensive line and tight end — three tackles (Ronnie Turner, Christian Martin, Kaedin Massey) and two tight ends (Javeon Fricks, Gavin Hoffman) joining returnees Matt Brown and Joe Eisworth — a direct response to the protection issues that contributed to Joshua Clark's bowl-game injury. Between Allen in the backfield and the O-line reinforcements up front, this is a program explicitly building infrastructure for whoever wins the quarterback job to succeed behind.

SEGMENT 07

The Heisman Watchlist, With A New Name

Officially On The Board
Jordan Molinich HB · SO · Southern
After an entire 2026 season — a national rushing record, a Doak Walker Award, a 1st Team All-American nod — that produced zero weeks on the Heisman watchlist, Molinich opens 2027 already on it. This report series tracked that snub across five separate checkpoints last year and called it, at the time, "the single injustice this report series has tracked all year." It's resolved now, before he's even played a snap.

He's one of five names on the board so far: Bryce Underwood (Michigan, QB-JR), Eli Holstein (Pittsburgh, QB-SR RS), Julian Lewis (Colorado, QB-JR), and Walker White (Baylor, QB-JR RS) round out an otherwise all-quarterback list. Molinich is the only running back and the only Group of Five-tier player among them — both firsts for this dynasty.

SEGMENT 09

Way-Too-Early CFP Picks

Bold Winner Pick
Texas repeats over Alabama

Arch Manning is gone to the NFL, but a 15–1 champion with Joey McGuire-caliber program continuity (his Texas Tech counterpart just won Best HC) doesn't fall off a cliff in one offseason. Alabama, preseason No. 3 after a semifinal run, is the most credible challenger on paper.

Notre Dame (preseason No. 2) and Miami (No. 4) are the other two programs positioned to make a real push, both riding continuity from 12-2-or-better 2026 seasons. Nebraska's preseason No. 9 is the number most worth watching — a program that finished the 2026 CFP poll at No. 15, three spots outside the 12-team field, now positioned inside the preseason top 10.

SEGMENT 10

Southern's Projection

Call it 6–6, maybe 7–5 with a favorable quarterback answer and health at running back. The formula hasn't changed: Jordan Molinich is going to get his yards regardless of who's blocking or throwing around him, and the question is whether a rebuilt offensive line and an unproven quarterback room can keep opposing defenses from selling out entirely against the run the way better teams started to late in 2026. The three rematch games — Tulane, Memphis, UTSA — are the actual referendum on this program's ceiling; all three ended Southern's 2026 season in exactly the way this report series predicted would define the year, and beating even one of them flips the emotional arc of 2027 entirely.

The bigger picture is the one that matters more than any single record projection: a program that opened this dynasty at prestige 1 and rank 136 now has a reigning national award winner, a five-star signee, and a Heisman watchlist name heading into year three. Whatever the win total ends up being, Southern is no longer a surprise story. It's a program other teams are building game plans specifically to stop — which is its own kind of arrival.
