SEGMENT 01

2026, In One Paragraph

Voss
Renee, set the table. Texas just won a national title in about as dominant a fashion as this format allows.
Castellan
54–14 over Georgia in the championship game, and it was personal — Georgia had beaten Texas 41–34 in the SEC Championship six weeks earlier, so that's a 43-point swing on the exact same fixture. Arch Manning closed his career with the Heisman, the Maxwell, the Davey O'Brien, a title, and Texas's single-season and career passing records: 4,831 yards, 50 touchdowns, 85 career touchdowns. He's in the NFL now.
Voss
And the year before that, it was Notre Dame cutting down the nets — a 34–31 win over Texas Tech, with Fernando Mendoza winning the Heisman for Indiana the same season, not for the champion. The award and the title have gone to different programs both years this chronicle has tracked.
Castellan
Which is the pattern worth carrying into 2027: this format does not reward continuity at the top. Notre Dame reloaded in 2026 and still lost their playoff quarterfinal to the eventual champion. Whoever's cutting down the nets in January almost certainly isn't the same program that did it in either of the last two years.

Voss
Three jobs changed hands this cycle, all set up to debut in 2027 — a blue blood in Oklahoma, a resurgent power in USC, and a rebuild in Kentucky.
Castellan
Three very different jobs, three very different seats. Stein inherits a program that's been stuck at 4–8 two years running. Minter takes over a USC team that still has talent even at 5–7. And Surace is walking into the toughest build of the three — Kentucky hasn't finished above 4–8 in this chronicle's tracked history.

Voss
Texas at No. 1 is obvious. What jumps out to you further down the board?
Castellan
No. 2 and No. 6 tell the whole 2025 title game story on their own — Notre Dame and Texas Tech, the two teams that played for it all two years ago, both still elite. And Nebraska at No. 9 is the number worth circling: they finished the 2026 CFP poll at No. 15, three spots outside the field entirely, and they've jumped six spots into the preseason top 10. That's either a program the polls are betting big on, or a preseason number that's ahead of itself.
Voss
Miami at No. 4 has the résumé to back a top-five spot — back-to-back ACC titles in 2025 and 2026, plus a 2025 CFP semifinal run before losing to Texas Tech in the Fiesta Bowl.
Castellan
Right, that's not a team riding one good season into this ranking. And down at No. 25, Auburn closing out the board is a name we'll come back to in a minute — their head coach is also on this year's hot seat list, which is its own kind of vote of confidence.
SEGMENT 04

Hot Seat Watch 2027

ON SCREEN: HOT SEAT BOARD, 2027
Auburn Hot Seat

Hugh Freeze survived 2026 at 6–4. No other tracked history for this program — a repeat name from last year's board with nothing else on the ledger either way.

Coastal Carolina Hot Seat

Tim Beck's 1–8 finish in 2026 never resulted in a change. Back on the list, unsurprisingly, with no other tracked history for the program.

Mississippi St. Hot Seat

Jeff Lebby returns to this board for the second straight year without an actual change happening. No other tracked history for the program.

Minnesota Hot Seat

P.J. Fleck, a long-tenured name, is a new addition to this list — the most notable program on the entire 2027 board by reputation alone.

Tennessee Hot Seat

Josh Heupel is fresh off beating Southern Jaguars 44–31 in the 2026 Gasparilla Bowl — a program with real recent success still landing on this board.

Virginia Tech Hot Seat

Brent Pry still has a real accomplishment on the résumé: a 36–22 win over Cincinnati in the 2025 StaffDNA Cure Bowl.

Wake Forest Hot Seat

Jake Dickert is a repeat name on this board. No other tracked history for the program either way.

Voss
Seven coaches on notice entering one offseason — that's the largest hot-seat class this desk has tracked.
Castellan
And it's a mixed bag by program pedigree. Minnesota is the name that stands out just by reputation. But Pry and Heupel both have real recent accomplishments sitting right next to their names on this board — a bowl win and a signature win, respectively — while three of the other five (Auburn, Coastal Carolina, Mississippi St., Wake Forest) genuinely have nothing else tracked in either direction. That's not this desk being lazy about their histories — there's simply nothing else on the ledger to report.

After a 2026 season that produced a national rushing record and a Doak Walker Award and still somehow zero Heisman weeks, having him on the board at all before he's played a snap is the story. This desk's local coverage flagged that snub across five separate checkpoints last year and called it, at the time, the single injustice this report series tracked all year. It's resolved now, before kickoff.
SEGMENT 06

Way-Too-Early CFP Pick

Bold Winner Pick
Texas Repeats Over Alabama
Voss
Make the case for back-to-back.
Castellan
Arch Manning is gone to the NFL, but a 15–1 champion with Joey McGuire-caliber program continuity behind the scenes — his Texas Tech counterpart just won the Best HC Award at 12–1 — doesn't fall off a cliff in one offseason. Alabama, preseason No. 3 after a semifinal run, is the most credible challenger on paper. Notre Dame at No. 2 and Miami at No. 4 are the other two programs positioned to make a real push, Notre Dame riding continuity from a 12–2 2026 season and Miami off an 11–3 finish that still included a second straight ACC title.
Voss
And Nebraska's No. 9 preseason slot is the number you flagged earlier as one to watch either way.
Castellan
Right — a program that finished three spots outside the field last year now sitting inside the preseason top 10 is either going to look prescient by December or become the cautionary tale about trusting a preseason number too early. That's the swing storyline of the whole season outside the top four.
SEGMENT 07

What To Watch

Voss
Close us out. What's the one thing you're tracking above everything else once 2027 actually kicks off?
Castellan
Whether this format's own pattern holds a third straight year. Two champions, two different programs, and the individual awards going somewhere else entirely both times. If Texas actually repeats, that's the real headline of the season regardless of who else rises or falls — a program breaking a trend this chronicle has treated as close to a law of nature.
Voss
And on the ground level, a seven-coach hot seat board and three brand-new head coaches debuting at Oklahoma, USC, and Kentucky means the coaching carousel is going to be as big a story by December as anything that happens on the field.
Castellan
Agreed. Preseason polls are just a starting line. We'll be back with the actual results.
